# Age-by-Age Developmental Framework for Coaching Tennis (Tots to Seniors)

Research notes compiled 2026-10-09. Primary documents were downloaded and read in full where possible: the ITF 2026 Rules of Tennis (Appendix I ball table and Appendix VIII), the USTA Net Generation Practice and Play Plans (Red, Orange, Green, Yellow), the Net Generation Competencies and Assessment sheets, the USTA American Development Model (ADM) framework, USTA Tennis 101 for Adults, the LTA coach/player ratio guidance, and the LTA Walking Tennis coach guide. Some sites (tennis.com.au, itfcoachingreview.com) blocked direct fetching (HTTP 403). For those, the notes rely on search-result snippets and flag this.

**Source dating.** Net Generation coach plans are (c) 2017 (Red/Orange/Green) and (c) 2019 (Yellow). The ADM PDF dates from August 2019. LTA ratio guidance is from July 2020. The LTA Walking Tennis guide is from May 2021. The CAC trainability material dates from 2011. The ITF Rules are the 2026 edition, which is current. I did not find newer replacements for these documents, but the USTA may have updated curricula behind its coach login.

## 1. Stage specifications (court, net, ball, racquet) and player characteristics

### Takeaway
All major federations agree on the red → orange → green → yellow sequence and on its court and ball dimensions, which the ITF Rules of Tennis set. The red court is about 36-42 ft long with a net about 31.5-33 in high. The orange court is about 58-60 ft with a net of 31.5-36 in. Green is played on the full 78 ft court. Where federations differ is the age attached to each colour: the LTA moves children to yellow ball earliest (10+), and Tennis Australia's published ages run later than the ITF's. Player characteristics move from fundamental movement and following single-step instructions at age 3-5 toward independent decision-making, goal-setting and self-analysis by green and yellow ball.

### Cited Findings

**Adult levels (NTRP)**
- **3.0:** fairly consistent on medium-paced shots but lacks directional control, depth and pace. Most common doubles formation is one up, one back. Uncomfortable at the net.
- **3.5:** stroke dependability with directional control on moderate shots, but still lacks depth and variety. More aggressive net play and developing doubles teamwork.
- **4.0:** dependable strokes with direction and depth on both sides. Uses lobs, overheads, approaches and volleys "with some success". Occasionally forces errors on serve. Rallies may be lost to impatience.

— [NTRP Guidelines Sheet (USTA definitions reproduced)](https://www.gretanc.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/NTRP-Guidelines-Sheet.pdf) (search snippet; secondary reproduction of USTA text)

### Inferences
- The ITF ranges are the "legal" specs. USTA, LTA and Tennis Australia each pick values inside them. The USTA red net of 2'9" (33 in) sits at the top of the ITF red range. The LTA's 80 cm red/orange net is at the bottom.
- For a private coach, the practical defaults are:

  | Stage | Court | Net | Racquet |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Red | 36 ft x 18 ft | ~2'9" | 19-23 in |
  | Orange | 60 ft x 21 ft (singles) | ~3 ft, or regulation lowered | 23-25 in |
  | Green | 78 ft full court | regulation 3 ft | 25-26 (27) in |
  | Yellow | 78 ft full court | regulation | 26-27 in |

- Age labels conflict, so coaches should place by competency, not birthday:
  - Orange: ITF 8-10; USTA municipal pages 7-10 or 8-10; LTA 8-9; Tennis Australia partner page 9-10.
  - Green: ITF 9-10 (advanced); USTA 9-11; LTA 9-10; Tennis Australia partner page 11-12.
  - Yellow: LTA 10+; USTA 11+; Tennis Australia 12+.
  
  Every federation explicitly says the ages are guides.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch Tennis Australia's official Hot Shots spec page (403), so the sub-stage court and net dimensions are unverified.
- I found no official spec for a "pre-red/tots" court separate from red. Net Gen Pre-Rally and LTA Blue use the red ball, playground or foam balls and flexible space. Tennis Australia Blue uses the red ball with 19-23 in racquets.
- The USTA "tennis sized right" page now redirects. I could not confirm the current official USTA age labels per colour from a primary USTA page; the U.S. figures above come from municipal and section pages.

## 2. Session design: length, play vs. instruction, group size, lesson structure

### Takeaway
USTA Net Generation plans scale session length with stage:

| Level | Session length |
|---|---|
| Pre-Rally | 45 min |
| Red 3 to Orange 3 | 60 min |
| Orange 2 to Yellow | 90 min |

Every level keeps the same skeleton: warm-up → (character) → athletic and tennis skills → games → "play at home" review. The game/competitive block grows from about 22-27% of the session at Pre-Rally to about 33-44% at Yellow. LTA's maximum coach:player ratios are 1:10 for U10 and 1:12 for older juniors and adults, with 2-6 players per court treated as optimal. Adult beginner programs (USTA Tennis 101, ITF Tennis Xpress) run 6 sessions of about 60-90 minutes and start with slower balls.

### Inferences
- **Play vs. instruction share** (calculated from the Net Gen tables):
  - Explicitly competitive or game time is about 22-27% at Pre-Rally (10-12 of 45 min) and 25-33% at Red 3 (15-20 of 60).
  - It is about 28-33% at Orange 2 and Green (25-30 of 90), and 33-44% at Yellow (30-40 of 90).
  - Even the "skills" blocks are activity-based (cooperative rallies, feeds with progressions), not lectures. "Character" talk is capped at ≤3 min at every level.
  - The adult Tennis 101 plan moves from about 0% to 83% match play (45 of 54 min) by Class 6.
- **Private-lesson translation:** 30-45 min for ages 3-5. 45-60 min for red and early orange. 60 min for orange, green and early yellow. 60-90 min for competitive juniors. 60 min for adult beginners. 60-90 min for league adults. 60 min or less for seniors, with a longer warm-up and cool-down.
- Keep coach talk under about 30-60 seconds per block. The plans cap character talk at ≤3 min and say to keep athletic activities under 5 min each at red.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative federation figure for "balls per minute" or contacts per lesson for any stage. Indirect evidence on hitting opportunities appears in Section 5 (rally length on modified courts).
- I found no federation guideline for private (1:1) lesson length by age; the figures above are inferred from group plans.
- I found no official session-length guidance specific to seniors aged 55+/65+ beyond the LTA Walking Tennis structure.

## 3. Technical and tactical milestones per stage (what to master before moving up)

### Takeaway
Net Generation's competency sheets are the most concrete published milestone ladder. They cover Pre-Rally → Red 3/2/1 → Orange 3/2/1 → Green 2/1 → Yellow 2/1, split into Athlete (feet/hands), Player (baseline, serve/return, transition, other) and Person (game, character). The ladder runs from rolling the ball and self-rallying (Pre-Rally), through overhand serve into the box and rallying from the service line (Red), and continuous topspin rallies, serve to three spots and doubles positioning (Orange). It finishes with weapon-building, serve-plus-one patterns and all-court doubles (Green/Yellow). The LTAD stage models (ADM, Tennis Canada) frame the same journey as FUNdamentals → Learn to Train → Train to Train/Compete.

### Cited Findings
Unless marked otherwise, every item below is quoted or paraphrased from the Net Gen competency sheets: [Pre-Rally/Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Competencies-PreRally-Red.pdf), [Orange](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Competencies-Orange.pdf), [Green](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Competencies-Green.pdf).

**Pre-Rally (tots, roughly 3-5)**
- Feet: runs coordinating opposite arm and leg; shuffles in three directions; hops on one foot and lands on two; zigzag runs.
- Hands: tosses and catches a large ball with two hands.
- Tennis: rolls the ball with the racquet along the ground and keeps a rolling rally with a partner; self-rallies in a small space; drop-hits to a large target. Serve is an underhand toss and send.
- Game: counts rallies; understands in and out.

**Red 3**
- Rallies with a partner in a small space without a net; drop-hits side to side, high/low and deep/short.
- Serves underhand or overhand into the correct box from inside the service line.
- Grips: Eastern forehand; a simple low-to-high swing.
- Volleys with a blocking motion.
- Scores with a visual aid.

**Red 2**
- Rallies over the net from the service line.
- Serves overhand to the opponent's forehand and backhand; returns second serve deep.
- Approach-plus-volley combination from a feed; split-step before the volley.
- Remembers and announces the score.

**Red 1**
- Rallies crosscourt, down the line and side to side from the baseline (36-ft court). Topspin is developing, and slice comes from a feed.
- Uses open or semi-open stance on wide balls; crossover recovery.
- Continental grip on serve and volleys; "trophy" position for overheads; developing slice and body serve.
- Understands faults and lets; can play best-of-three tiebreaks.

**Orange 3**
- Rallies consistently to the centre with depth and "increased net height".
- Serves consistently in the box from both sides; continental grip developing.
- Volley-to-groundstroke rallies; overhead to left and right.
- Understands basic doubles positions; uses traditional 15-30-40 scoring; can play a short set.

**Orange 2**
- Depth and direction to targets; more topspin with speed; slice developing on low midcourt balls.
- Direct first serve to forehand or backhand; spin on second serve.
- Recognizes the short ball to attack; angles high volleys.
- Lobs over the net player; one-up/one-back doubles; ad or no-ad short set.

**Orange 1**
- Takes deep balls on the rise; exploits weaknesses; changes rally tempo for offense and defense.
- Serves to three spots (wide/body/T) on both sides; establishing serve and first-ball combinations; attacks weak second serves.
- Drive volleys; two-up doubles with communication; identifies the opponent's game style.

**Green 2**
- Depth and direction on the full court; topspin angle off wide midcourt balls; developing inside-out forehand.
- Directs first serve; returns first serve deep down the centre.
- Developing down-the-line, angle and lob passing shots; scores set tiebreaks and match tiebreaks.

**Green 1**
- Combines height, spin and speed to keep the opponent deep; dictates with the forehand; drop shots.
- Serves to three spots on both sides; more consistent second serve with depth and spin; returns on the rise.
- Comes to net in varied ways (approach with topspin or slice, drive volley, sneak/delay attack); "Developing a weapon."
- Doubles: two up, poach, switch. Slides on clay.
- Routines before, during and after matches.

**Yellow 2 → 1 (Net Gen Yellow chapters, (c) 2019)**
- Yellow 2 aim: "Maintain skills learned from Green and adapt to the Yellow Ball". Chapter themes: Keep it Deep, Double Trouble, Over and Under, Move It, Over My Head, Team Up.
- Yellow 1 themes: Open it Up, Ridiculous Return, Spin It, Attack Mode, Offense/Defense, Who's the Boss?, Dubz Rules, Prime Time.
- In the community version, Yellow 2 is the entry level and Yellow 1 is the top level.

— [Net Gen Yellow curriculum](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/curriculum-center/coach-samples/assets/Net-Generation-Coaches-Community-Yellow-Ball-Manual-Sample.pdf)

**Level objectives (Net Gen plans)**

| Red 3 | "Develop skills of serve, rally, and score." |
| Red 2 | "Learn to play from all areas of 36-foot court." |

— [Practice Plan Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Red.pdf); [Practice Plan Orange](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Orange.pdf); [Green curriculum](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/curriculum-center/coach-samples/assets/Net-Generation-Coaches-Curriculum-Green-Sample.pdf)

- LTA stage descriptors:
  - Red: "Serve, rally and score", with overarm serves, rallies and volleys and relaxed competition.
  - Orange: "Develop a rounded game" (tactics, problem-solving, knows all rules).
  - Green: "Test your skills" on the full court, refining technique.

- ADM Discover & Learn (5-11): "Learn the basic rules of the game", motor skills that transfer to other sports, "Emphasis on practice over competition", ABCs.
- ADM Develop & Challenge (12-18): "Developmentally appropriate training and competition", "Increased awareness of injury prevention".
- ADM Play for Life (18+): "Development of individualized game style".

**Adults**

### Inferences
- **Red → orange readiness** (from the Red 1 sheet): can rally from the 36-ft baseline crosscourt and down the line; serves overhand with a continuous motion into the box; can play tiebreak scoring; uses crossover recovery.
- **Orange → green readiness** (from the Orange 1 sheet): consistent rallies with shape and spin; serves to 3 spots; plays doubles positions; plays sets with traditional scoring.
- **Green → yellow:** Green 1 competencies, especially second-serve consistency with spin and returning from varied positions.
- Mapping LTAD terms to colours: FUNdamentals ≈ pre-red/red; Learn to Train / Developing (about 8-12) ≈ orange/green/early yellow; Train to Train / Consolidating (about 12-15) ≈ yellow juniors; Learn to Perform / Train to Compete (about 15-18) ≈ competitive high-schoolers.

## 4. Physical literacy and injury prevention by age

### Takeaway
For under-12s, the federations prioritize the ABCs (agility, balance, coordination), fundamental movement skills and multi-sport play, with a skill window around 8-12 before the growth spurt. The "windows of trainability" concept is widely used but has weak empirical backing.

For juniors, the main injury controls are:
- train fewer hours per week than your age;
- delay kick serves until about 13;
- watch rapid-growth periods;
- cap the number of matches and tournaments.

For adults, risk rises above about 6-10 hours a week and after age 40. Elbow, low back, shoulder and knee injuries are the most common. Seniors benefit from slower formats, longer warm-ups and cool-downs, and balance work.

### Cited Findings

**Physical literacy and trainability**
- The ADM pushes physical literacy for ages 5-11 ("agility, balance, and coordination"), sport sampling, and "Free play to discover sport and new skills". Its glossary defines early specialization as specialization before puberty. — [USTA ADM Framework](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/coach-organize/content-fragments/resource-library/assets/pdfs/adm-framework.pdf)
- Net Gen athletic-skill progressions run as follows:
  - Pre-Rally: run, shuffle, hop.
  - Red 3: gallop, skip, two-foot jumps.
  - Red 2: half-jump rope, overhand throw into the service boxes.
  - Orange 1: one-leg continuous jumps, single-jump rope, throwing a football spiral.
  - Green 1: explosive starts, double-jump rope.
  
  — [Net Gen Competencies](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Competencies-PreRally-Red.pdf); [Orange](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Competencies-Orange.pdf); [Green](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Competencies-Green.pdf)
- Windows of optimal trainability (Coaching Association of Canada / Cross Country Canada 2011, adapted from Balyi & Way 2005):
  - The skill window begins at about 9 for boys and 8 for girls and ends at the onset of the growth spurt.
  - Speed 1 (repetitions under 5 s) is for girls during FUNdamentals and for boys at the end of that stage.
  - Suppleness: 6-10 years for both sexes.
  - Aerobic, strength and anaerobic windows are keyed to the growth spurt and peak height velocity (PHV), not to chronological age.
  
  — [CAC/Cross Country Canada – Trainability](https://mail.crosscountrybc.ca/sites/default/files/documents/Optimal-Trainability-%281%29.pdf)
- A general LTAD summary describes a pre-puberty "speed training window" for quickness, agility and change of direction. — [TASS LTAD summary (The Papare)](https://www.thepapare.com/long-term-athletic-development-by-tass/) (secondary)
- **Critique:** Ford et al. (2011) found the LTAD model one-dimensional and lacking empirical, especially longitudinal, evidence for "windows of opportunity". — [Cardiff Met – Ford et al. 2011](https://pure.cardiffmet.ac.uk/en/publications/the-long-term-athlete-development-model-physiological-evidence-an/) (snippet)

**Junior injury prevention**
- AMSSM guidance:
  - "Limit practicing kick serves until at least 13 years old."
  - Play fewer hours per week than the player's age and fewer tournaments per year than their age.
  - Play multiple sports before adolescence.
  - Use tennis-specific warm-ups and stretch at the end.
  
  — [AMSSM / Sports Med Today – Tennis](https://www.sportsmedtoday.com/tennis-ct-73.htm)
- Emory Tennis Medicine junior risk tiers:

  | Weekly volume | Risk |
  |---|---|
  | >10-16 hrs/week | high |
  | 6-10 hrs/week | intermediate |
  | <6 hrs/week | low |

  High-risk competition loads are ">40 matches/yr or >12 tournaments/year" and ">4th match in tournament". Risk is higher in older male juniors and during "rapid growth". High-risk injuries include spondylolysis, stress fractures, growth-plate injuries and OCD. — [Emory Tennis Medicine – Risk factors](https://www.emoryhealthcare.org/centers-programs/sports-medicine-center/tennis-medicine-program/risk-factors)
- The kick serve produces the highest lumbar loading. Elite adolescent servers experience lumbar loads of up to about 4x body weight, and lumbar hyperextension raises stress-fracture risk. — [Effects of Lumbar Spine Abnormality and Serve Types… (PMC7801574)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7801574/) (search snippet)
- A systematic review of serve injury risk factors lists weekly tennis volume, dominant-shoulder rotation strength, internal-rotation range of motion, age, body mass and height. — [Open University ORO – serve injury risk factors](https://oro.open.ac.uk/105642) (snippet)
- Rapid bone growth during PHV can outpace muscle and tendon adaptation, temporarily raising injury risk. Training should be tailored to maturity status. — [PMC12101259 scoping review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12101259/) (snippet)
- Net Gen notes for Orange 2 upward: a dynamic stretch at the start and a static stretch at the end "are important". — [Practice Plan Orange](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Orange.pdf)

**Adults**
- Emory adult risk:
  - Volume: high at "10 or more hrs/wk", intermediate at 6-10.
  - Age: high above 40, intermediate at 30-40.
  - Common injuries: elbow, low back, rotator cuff and knee.
  
  — [Emory Tennis Medicine](https://www.emoryhealthcare.org/centers-programs/sports-medicine-center/tennis-medicine-program/risk-factors)
- AMSSM: adults should take caution above 6 h/week, "particularly beyond 10". — [AMSSM / Sports Med Today](https://www.sportsmedtoday.com/tennis-ct-73.htm)
- Acute injuries in recreational players come mainly from falls and twists. About 10% need surgery, mainly for fractures, meniscal tears and Achilles ruptures. — [PMC7789623](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7789623) (snippet)

**Seniors**
- The LTA designed Walking Tennis for older adults to address "falls prevention, osteoporosis, diabetes, cognitive degeneration" and social isolation. Rules: no running or jumping, the ball may bounce twice, and a smaller court or modified equipment can be used. — [LTA Walking Tennis coach guide (2021)](https://www.lta.org.uk/4ac92d/siteassets/play/adult/file/coach-guide.pdf)
- Older tennis players show adapted automatic postural responses to balance perturbations. Tennis may be a promising way to train balance for fall prevention. — [Reactive response of older tennis practitioners… (J Sci Sport Exerc)](https://journal.hep.com.cn/josisae/EN/10.1007/s42978-021-00130-4) (snippet)
- In senior racquet players aged 50-66 (a paddle-tennis/padel cohort, not tennis), injuries were located in the elbow (29.8%), lower back (27.5%), knee (22.9%), "tennis leg" (22.1%) and shoulder (20.6%). — [RAMD – paddle-tennis senior players](https://ws154.juntadeandalucia.es/ojs/index.php/ramd/article/download/253/342?inline=1) (snippet; different sport, use with caution)
- In the Copenhagen City Heart Study (8,577 adults, up to 25 years of follow-up), tennis players gained 9.7 years of life expectancy versus sedentary people. That compares with 6.2 for badminton, 4.7 for soccer, 3.7 for cycling, 3.4 for swimming, 3.2 for jogging and 1.5 for health-club exercise. This is an observational study. — [PubMed 30193744](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30193744/); [AARP summary](https://www.aarp.org/health/healthy-living/info-2018/tennis-helps-life-expectancy.html)

### Inferences
- **Serve volume for young players.** No federation publishes a "pitch count". The defensible controls are:
  - hours per week below age, and below about 10-16 h for juniors;
  - no kick serve practice before about 13;
  - extra caution during growth spurts;
  - spreading serve volume across sessions.
  
  For a private coach, that means short serve blocks mixed into play, not long basket-serving sessions, for red, orange and green players.
- **Adults over 40:** a longer dynamic warm-up (calves and Achilles, hips, thoracic rotation, shoulder external rotation), gradual progression of hitting volume, and doubles-first match play.
- **Seniors:** Walking Tennis principles (double bounce, smaller court, slower balls) are an on-ramp. A balance and footwork component doubles as fall-prevention.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative numeric serve-count limit by age.
- Two claims appeared in a search summary but could not be traced to a page that states them, so they are excluded:
  - specific Balyi speed-window ages (boys 7-9 and 13-16; girls 6-8 and 11-13);
  - "Tennis Canada: no specialization before age 10".
  
  I could not locate a Tennis Canada primary LTAD document.
- I found no tennis-specific peer-reviewed data on Achilles or calf injury incidence by age. The cited Achilles concern is general racquet-sport (largely pickleball) reporting.
- I found no USTA or ITF document giving senior-specific recovery intervals. A "60+ should warm up 20 minutes and play ≤2 hours" claim appeared only in an unattributed search snippet and was excluded.

## 5. Coaching style by age (games-based/constraints-led, feedback, adult learners)

### Takeaway
For children, both research and federation curricula support the same approach: modify the court, ball and racquet (constraints) and teach through games and cooperative challenges. Use simple cue phrases and demonstrations rather than long technical explanations. Shift gradually from directed instruction to questioning and player decision-making by orange and green ball.

Adults need a different approach. Learning should be active (lots of balls, playing early), individualized feedback should be given while they play, and coaches should respect adults' prior experience, expectations and possible apprehension.

### Cited Findings

**Research on modified equipment and courts**
- Fitzpatrick, Davids & Stone filmed 48 juniors over 1,010 points. Mini Tennis Red rallies averaged 7.36 ± 6.06 shots versus 3.83 ± 2.40 at full ball. Forehands made up 66.40% of shots at Red versus 45.96% at full ball. Modified stages "afford children more opportunities to develop their skills". — [Sheffield Hallam SHURA – Fitzpatrick et al.](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/13997/)
- Buszard, Farrow, Reid & Masters (2014): children hit the most successful forehands with the smallest racket and least-compressed ball. They also more often swung low-to-high and struck the ball in front and to the side. — [Buszard et al. 2014 (via The FA)](https://www.thefa.com/-/media/education-hub/files/buszard-et-al-2014-effect-of-modifying-tennis-equipment-on-early-skill-development); [MSU research review 2025](https://education.msu.edu/sport-coaching-leadership/research-review/2025/04/how-do-you-develop-your-athletes-skills-modifying-sport-for-skill-development) (snippets)
- Larson & Guggenheimer (2013) studied 8 children aged 7-9. With scaled equipment, the success rate was 58% versus 45% with standard equipment. Shots landed 1.2 m closer laterally and 1.7 m closer longitudinally to targets. — [JSSM via PMC3761839](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3761839)

**Federation coaching style**
- ADM coaching style for ages 5-11:
  - "Cooperative style of coaching and teaching".
  - "Allow children to make decisions on practice and play".
  - "Positive and supportive kid-centered learning environment".
  - "Foster independence".
  
  For 12-18: "Ask kids what they want to do" and "Encourage student led practice". — [USTA ADM Framework](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/coach-organize/content-fragments/resource-library/assets/pdfs/adm-framework.pdf)
- Net Gen plans give a Mission, Goal and Progressions for each activity. They tell the coach to demonstrate ("Coach demonstrates a specific movement, then players copy"). At yellow, the character piece should be led as "a facilitator instead of a lecturer. Ask questions". — [Practice Plan Orange](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Orange.pdf); [Yellow curriculum](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/curriculum-center/coach-samples/assets/Net-Generation-Coaches-Community-Yellow-Ball-Manual-Sample.pdf)
- Net Gen Green teaching tip: moving from the 60-ft to the 78-ft court, "Most players' overall skill level will drop, and that should be expected". Work on court coverage first. — [Green curriculum](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/curriculum-center/coach-samples/assets/Net-Generation-Coaches-Curriculum-Green-Sample.pdf)
- Cue-word style for adults (USTA Tennis 101): "Small swing, short distance", "Turn sideways, controlled toss", "Keep racquet up above the wrist", "Split step as partner hits the ball", "Tap up ball eye-high and bend your knees". — [USTA Tennis 101 for Adults](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/USTA-Tennis-101-for-Adults.pdf)

**Adult learners**
- ITF Coaching & Sport Science Review, "Principles of Adult Learning":
  - Learning should be active, not passive.
  - "Adults need to hit lots of balls"; much instruction should be given individually while adults play.
  - "Learning is individual and personal".
  - Adults differ from children, and coaches often struggle to transfer kids' methods.
  
  — [ITF CSSR – Principles of Adult Learning](https://itfcoachingreview.com/index.php/journal/article/view/453) (search snippet; site returned 403)
- Adults bring experiences, values, expectations and preconceptions. "School lapsers" returning after years away may be apprehensive. — [ITF CSSR article](https://itfcoachingreview.com/index.php/journal/article/download/410/1118/1703) (search snippet)
- Walking Tennis (seniors): "facilitating player self-learning". Technical guidance should be "limited to fundamentals". Keep coaching tips "light and easy to follow" and allow plenty of time for matches. — [LTA Walking Tennis coach guide](https://www.lta.org.uk/4ac92d/siteassets/play/adult/file/coach-guide.pdf)
- PTR runs separate education pathways for 10 and under, 11-17, Performance, and Adult Development. — [search snippet via susanzaro.com – PTR](https://www.susanzaro.com/site/2014/06/06/steve-keller-ptr/) (secondary; older)

### Inferences
- **Ages 3-8:** one cue at a time, a demonstration over explanation, and a game within 2-3 minutes. Use imaginative framing and team challenges, and praise effort, which matches the Net Gen character themes.
- **Ages 8-12:** begin guided discovery ("Where did that ball land? Why?"). Introduce simple tactics through conditioned games.
- **Teens:** questioning, goal-setting, self-analysis and routines, which match the Green 1 competencies.
- **Adults:** explain the "why" briefly. Get them rallying with slower balls in the first session to reduce the fear of looking bad. Give individual feedback while they keep hitting, and respect time limits with turnkey 60-minute structures.

### Gaps
- I could not open the full text of the ITF adult-learning articles (403). Specific claims about adult "fear of looking bad" and time constraints are not directly sourced beyond the apprehension note.
- I found no tennis-specific study on optimal feedback frequency by age.

## 6. Ball-colour transition criteria and assessment checks

### Takeaway
The USTA uses three routes, any one of which moves a child up a colour:
- an age trigger (turn 9 → green, turn 11 → yellow);
- accumulated PlayTracker points;
- for young advanced players, a coach assessment (85%).

Net Generation's assessment sheets score each competency 1-2-3 per level. The ITF and the LTA place players by ability, using ages only as guides.

### Cited Findings
- USTA progression rules:
  - Red → orange: earning 1,000 red play points is recommended first.
  - Orange → green: the player turns 9, earns 1,000 orange play points (10 events), or "achieves 85% on a coach assessment (only for 7 and 8 year olds)".
  - Green → yellow: the player turns 11, or earns 1,500 green PlayTracker points (maximum of 500 green play points).
  - Points: 100 participation points per red or orange event. Green events add win points (100 for singles, 50 for doubles).
  
  — [USTA Midwest – Youth Progression](https://www.usta.com/en/home/play/youth-tennis/programs/midwest/youth-progression.html)
- Net Gen assessment sheets (Pre-Rally, Red 3/2/1, Orange 3/2/1, Green 2/1) score each item 1-2-3 under Head, Feet and Hands, plus Game. Examples:
  - **Red 1:** "Rallies crosscourt, down the line and side to side with partner from baseline"; "Coordinates knee bend with top of toss"; "Can play best-of-three tiebreaks".
  - **Orange 1:** "Can serve to three spots"; "Attacks weak second serve on the rise and comes to net"; "Identifies opponent's game style and weaknesses".
  - **Green 1:** "Combines height, spin and speed to keep opponent deep"; "Plays return of serve on the rise"; "Analyzes own performance after match".
  
  — [Net Gen Assessments Pre-Rally/Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Assessments-Pre-Rally-Red.pdf); [Orange](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Assessments-Orange.pdf); [Green](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Assessments-Green.pdf)
- Net Gen calls its curriculum "competency based", spelling out "the skills and knowledge each player should have before moving to the next ball and court size". — [Green curriculum](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/curriculum-center/coach-samples/assets/Net-Generation-Coaches-Curriculum-Green-Sample.pdf)
- Net Gen Red 2 end-of-level note: players "may not be able to serve and rally from baseline, but they can put the ball in play and rally, and that's good enough!" for a first Play Day. — [Practice Plan Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Red.pdf)
- LTA: "children are placed based on their ability". — [LTA ClubSpark](https://clubspark.lta.org.uk/LlanelliTSC/EventsV2/LTAYouth) (snippet)

### Inferences
- A private coach can turn the final-level sheet of each colour (Red 1, Orange 1, Green 1) into a readiness check: score each item 1-3 and move up at an average of about 2.5 or above. That roughly matches the USTA's 85% coach-assessment threshold.
- Expect a temporary skill drop after each move up, as the Net Gen Green tip warns. Allow about 4-8 weeks of "adapt" sessions (the Orange 3, Green 2 and Yellow 2 objectives).

### Gaps
- I did not find the actual USTA 85% coach-assessment form, or confirm whether it is identical to the Net Gen assessment sheets.
- I did not find the Tennis Australia or ITF progression checklists in primary form. The ITF says ages are recommendations and gives no checklist in the Rules.

## 7. What to work on for typical student goals

### Takeaway
The published programs map onto five common goals:
- **Complete beginners (any age):** slower ball plus "serve, rally, score" from session 1 (ITF Tennis Xpress, USTA Tennis 101).
- **Kids who want to play matches:** competency progression toward the Red 1 / Orange 1 checklists plus Play Days and Team Challenge events.
- **High-school hopefuls:** Yellow-level all-court skills, doubles, and volume management (ADM Stage 2).
- **League players:** NTRP-descriptor gaps (depth, net play, doubles teamwork).
- **Fitness/social players:** Cardio Tennis or Walking Tennis formats.

### Cited Findings
- **Complete beginner adult:** Tennis Xpress is 6 sessions totalling 9 hours, using slower balls toward green-ball competitive points on a full court. It targets complete beginners, coached-but-never-played adults and returners. — [Munster Tennis – Tennis Xpress](https://www.munstertennis.ie/tennis-xpress.html)
- **Complete beginner adult:** USTA Tennis 101 runs 6 classes:
  1. Rally Now
  2. Rally Again!
  3. Get to the Net!
  4. Double Trouble
  5. Hit 'Em High, Hit 'Em Low
  6. Game On!
  
  It progresses through foam/red → orange → green balls and ends with 45 minutes of team match play. — [USTA Tennis 101 for Adults](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/USTA-Tennis-101-for-Adults.pdf)
- **Kids wanting matches:** Net Gen Red 2 says players should be ready for a Play Day and then "join a team" (Junior Team Tennis). The Red 1 competency "Can play best-of-three tiebreaks" sets the match-format target. — [Practice Plan Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Red.pdf); [Competencies](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Competencies-PreRally-Red.pdf)
- **High school / competitive teens (12-18):** ADM recommends 2-6 hrs/week for active participants and 10-16 hrs/week for "Strive to Excel". Events include middle- and high-school play, and fun and socialization remain emphasized "to avoid burnout". — [USTA ADM Framework](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/coach-organize/content-fragments/resource-library/assets/pdfs/adm-framework.pdf)
- **League/NTRP:** a 3.0 player lacks depth and directional control and is uncomfortable at the net. A 3.5 player has directional control but lacks depth and variety. A 4.0 player has depth on both sides plus usable lobs, overheads and volleys. — [NTRP Guidelines Sheet](https://www.gretanc.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/NTRP-Guidelines-Sheet.pdf) (snippet)
- **Fitness/social:** LTA ratio guidance notes cardio tennis sessions can exceed the 1:12 adult ratio. — [LTA coach/player ratios](https://www.lta.org.uk/4abcbb/siteassets/about-lta/file/coach-player-ratios.pdf)
- **Fitness/social:** Walking Tennis emphasizes social interaction, informality and fun, with a post-session social. — [LTA Walking Tennis guide](https://www.lta.org.uk/4ac92d/siteassets/play/adult/file/coach-guide.pdf)
- **Fitness/social:** tennis showed the largest life-expectancy association (+9.7 years) among the sports in the Copenhagen study. — [PubMed 30193744](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30193744/)

### Inferences
- **High-school tryouts** (no source found):
  - a reliable first and second serve, ideally to 2-3 spots (Orange 1 / Green 1 competencies);
  - consistent crosscourt rallies with depth;
  - doubles fundamentals (positioning, poaching, two-up);
  - match routines.
  
  The USTA's own high-school coaching drill videos (Section 8) fit this.
- **League 3.0 → 4.0:**
  1. depth and direction under moderate pace;
  2. net confidence (volley and overhead);
  3. doubles formations and teamwork;
  4. patience and shot tolerance, since NTRP says 4.0 rallies are lost "due to impatience".
- **Senior social players:** doubles, slower or green balls if needed, and balance and footwork warm-ups.

### Gaps
- I found no official tryout criteria from a high-school federation or the USTA.
- No PTR or USPTA adult-development curriculum was accessible to cite for NTRP-specific lesson content.
