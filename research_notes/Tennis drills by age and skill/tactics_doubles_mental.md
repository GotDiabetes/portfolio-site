## Key Question 1: What are well-known situational and live-ball games (King/Queen of the Court, plus-minus, crosscourt-only, approach & pass, serve +1 / return +1, moonball challenges, Up and Back, Canadian doubles, etc.)?

### Takeaway
The standard live-ball games are King/Queen of the Court, Champs & Chumps, One Ball Live (Two Ball), Around the World, Up and Down the River, crosscourt-only half-court points, Plus/Minus scoring, first-strike (serve +1 / return +1) games, approach-and-pass games and Canadian doubles (2v1). All of them are documented with concrete rules by USTA Net Generation plans, USHSTA, Wikipedia's "Tennis games", TeachPE, Sportplan and LTA. Their common design is that points start live (serve or drop-hit, often "two hits to start"), the scoring is modified to reward a tactical goal, and winners and losers rotate to keep groups moving.

### Cited Findings
- **King of the Court:** 3+ players on a full singles court. Points start with a serve or drop hit, and a challenger who wins replaces the "king"; the loser joins the queue. **Champs and Chumps** (6+ players): winners go to the champs line and losers to the chumps line, timed. **One Ball Live:** 8+ players, court split vertically, two balls drop-hit at once; when one point ends, "one ball live" is called and the remaining ball is played across the full court; first to 10. **Around the World:** 4+ players, hit and run to the other side, three errors and you are out. — [Wikipedia: Tennis games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_games)
- **Two Ball rule detail:** after one pair loses a point they call "One ball live!" and the remaining ball is played out among all four players with the court no longer split. The team that wins stays and two new players replace the losers. — [TeachPE tennis games (search-result summary)](https://www.teachpe.com/?p=2163); rules also in [Wikipedia: Tennis games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_games)
- **Crosscourt-only half-court points:** the court is divided along the extended center service line and the ball must stay in the opponent's forehand half. Points use "two hits to start", a backhand loses the point, games go to 4, 7 or 11, then the players repeat on the backhand diagonal. — [TennisOne "Crosscourt Points" (search-result excerpt; full page is behind the TennisPlayer membership)](https://tennisone.tennisplayer.net/content/Lessons/Drills/cosscourt.points.jy/crosscourt.points.php)
- **Plus/Minus:** a clear winner or forced error scores +1, any unforced error scores -1, and the game is played to +7 or -7. It "encourages the players to be more pro-active… but also… not make silly errors". — [Sportplan: Plus and Minus Rewards](https://www.sportplan.net/drills/Tennis/Rally-Drills/Plus-and-Minus-Rewards-TA00011.jsp). The LTA lists a "+/- Five" cumulative variant and "Belts" (win three consecutive points to earn a belt). — [LTA: How to get more from your tennis practices](https://www.lta.org.uk/play/tennis-tips-and-techniques/how-to-get-more-from-your-tennis-practices/)
- **Serve +1 and Return +1:** Tennis Warehouse published a Serve Plus One drill by WTA player Jamie Loeb (verified video below). Coachlife describes a Return +1 drill in which serves come from ¾ court to cut reaction time, and the focus is "the return plus first ball (cross-court or down-the-line)". — [Coachlife: Return +1 Drill](https://www.coachlife.com/videos/from-split-step-to-return-plus-one)
- **Approach & Pass ("Cautious Approach"):** approach shot, then follow it to the net, then the opponent attempts a pass. "Lobs are not allowed", and players swap only when the passer hits a successful pass. — [TeachPE](https://www.teachpe.com/?p=2163)
- **Moonball challenges:** "The Switch – 2" alternates moonballs-only and drives-only by point, to 7. "Small Changes – 4" is a tiebreak in which one player may hit only moonballs, to test "beat the pusher". — [CoachTube: 20 Mental Strength Tennis Drills](https://coachtube.com/course_lesson/best-tennis-drills-for-coaches/20-mental-strength-tennis-drills/19921068)
- **Up and Down the River:** USTA Net Generation (green) uses it for depth games: winner moves up half a court and loser down. — [USTA Net Generation Green Ball Practice & Play Plans (PDF)](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf). The adult social version uses rotating courts with timed rounds of 10–15 minutes and a tiebreak format. — [VisitMO event listing](https://www.visitmo.com/events/up-and-down-the-river-doubles)
- **Canadian doubles (2v1):** "the team of two players can only hit the ball within the single player's singles lines, whilst the single player can hit into the full doubles court". — [Wikipedia: Canadian doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_doubles)
- **"Up and Back":** USTA Net Generation Yellow 1 has a cooperative "Up and Back Volley" (slice approach, split, volley, retreat, alternating). — [USTA Net Generation Yellow Ball Plans (PDF)](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Yellow.pdf)

#### Drill library A — Singles tactics & point play

**S1. Crosscourt Half-Court Points (crosscourt-only game)**
- Ages / stage: yellow juniors 11-14; teens 14-18; adult intermediate; adult advanced. For green 9-11, use the Net Gen "Half-Court Battle" variant below. (Stage mapping inferred.)
- Level: intermediate (advanced with progressions)
- Skill area: singles tactics / point play
- Works on / fixes: hitting to the middle with no plan; changing direction too early; building crosscourt consistency and dominance.
- Players & equipment: 2 per diagonal (4 per court using both diagonals); a basket of balls; throw-down lines (TDLs) or cones to mark the center line extension.
- Setup: Split the court lengthwise along the extended center service line, from the center mark to the net and through to the opposite center mark. Both players stand on the forehand diagonal (deuce-to-deuce for two right-handers). The ball must land in the opponent's forehand half: singles sideline to center line, net to baseline, alley out. — [TennisOne Crosscourt Points](https://tennisone.tennisplayer.net/content/Lessons/Drills/cosscourt.points.jy/crosscourt.points.php)
- Steps / rules / scoring: Drop-hit to start; the point goes live after each player has hit one ball ("two hits to start"). Forehands only, so hitting a backhand loses the point. Games to 4, 7 or 11, then repeat on the backhand diagonal. — same source
- Coaching cues: "Crosscourt = more court, lower net." "Aggressive patience." "Change direction down the line when the time is right." (Rationale from [TalkingTennis, Lizzie Flint, LTA Level 3](https://talkingtennis.net/blog-posts/smart-tennis-why-you-should-hit-a-lot-more-balls-cross-court).) "Height over the middle of the net" is inferred.
- Progression: Net Gen "Half-Court Battle" (green ball): serve to start, alternating every two serves, forehands only, games to 7. Progressions are to play straight ahead then crosscourt, and then the server may hit forehands only. — [USTA Net Gen Green PDF](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf). Another option (inferred): allow one down-the-line change of direction on a short ball, as in S6.
- Regression (inferred): cooperative crosscourt rally count first (for example, a 10-ball streak), then compete. Use a green ball or the 60-ft court.
- Source URL: https://tennisone.tennisplayer.net/content/Lessons/Drills/cosscourt.points.jy/crosscourt.points.php
- Video (related topic, not this exact drill): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ESYuG4qObNc — "Tennis Tactics - Where To Aim In Singles" — Top Tennis Training - Pro Tennis Lessons

**S2. Deep-Ball Rally (past-the-service-line game)**
- Ages / stage: teens 14-18 (high school source); yellow juniors 11-14; adult beginner; adult intermediate (stage mapping inferred)
- Level: beginner–intermediate
- Skill area: point play / singles tactics (depth)
- Works on / fixes: going for too much (control over power), short balls, impatience.
- Players & equipment: 2+ per court; chalk or TDLs.
- Setup: Players stand on the baseline, either full singles court or one half of the court (straight ahead or crosscourt). The service line is the "deep line".
- Steps / rules / scoring: Players start rallying, and the point is "live" after each player has hit once. "Players must hit balls past the service line, or they lose the point." First to a set number of points wins. Rotate King-of-the-Court style: winners move up one court and losers move down. — [USHSTA Deep-Ball Rally](https://ushsta.org/?p=638)
- Coaching cues: "Control is a much more important skill… than power" (USHSTA). Height buys depth: Sportplan's "Height for Depth" puts racquets in the net to force height. — [Sportplan Height for Depth](https://www.sportplan.net/drills/Tennis/Rally-Drills/Height-for-Depth-TA00050.jsp). "Twice net height over the net" is inferred.
- Progression: move the deep line back to ¾ court with chalk; require players to stand inside the baseline (half-volley/short-hop practice); draw a line 1–2 ft behind the baseline; after 5+ minutes, pause for a technique review and then resume. — [USHSTA](https://ushsta.org/?p=638)
- Regression (inferred): cooperative count of balls past the service line; doubles-width court.
- Source URL: https://ushsta.org/?p=638
- Video: none found that shows this drill. General alternative (content not confirmed to include it): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n-yJ_FhW8os — "Simple Yet Powerful Tennis Singles Drills To Improve Your Game Quickly" — Meike Babel Tennis

**S3. Up and Down the River (green ball depth game)**
- Ages / stage: green 9-11
- Level: beginner–intermediate
- Skill area: point play (depth and consistency)
- Works on / fixes: short, flat balls; inconsistent rallies.
- Players & equipment: two pairs per court; green-dot balls; 25"+ racquets on the 78-ft court; TDLs. — [USTA Net Gen Green PDF](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)
- Setup: Half-courts with boundaries from the middle line to the doubles line. TDLs split each half from the service line to the baseline, creating a deep target zone.
- Steps / rules / scoring: Start points after a serve and return. Both players must let the ball bounce (no volleys). Play to 4 points, then volley cooperatively until the last court finishes. When all courts are done, the winner moves up half a court and the loser moves down. Goal: "Use height, spin, and speed to keep ball deep."
- Coaching cues: "Height, spin, speed = depth." "Roll it deep."
- Progression: (1) start points straight ahead with one stroke only; (2) crosscourt with the outside stroke only. (USTA)
- Regression (inferred): cooperative rally into the deep zone; coach feeds the first ball.
- Source URL: https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf
- Video (related, a full green-ball class): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DVRyIjzJ3kI — "Complete Green Ball Class: Rally, Volley, & Point Play" — New Rochelle Racquet Club

**S4. Sideliner (angle game)**
- Ages / stage: green 9-11 (also useful for yellow juniors 11-14, inferred)
- Level: intermediate
- Skill area: singles tactics (width and angles)
- Works on / fixes: hitting everything to the middle; not using the court's width.
- Players & equipment: 4–6 per court; TDLs; green balls.
- Setup: Half-court crosscourt diagonals split by TDLs, with alleys counting. Two pairs can play at the same time.
- Steps / rules / scoring: All points crosscourt. Start with a serve, then players must hit the outside stroke. Score for winning the point and for forcing the opponent outside the sideline. Use an up-and-down-the-river or champion-of-the-court format. Goal: "Use spin and proper height to move ball off court." — [USTA Net Gen Green PDF](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)
- Coaching cues: "Pull them past the sideline." "Spin + height = angle." (inferred)
- Progression: (1) every ball must bounce; (2) play the full court. (USTA)
- Regression (inferred): cooperative "mini-tennis angles" inside the service boxes (the Net Gen warm-up "Mini-Tennis Angles" aims to make the ball bounce in the alley).
- Source URL: same USTA Green PDF
- Video (related, green-ball point play): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ngCFXzmP398 — "Green Ball Class: Serve and Point Play" — New Rochelle Racquet Club

**S5. Defend and Recover (angle + recovery game)**
- Ages / stage: green 9-11 (Net Gen Green 2 and Green 1); yellow juniors 11-14 (inferred)
- Level: intermediate
- Skill area: singles tactics / point play
- Works on / fixes: not recovering after a wide ball; using only one direction.
- Players & equipment: teams of 2–3 on opposite baselines; green balls; cones or poly-spots for the recovery spot.
- Setup: The feeder stands on the far right side of the baseline, behind the alley. The "angler" starts at the center of the baseline.
- Steps / rules / scoring: The feeder feeds crosscourt to the service box, then recovers to center and split-steps with the left foot on the center mark. The angler moves up, plays a short-court angle, recovers to the baseline, and the point is played out. Rotate after each point, play games to 7 and switch roles. Switch the feed side every other game. Goal: "Move opponent with combinations of shots (angle/down-the-line)." — [USTA Net Gen Green PDF](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)
- Coaching cues: "Hit and recover to the middle of their possible replies." "Split as they hit." (inferred)
- Progression: (1) the angler plays the first ball down the line and can come in; (2) the angler may choose angle or down the line; (3) an angle must bounce inside the service box, and a down-the-line ball must bounce past the service line. (USTA)
- Regression (inferred): feeder-only (no live point); hand feed.
- Source URL: same USTA Green PDF
- Video: none found that shows this drill.

**S6. I'm the Boss / Who's the Boss? (change-of-direction rule game)**
- Ages / stage: green 9-11 ("I'm the Boss", Green 1); yellow juniors 11-14 ("Who's the Boss?", Yellow 1 chapter); adaptable to adult intermediate (inferred)
- Level: intermediate–advanced
- Skill area: singles tactics (when to change direction)
- Works on / fixes: going down the line from a defensive position; no plan.
- Players & equipment: teams of 2–3, full-court points; balls.
- Setup: The serving team members are the "bosses". Serve from the baseline.
- Steps / rules / scoring: The return goes crosscourt to the server, and the point continues crosscourt with outside strokes. Only the boss may change direction, and only with a forehand; returners must hit everything crosscourt. After the first change of direction, play is open. The boss gets a bonus point for changing direction with a forehand and winning the point. On ad-side points (right-handers), the boss may change direction with an inside-in forehand. Games go to 7, then switch servers. Goal: "Wait for short ball or sitter on baseline to change directions." — [USTA Net Gen Green PDF](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)
- Coaching cues: "Crosscourt until the short ball." "Change direction on your forehand, from inside the baseline."
- Progression: (1) outside stroke only, then any stroke during the rally; (2) the boss gets a negative point for losing a point on a change of direction; (3) both sides can be boss. (USTA)
- Regression (inferred): the coach feeds a designated short ball as the "change" cue.
- Source URL: same USTA Green PDF (Yellow version listed in the [Yellow PDF](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Yellow.pdf) table of contents)
- Video: none found that shows this drill.

**S7. Serve +1 (first-strike game)**
- Ages / stage: green 9-11 ("Team Up and Down the River"); yellow juniors 11-14; teens 14-18; adult intermediate; adult advanced (inferred mapping)
- Level: intermediate–advanced
- Skill area: point play / singles tactics (serving situation)
- Works on / fixes: serving with no plan for the next ball; passive first shot.
- Players & equipment: teams or pairs; balls; TDLs to mark the 60-ft court sidelines inside the 78-ft court for the green version.
- Setup (USTA green version): Start the point with a serve and return, and the returner hits into the 60-ft-court sideline.
- Steps / rules / scoring: The server tries to move the opponent around and use the extra space. Play one point, rotate, play to 7 and switch servers. After both sides have served, the winning team moves up a court. Goal: "Use all five controls to move opponent: direction, height, depth, speed and spin." — [USTA Net Gen Green PDF](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf). Adult/teen version "Four or Less": points count only if won in 4 shots or fewer, played in 5-minute rounds. — [CoachTube 20 Mental Strength Drills](https://coachtube.com/course_lesson/best-tennis-drills-for-coaches/20-mental-strength-tennis-drills/19921068)
- Coaching cues: "Serve to a spot, then hit your forehand to the open court." "Serve + forehand." (inferred)
- Progression: (1) the server must hit a forehand on the second ball; (2) use the 78-ft-court sidelines after the return and next ball (USTA). Adults (inferred): name the serve target (T or wide) and the +1 target before each point.
- Regression (inferred): the coach acts as returner with a controlled return to the middle.
- Source URL: https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf

**S8. Return the Cannon / Return +1 (returning situation)**
- Ages / stage: green 9-11 ("Return the Cannon"); teens 14-18; adult intermediate; adult advanced (Return +1) (inferred mapping)
- Level: intermediate
- Skill area: point play (returning situation)
- Works on / fixes: missed or blocked returns; no plan after the return.
- Players & equipment: two pairs per court on half-courts; TDLs.
- Setup: Split the court in half with TDLs and play all points crosscourt. The server stands **on the service line**.
- Steps / rules / scoring: The server tries to hit an unreturnable serve. The server wins the point if the returner can't return crosscourt; the returner wins the point for a crosscourt return. Play to 4, switch roles, then switch sides. — [USTA Net Gen Green PDF](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)
- Coaching cues: "Gentle split step, racquet back early, meet it in front." Against big servers, "return through the center". — [Coachlife Return +1](https://www.coachlife.com/videos/from-split-step-to-return-plus-one)
- Progression: (1) play out the point on the half-court, with the returner scoring 2 for winning the rally; (2) serve from between the service line and baseline (USTA). Adults: Coachlife Return +1, where serves come from ¾ court and the focus is the return plus the first ball crosscourt or down the line.
- Regression (inferred): the server stands at the baseline with second serves only.
- Source URL: https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf
- Video: none on YouTube found that shows this drill. Coachlife hosts its own video at the page above, which is not a YouTube link. Related USTA video in M2.

**S9. Approach & Pass (Cautious Approach / Two-on-One Attack)**
- Ages / stage: green 9-11 (Two-on-One Attack); yellow juniors 11-14; teens 14-18; adult intermediate; adult advanced (inferred mapping)
- Level: intermediate–advanced
- Skill area: point play (approaching vs passing situations)
- Works on / fixes: approaching off the wrong ball; not closing; no passing-shot options.
- Players & equipment: 2 (Cautious Approach) or 4–5 (Two-on-One Attack); balls.
- Setup: Cautious Approach uses one player at the baseline (passer) and one who approaches. Two-on-One Attack puts a net player and a baseliner on one side covering the doubles court, with extra players lined up behind the baseliner. The single "champ" on the opposite baseline covers a half-court from the middle to the doubles line.
- Steps / rules / scoring: **Cautious Approach:** hit the approach, follow it to the net, and the opponent attempts a pass. "Lobs are not allowed". Swap only when the passer hits a successful pass. — [TeachPE](https://www.teachpe.com/?p=2163). **Two-on-One Attack:** the champ gets one serve and the returner comes to the net, then the point is played out two-on-one. The champ can't lob the first two balls. After each point, the baseliner moves to the net and the net player to the back of the line. After 7 points, a new champ comes over. — [USTA Net Gen Green PDF](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)
- Coaching cues: "Take return early, use hop move to approach net" (USTA). Passers need three passes: "down-the-line, short-angle and topspin lob" (USTA Triples coaching tip).
- Progression: (1) the champ must lob within the first two shots; (2) the return must be a forehand (USTA). Allow lobs in Cautious Approach (inferred).
- Regression (inferred): the coach feeds the approach ball, and the passer may only pass down the line.
- Source URL: https://www.teachpe.com/?p=2163 ; https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf

**S10. King/Queen of the Court (singles)**
- Ages / stage: all stages, from green 9-11 through senior (inferred). Groups of 3–6.
- Level: beginner–advanced (adjust the start method)
- Skill area: point play / group
- Works on / fixes: match intensity; playing one point at a time; keeping large groups active.
- Players & equipment: 3+ players; one court; balls.
- Setup: The "king/queen" stands on one side; challengers line up behind the opposite baseline.
- Steps / rules / scoring: A challenger plays a singles point started with a serve or drop hit. If the challenger wins, they replace the king; the loser goes to the back of the queue. The king scores a point for each point won while on the throne, and the coach times rounds. — [Wikipedia: Tennis games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_games). USHSTA uses an elimination King/Queen format for its short crosscourt angle drill. — [USHSTA Short, Crosscourt Angles](https://ushsta.org/?p=372)
- Coaching cues: "One point at a time." "Start every point with a plan." (inferred)
- Progression (inferred): the challenger must win 2 in a row to take over; serve starts only; the king must win with a forcing shot.
- Regression (inferred): coach feed starts; cooperative "3-ball rally before live".
- Source URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_games

**S11. Plus/Minus (+7 / −7)**
- Ages / stage: teens 14-18; adult intermediate; adult advanced (Sportplan rates it intermediate–advanced); yellow juniors 11-14 (inferred)
- Level: intermediate–advanced
- Skill area: point play
- Works on / fixes: going for too much / unforced errors; also passive "not-losing" play.
- Players & equipment: 2; full court; balls.
- Setup: Full singles court, with points started from the hand (drop feed).
- Steps / rules / scoring: +1 for a clear winner or forced error, −1 for any unforced error. First to +7 or −7. — [Sportplan](https://www.sportplan.net/drills/Tennis/Rally-Drills/Plus-and-Minus-Rewards-TA00011.jsp). LTA variant: "+/- Five". — [LTA](https://www.lta.org.uk/play/tennis-tips-and-techniques/how-to-get-more-from-your-tennis-practices/)
- Coaching cues: "Win the point, don't just avoid losing it" (Sportplan's emphasis). "Make them hit one more" (inferred).
- Progression (inferred): start with a serve; −2 for a double fault or an unforced error inside the first 4 shots.
- Regression (inferred): +/−5; ball must land past the service line (combine with S2).

**S12. Moonball Challenge ("The Switch – 2" / "beat the pusher" tiebreak)**
- Ages / stage: yellow juniors 11-14; teens 14-18; adult intermediate (league players who lose to pushers) (inferred)
- Level: intermediate
- Skill area: singles tactics / mental (patience)
- Works on / fixes: frustration or errors against moonballers; impatience.
- Players & equipment: 2; full court.
- Setup: Full singles court.
- Steps / rules / scoring: **Switch – 2:** players alternate by point between "ONLY high balls (moonballs)" and "ONLY regular (drive) shots", to 7 points. **Small Changes – 4:** a regular tiebreak where one player "is allowed to hit only moon balls", to test the ability to "beat the pusher". — [CoachTube 20 Mental Strength Drills](https://coachtube.com/course_lesson/best-tennis-drills-for-coaches/20-mental-strength-tennis-drills/19921068)
- Coaching cues: "Patience is a weapon." "Take it early or step in and swing high-to-high." (inferred)
- Progression (inferred): the attacking player may only win the point at the net (swinging volley or overhead).
- Regression (inferred): cooperative rally of moonballs landing past the service line (S2 rules).
- Source URL: https://coachtube.com/course_lesson/best-tennis-drills-for-coaches/20-mental-strength-tennis-drills/19921068
- Video: none found that shows this drill.

**S13. Crazy Feed (heavy-topspin attack/defend)**
- Ages / stage: green 9-11 (Net Gen Green 2); yellow juniors 11-14 (inferred)
- Level: intermediate
- Skill area: singles tactics (attacking with height and spin)
- Works on / fixes: flat, short attacking balls; not using the court's length and width.
- Players & equipment: teams of 3–4 on opposite baselines; green balls.
- Setup: Full-court points, two players at a time. One side attacks and the other defends.
- Steps / rules / scoring: The attacker self-feeds a deep ball from behind the baseline, hitting high and deep to the opponent's weakness, then the point is played out. The attacker can't come to the net off the feed. Rotate every point. Attackers score 1 for winning a point and defenders score 2. First to 7, then change teams or order. Goal: "Take advantage of increased length and width by rolling balls deep to corners." — [USTA Net Gen Green PDF](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)
- Coaching cues: "Loop it high and heavy." "Push them behind the baseline." (inferred)
- Progression: (1) self-feed forehands, then backhands; (2) the defender feeds a high, deep ball with little spin and the attacker hits back with heavy topspin. (USTA)
- Regression (inferred): the coach feeds; points count only if the attacker's ball lands past the service line.
- Source URL: same USTA Green PDF
- Video: none found that shows this drill.

### Inferences
- Across sources, the live-ball games share three design levers: how the point starts (serve, drop-hit, "two hits to start"), modified scoring that rewards the tactic (bonus for a change of direction, +1/−1, wide-ball points), and a court restriction (half court, crosscourt only, service-line deep zone). A coach can turn any of the five classic game situations (serving, returning, rallying, approaching, passing) into a game by adjusting these three levers.
- The same games scale from green-ball kids to adults mostly by ball and court (green ball on the 78-ft court, or the 60-ft court) and start method (feed, then serve), not by changing the rules.

### Gaps
- No primary source was found for a named game called "21" or "Two-bounce" (a rally where the ball may bounce twice) with standard rules. Those are not documented here.
- TennisOne/TennisPlayer pages are behind a membership and returned certificate errors when fetched. The crosscourt-points rules come from the search-result excerpt.
- I found no reliable source for standard scoring of Canadian doubles. One unattributed search summary said the single player serves until the pair wins two points against them, and games go to 11, win by 2. That has not been confirmed with a source.

## Key Question 2: How do coaches teach basic singles tactics (crosscourt percentages, depth, the game situations) and doubles basics (formations, poaching, I-formation, communication) at each level?

### Takeaway
Coaches teach singles tactics through ball controls and percentage play. USTA Net Generation builds green and yellow juniors on "five ball controls" (direction, height, depth, speed, spin) applied in tactical games. Crosscourt is the default because the net is 6 inches lower in the center and the diagonal is 82.5 ft (singles) or 86 ft (doubles) versus 78 ft down the line. Doubles basics progress from one-up/one-back positioning and all-position rotation, to diagonal poaching off the serve, to I-formation and Australian formations with signals for league and high-school players.

### Cited Findings
- The net is "3 ½ feet" at the post and "just 3 feet" in the middle. The down-the-line length is 78 ft, the crosscourt singles diagonal is 82½ ft and the doubles diagonal is 86 ft. Players should use "aggressive patience" crosscourt, then "change the direction of the ball and finish the point down the line when the time is right". — [TalkingTennis (Lizzie Flint, LTA Level 3 coach)](https://talkingtennis.net/blog-posts/smart-tennis-why-you-should-hit-a-lot-more-balls-cross-court)
- USTA Net Generation practice plans have this structure: warm-up (partner-based control and the theme), skill development (movement plus "all five ball controls (direction, height, depth, speed, spin)"), then games where "skills learned from practices will be applied to tactical situations". Green is played on the 78-ft court with a green-dot ball and 25"+ racquets. — [USTA Net Gen Green PDF](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)
- The Net Gen Green chapter themes trace a tactical progression: Going Green, Open Up the Court, Put It in Neutral, Attack and Defend, Serve to Spots, Make the Pass, Return to Sender, Game On! (Green 2); then Hit It Heavy, I'm the Boss, Drop It, Three Spots, Make Your Move, Double Trouble, Crack the Code, Prime Time (Green 1). — same PDF
- The Net Gen Yellow chapters are Keep it Deep, Whatcha Got?, Double Trouble, Over and Under, Move It, Over My Head, Team Up, Let's Play (Yellow 2); then Open it Up, Ridiculous Return, Spin It, Attack Mode, Offense/Defense, Who's the Boss?, Dubz Rules, Prime Time (Yellow 1). — [USTA Net Gen Yellow PDF](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Yellow.pdf)
- Tennis Australia frames strategy around four areas: serving, returning, baseline and net play. — [Tennis Australia strategy (search-result excerpt; direct fetch returned 403)](https://tennis.com.au/learn/how-to-play/strategy)
- In the I-formation, the net player "crouches down… on the centre service line" and uses hand signals behind the back. The server typically moves to the side opposite the net player. The goal is to intercept the return. — [Wikipedia: I-formation (tennis)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-formation_(tennis))
- In the I-formation the net player sits about 10 ft from the net, halfway between the net and service line, and advances at the sound of the serve. The server stands near the center aligned with the partner, and the ideal serve is down the T. Mix the I with normal Australian, which is "more complicated" and needs advance partner discussion. — [Kathleen Krajco, TennisServer "Wild Cards"](https://www.tennisserver.com/wildcards/wildcards_02_10.html)
- Poaching technique: move "on an angle… finishing two steps past the center service line". The volley goes "at the feet of the opposing opponent" or "on a sharp angle". — [USHSTA Poaching on an Angle](https://ushsta.org/?p=542). Net players should read opponents and not turn to watch their partner. — [USHSTA Rally Poaching](https://ushsta.org/?p=558)
- USTA coaching tip: "To develop a good poaching mindset, tell players to poach as if they are alone on his/her side of the court." The poacher "moves diagonally forward as serve bounces." — [USTA Net Gen Yellow PDF](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Yellow.pdf)
- USTA doubles positioning goal for yellow ball: "Move with ball at net, rally crosscourt from baseline." Coaches pause play to ask players how they responded to doubles situations. — same Yellow PDF
- Senior doubles and singles tips: start inside the baseline, keep points short with strong serves and returns, use the deep-ball/drop-shot/lob combination, use the lob "to break the rhythm or catch your breath", and use the slice backhand to keep the ball low. — [Brian Walters, "Tennis tips for senior players", SW News Media](https://swnewsmedia.com/lakeshore_weekly/news/opinion/columnists/commentary-tennis-tips-for-senior-players/article_1c2a8197-2623-5dd4-b459-6714ccdf3db9.html)

#### Drill library B — Doubles

**D1. Rally Poaching (crosscourt rally with live net players)**
- Ages / stage: teens 14-18 (high school source); adult intermediate; adult advanced (inferred)
- Level: intermediate
- Skill area: doubles
- Works on / fixes: frozen net players; watching their partner instead of the opponents.
- Players & equipment: 4; balls.
- Setup: Two baseliners stand crosscourt from each other on one diagonal. Each baseliner's partner starts **at the service line** in the other half of the court.
- Steps / rules / scoring: Either baseliner drop-hits and the two rally crosscourt. "As soon as the second baseline player hits the ball, the service line players may move to net positions." Baseliners use depth, short balls, spin and speed to force errors. Net players watch their baseline opponents and pick off weak balls. No lobs unless both opponents are at the net. Play 10 points, then baseliners and net players swap and play 10 more. Step 5 variation: start with second serves only, where one fault loses the point and each player serves 4+ points. — [USHSTA Rally Poaching](https://ushsta.org/?p=558)
- Coaching cues: "Watch their racquet, not your partner." "Pick off the floater."
- Progression: second-serve start (above). Inferred: allow down-the-line passes at the net player.
- Regression (inferred): net players start already at net; the baseline rally must stay cooperative for 3 balls before going live.
- Source URL: https://ushsta.org/?p=558
- Video (topic match): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S7g7z6RJtKA — "Tennis Drills - Doubles Tactics - Situation Drills - Doubles Poaching Drill #1" — Rope Tennis Education

**D2. Poaching on an Angle (feed drill)**
- Ages / stage: teens 14-18; adult intermediate; yellow juniors 11-14 (inferred)
- Level: beginner–intermediate
- Skill area: doubles
- Works on / fixes: poaching sideways along the net (which loses angle and time); bad poach footwork.
- Players & equipment: one line of players plus the coach; basket.
- Setup: Player A stands at the net in the deuce court, with others lined up behind the service line. The coach stands on the baseline opposite Player A.
- Steps / rules / scoring: (1) A volleys the coach's first feed back to the coach. (2) The coach plays the ball crosscourt, simulating a return; use a bounce-feed if control is limited. (3) As the ball bounces, A "moves to poach immediately… on an angle… finishing two steps past the center service line". (4) A volleys at the feet of the opposing net player or on a sharp angle away from both. (5) A rotates to the end of the line. — [USHSTA](https://ushsta.org/?p=542)
- Coaching cues: "Go forward AND across." "Two steps past the center line." "Volley to the feet or the angle."
- Progression (inferred): live returner chooses crosscourt or down the line; poacher fakes or goes.
- Regression: start at the service line with a mid-court volley before the poach (USHSTA variation).
- Source URL: https://ushsta.org/?p=542
- Video (topic match, footwork): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iURc4-VGurs — "The CORRECT Poaching Footwork for Doubles Tennis" — Coach Adrian

**D3. Poach and Play (live poach off the serve)**
- Ages / stage: green 9-11 (Green 1 "Double Trouble"); yellow juniors 11-14 (Yellow 1); adult intermediate (inferred)
- Level: intermediate
- Skill area: doubles
- Works on / fixes: not moving at the net; late poach timing.
- Players & equipment: 4–6 per court; balls.
- Setup: One up / one back formation, with the coach as returner at first.
- Steps / rules / scoring: Start the point with a serve, and the return must go crosscourt. The server's partner "moves diagonally forward as serve bounces, and intercepts return as volley". Play out the point. Adjust scoring and rotation to the group size and give equal time on the deuce and ad courts. Goal: "Time poach so that movement to volley is continuous." — [USTA Net Gen Green PDF](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf); [Yellow PDF](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Yellow.pdf)
- Coaching cues: "Go on the bounce of the serve." "Poach as if you're alone on your side" (USTA tip).
- Progression: (1) players return serves; (2) the returner's partner poaches off the second-serve return; (3) the returner can hit crosscourt or down the line, and the net player can fake, stay or poach (USTA). Home challenge: "Poach a minimum of one time per game when partner serves."
- Regression (inferred): the coach hand-feeds the "return" to a fixed spot.
- Source URL: Net Gen Green/Yellow PDFs above

**D4. I-Formation Serving Games**
- Ages / stage: teens 14-18 (high school doubles); adult intermediate; adult advanced (league 3.5–4.5) (inferred; Wikipedia says the formation suits league, club and intermediate players)

**D6. Gladiator Dubz (approach on the short ball, Champs vs Challengers)**
- Ages / stage: yellow juniors 11-14 (Yellow 1); teens 14-18; adult intermediate (inferred)
- Level: intermediate
- Skill area: doubles (transition, getting two up)
- Works on / fixes: staying back on short balls; one up/one back gap.
- Players & equipment: pairs, up to 4 pairs per court.
- Setup: The Champs team is on one side. Challenger teams line up behind the opposite baseline. Both teams start one up / one back.
- Steps / rules / scoring: Challengers serve to the Champs and play the point. "After serve, if ball bounces inside service box, baseliner must come to net." Challengers rotate after each point. Challengers keep score, and on reaching 2 points they must shout "Gladiator" before serving. If they shout and win the point, they take the Champs spot (no shout means no takeover). Countdown 3-2-1 when new Champs come over. Goal: "take short ball on the rise and split-step inside service line." — [USTA Net Gen Yellow PDF](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Yellow.pdf)
- Coaching cues: "Short ball = go." "Split inside the service line."
- Progression: (1) Challengers take over only if the point is won with a volley or overhead; (2) players decide which ball to approach on. (USTA)
- Regression (inferred): the coach feeds a short ball to start every point.
- Source URL: Yellow PDF above

### Inferences
- The USTA sequence for juniors is clear: one up/one back positioning (All Position Doubles), then diagonal poaching off the serve (Poach and Play), then transition to two up (Gladiator Dubz, Both Sides Attack). That sequence suits adult beginners and intermediates too, with I-formation and Australian added for adult 3.5–4.5 league and high-school players.
- For seniors, the Walters tips (start inside the baseline, short points, lobs, low slices) suggest favoring D7 (lob games), D5 (positioning) and short-point serve/return games over long-rally drills.

### Gaps
- I found no source that confirms the exact phrase "five game situations" as a USTA/PTR standard. One search summary says it is "not universally standardized". Tennis Australia uses four areas (serving, returning, baseline, net). ## Key Question 3: What mental-skills practices are recommended (between-point routines like Loehr's 16-second cure, breathing, cue words, pressure scoring in practice)?

### Takeaway
The consensus among sport-psychology and federation sources (Loehr, USTA Player Development, ITF, LTA) is: (1) a structured between-point routine (Loehr's four stages: positive physical response, relaxation, preparation, ritual); (2) simple tools inside it (deep breathing, short cue words, imagery); and (3) deliberately created stress in practice through modified scoring (start at 0-30 or deuce, sudden death at 40, second serves only, three-in-a-row). Routines are how the mental skills transfer to matches.

### Cited Findings
- Loehr's "16-second Cure", repeated before every point: (1) positive body language, "throw your shoulders back and walk purposefully"; (2) relaxation, "walk around… looking at your strings, so your eyes have a place to rest"; (3) preparation, "a confident gaze across the net as you decide where to hit your first shot"; (4) "Go through your serve or return rituals". Loehr also noted you can choke from dehydration or lack of sleep. Psychologist Alexandra Guhde warns that racquet smashing means "we rehearse our rage." — [Tennis.com, "Playing with fire"](https://www.tennis.com/news/articles/playing-with-fire-the-delicate-role-of-emotion-in-pro-and-rec-tennis)
- The 16 seconds is "a starting place and was never intended to be the final formula for anyone". Players must build their own rituals, and how players use the intervals between points is "the single most important factor in developing mental toughness". — [TennisOne Loehr training (search-result excerpt; page returned certificate error)](https://tennisone.tennisplayer.net/content/MentalToughness/Loehr/training2/train2.htm)
- Context: James Loehr died in 2026 at 83. — [Boston Globe, 2026-05-07 (search-result title)](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/05/07/metro/james-loehr-who-showed-athletes-power-mind-dies-83/)
- USTA Player Development: players should develop "breathing, positive and productive self-talk, visualization/imagery and goal-setting", integrated "via routines and creating stress in practice". Routines are "a series of behaviors and thoughts a player goes through to get to the next serve or return". Cue examples are "in control," "trust it," "go for it", plus tactical cues. Djokovic is cited deep-breathing before his return stance. — [USTA PD "Habits for Tennis Success" series: mental skills foundation](https://playerdevelopment.usta.com/About-USTA/Player-Development/habits_for_tennis_success_develop_the_mental_skills_foundation), [package mental skills into routines](https://www.playerdevelopment.usta.com/news/habits_for_tennis_success_package_mental_skills_into_routines), [training the mindset in practice](https://www.playerdevelopment.usta.com/news/habits_for_tennis_success_training_the_mindset_and_skills_in_practice/). These quotes come from search-result excerpts; the direct fetches returned 404, so the pages may have moved.
- ITF framework: the ITF Tennis Psychology manual's "5 Cs" are Commitment, Concentration, Control (of emotion), Confidence and Communication. The tools are goal setting, visualisation, self-talk and breathing, with exercises built into everyday squad training. — [ITF Coaching & Sport Science Review, "Integrating mental skills into your coaching" (search-result excerpt; fetch returned 403)](https://www.itfcoachingreview.com/index.php/journal/article/view/64)
- Pressure training "is efficacious as a means of enhancing performance and developing mental skills" when controlled and coach-guided. — [Univ. of Jyväskylä thesis, "Creating pressure training environment for elite tennis players" (search-result excerpt)](https://jyx.jyu.fi/jyx/Record/jyx_123456789_87780/Details)
- LTA pressure ideas: "Second serves only", "Start every service game 0-30 down", "Two points if you win the point at the net", "Rally of four before you can win a point", and having someone watch or record you. — [LTA (coach Matt Smith)](https://www.lta.org.uk/play/tennis-tips-and-techniques/how-to-get-more-from-your-tennis-practices/)
- Breathing: 4-7-8 (inhale 4 s, hold 7 s, exhale 8 s) and box breathing (4-4-4-4) to slow heart rate between points. — [On, "Mental strength in tennis: 10 tips"](https://www.on.com/en-in/stories/tennis-mental-strength). This is a brand blog, not a federation source, so treat it as practical guidance.
- Cue words after errors: "Next Time," "Nice Try," "Get Back," "Rebound". Five steps: identify the error types that trigger negative talk, notice where attention goes, pick a positive phrase, practise it in practice and matches, and visualize the error followed by the cue. Cites Karageorghis & Terry, *Inside Sport Psychology* (2010). — [Physical Education Update](https://www.physicaleducationupdate.com/public/860.cfm)
- Closing out: "Take three or four deep breaths". Play to win, not to lose. Gigi Fernandez suggests reversing the score mentally (at 5-3, think "3–5"), citing Roberta Vinci at the 2015 US Open. — [Tennis.com, Gigi Fernandez, "Mental tricks to closing out matches"](https://prod.tennis.com/news/articles/mental-tricks-to-closing-out-matches). "Get the first one in… take a little pace off"; "Play within yourself and stick with the tactics that helped you get where you are." — [Tennis.com, "Tip: serving out the match" (2010)](https://www.tennis.com/news/articles/tip-serving-out-the-match)

## Key Question 4: What drills keep adult group clinics fun and active (Cardio Tennis / USTA)?

### Takeaway
Cardio Tennis is a USTA-backed, music-driven group format. It uses low-compression balls, targets 65–85% of maximum heart rate, rotates players through hitting and sideline-fitness stations, and works best with about 10–12 per court. Its sessions run warm-up, theme, drills, conditioned points, then cool-down. The most practical group games for adult and senior social clinics are the rotation games (King of the Court, One Ball Live, Champs & Chumps, Around the World, Wipe Out, Team Doubles, Up and Down the River).

### Cited Findings
- Cardio Tennis has four core components: (1) low-compression balls (orange or green on a full court; red or sponge on red or badminton courts); (2) monitor heart rates, working in the cardio zone of 65–85% of max HR, with regular checks either manually or by wearable; (3) sideline activities so all students are active all of the time; (4) always have music playing. Optimum is 10–12 per court, with at least 5 per court for drills to work. Lesson structure: Warm Up, theme and key shots, Drills, Conditioned Points, Cool Down. The six themes are Baseline Warrior, Get Into Win, Got It Covered, Serve and Return, Working Together, Putting it All Together. — [LTA Cardio Tennis Introduction (Secondary Schools) PDF](https://lta.org.uk/4912a5/siteassets/roles/teachers/file/cardio-tennis/cardio-tennis-introduction.pdf)
- USTA Cardio Tennis drill (Michael Harper, USTA Manager for Cardio Tennis): mini-tennis with four players, short strokes to "keep the rally going", and players "tap the doubles alley before rotating back in". A challenge variation is to "sprint to the back fence". — [USTA: Cardio Tennis with Michael Harper – Tap out on the courts](https://www.usta.com/en/home/improve/tips-and-instruction/national/cardio-tennis-with-michael-harper-tap-out-on-the-courts.html)
- Cardio Tennis is "a high energy group fitness activity" that uses drills and games, with "great music", where participants keep heart rate in the 65–85% zone. Club programs group it by NTRP level (2.5 to 4.0+). — [search-result excerpts from club program pages, e.g. Waynesboro VA](https://www.waynesboro.va.us/241/13598)

Progressions: switch to two up/one back to encourage lobs; use doubles or singles boundaries. — [USTA Net Gen Green PDF](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)
- Steps / rules / scoring: as above.
- Coaching cues: "Close the net and angle volleys"; passers use "down-the-line, short-angle and topspin lob" (USTA).
- Progression / Regression: as in the USTA progressions above. Regression (inferred): coach feeds every ball.

## Key Question 5 (objective deliverable): Common tactical and mental problems, their causes, fixes and corrective drills

### Takeaway
Most recreational and junior problems trace to two root causes. Tactically, the player has no default pattern (crosscourt, deep, change direction only on short balls) and no positioning rules in doubles. Mentally, the player has no routine and has never practised under pressure. Each problem below maps to drills in the library above.

### Cited Findings

**P1. Going for too much / unforced errors**
- What it looks like: low-percentage winners from neutral or defensive positions; errors into the net or long early in rallies; down-the-line attempts from wide positions.
- Likely cause: overvaluing power; not knowing the crosscourt margin (net 6 in lower in the middle, 82.5 ft diagonal vs 78 ft) ([TalkingTennis](https://talkingtennis.net/blog-posts/smart-tennis-why-you-should-hit-a-lot-more-balls-cross-court)); scoring that rewards winners without punishing errors.
- Fix approach / cues: "Control is a much more important skill… than power" ([USHSTA](https://ushsta.org/?p=638)); "aggressive patience" crosscourt (TalkingTennis); require a "rally of four before you can win a point" ([LTA](https://www.lta.org.uk/play/tennis-tips-and-techniques/how-to-get-more-from-your-tennis-practices/)); make errors cost a point ([Sportplan +/-](https://www.sportplan.net/drills/Tennis/Rally-Drills/Plus-and-Minus-Rewards-TA00011.jsp)).
- Corrective drills: S1, S2, S11, M8; CoachTube "Put-Away is a No-Way", where points can be won only by opponent errors ([CoachTube](https://coachtube.com/course_lesson/best-tennis-drills-for-coaches/20-mental-strength-tennis-drills/19921068)).

**P2. No plan / hitting to the middle**
- What it looks like: every ball goes back to the center; opponents are never moved; no serve or return targets.
- Likely cause: practice limited to cooperative rallying; ball controls (direction, height, depth, speed, spin) not trained for tactical use ([USTA Net Gen](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)).
- Fix approach / cues: "Use all five controls to move opponent"; crosscourt until a short ball, then change direction ("Wait for short ball or sitter on baseline to change directions") (USTA); serve to a spot, then forehand to the open court.
- Corrective drills: S4, S5, S6, S7, S8, S13.

**P3. Losing focus after errors**
- What it looks like: errors come in clusters; the player rushes the next point; body language drops.
- Likely cause: no between-point routine; attention stays on the past error ([Physical Education Update](https://www.physicaleducationupdate.com/public/860.cfm)).
- Fix approach / cues: Loehr's four stages, with eyes on the strings "so your eyes have a place to rest" ([Tennis.com](https://www.tennis.com/news/articles/playing-with-fire-the-delicate-role-of-emotion-in-pro-and-rec-tennis)); cue words "Next Time / Rebound / Get Back".
- Corrective drills: M1, M6, M4, M5.

**P4. Tight under pressure / choking on big points**
- What it looks like: shorter swings, pushing the ball, double faults at 30-40 or deuce; going for nothing or everything.
- Likely cause: practice never includes pressure; breathing becomes shallow ("in tense situations, we sometimes forget to breathe", [Fernandez/Tennis.com](https://prod.tennis.com/news/articles/mental-tricks-to-closing-out-matches)); no performance cues.
- Fix approach / cues: create stress in practice through routines and scoring ([USTA PD](https://www.playerdevelopment.usta.com/news/habits_for_tennis_success_package_mental_skills_into_routines)); deep breathing before the stance (USTA PD cites Djokovic); cues "trust it", "go for it" (USTA PD); pressure training is effective when coach-controlled ([JYU thesis](https://jyx.jyu.fi/jyx/Record/jyx_123456789_87780/Details)).
- Corrective drills: M2, M3, M7, M1.

**P5. Poor doubles positioning (both back with a gap, one up one back with a diagonal hole, partners not moving together)**
- What it looks like: a gap opens between partners; the net player stays planted while the ball goes wide; the team doesn't move forward after short balls.
- Likely cause: the four positions have never been taught; no rule to "move with the ball"; uncertainty about when to come in.
- Fix approach / cues: "Move with ball at net, rally crosscourt from baseline" ([USTA Net Gen Yellow](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Yellow.pdf)); on a short ball, the baseliner must come in and split inside the service line (Gladiator Dubz); winning team steps up ([TeachPE Move Up](https://www.teachpe.com/?p=2163)).
- Corrective drills: D5, D6, D8, D11; video [dd7lPpZe3ig](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dd7lPpZe3ig) ("The #1 Doubles Positioning Mistake (3 Steps To Fix It)", The Tennis Mentor).

**P6. Not moving at net in doubles (the "statue" net player)**
- What it looks like: the net player never crosses; the returner rallies crosscourt freely; the net player turns to watch their partner hit.
- Likely cause: fear of being passed; not reading the opponent; poaching sideways instead of forward.
- Fix approach / cues: watch the baseline opponents, not your partner ([USHSTA Rally Poaching](https://ushsta.org/?p=558)); move "diagonally forward as serve bounces" and "poach as if… alone" on your side ([USTA](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Yellow.pdf)); finish "two steps past the center service line" and volley to the feet or the angle ([USHSTA](https://ushsta.org/?p=542)).
- Corrective drills: D1, D2, D3, D9, D4.

**P7. No between-point routine**
- What it looks like: the player walks straight to the line and plays fast after both wins and losses; inconsistent serve rhythm.
- Likely cause: routines are not taught or practised; players don't know that roughly 16 seconds can be used to recover ([TennisOne/Loehr excerpt](https://tennisone.tennisplayer.net/content/MentalToughness/Loehr/training2/train2.htm)).
- Fix approach / cues: use the four stages and personalize them, since Loehr's 16 seconds is "a starting place" (TennisOne); routines are "a series of behaviors and thoughts… to get to the next serve or return" (USTA PD).
- Corrective drills: M1, M6.

**P8. Negative self-talk**
- What it looks like: verbal self-criticism, racquet abuse, "I always miss this".
- Likely cause: anger rehearsed as habit; Guhde: "we rehearse our rage" ([Tennis.com](https://www.tennis.com/news/articles/playing-with-fire-the-delicate-role-of-emotion-in-pro-and-rec-tennis)); no replacement script.
- Fix approach / cues: a five-step cue-word method with phrases like "Nice Try" and "Rebound" ([Physical Education Update](https://www.physicaleducationupdate.com/public/860.cfm)); "positive and productive self-talk" as a core skill ([USTA PD](https://playerdevelopment.usta.com/About-USTA/Player-Development/habits_for_tennis_success_develop_the_mental_skills_foundation)); identify the real source of frustration (for example an untrusted backhand) and train it (Tennis.com).
- Corrective drills: M6, M1, M4.

**P9. Can't close out leads**
- What it looks like: broken when serving for the set; softer swings at 5-3; premature net rushes.
- Likely cause: "playing not to lose"; anxiety-driven tactical changes; low first-serve percentage under tension.
- Fix approach / cues: "Get the first one in… take a little pace off" and "stick with the tactics that helped you get where you are" ([Tennis.com 2010](https://www.tennis.com/news/articles/tip-serving-out-the-match)); 3–4 deep breaths, play to win, and reverse the score mentally ([Gigi Fernandez](https://prod.tennis.com/news/articles/mental-tricks-to-closing-out-matches)).
- Corrective drills: M9, M3 (sudden death at 40), M7; video [twMrBvmrUb0](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=twMrBvmrUb0) ("Stop choking! Simple Mental Toughness strategies to close out tennis matches", Meike Babel Tennis).

**P10. Losing to pushers / moonballers**
- What it looks like: impatience against high, slow balls; errors from overhitting.
- Likely cause: never practised against height; poor attack options against high balls.
- Fix approach / cues: practise "beat the pusher" with one player restricted to moonballs, and alternate moonball and drive points ([CoachTube](https://coachtube.com/course_lesson/best-tennis-drills-for-coaches/20-mental-strength-tennis-drills/19921068)).
- Corrective drills: S12, S2, S9 (approaching off high balls).

**P11. Poor lob defense / no lob in doubles**
- What it looks like: a two-up team gets lobbed repeatedly with no switching; a two-back team never lobs.
- Likely cause: overheads rarely practised; no switch communication.
- Fix approach / cues: lob-only constraint games and coach-lob starts ([TeachPE](https://www.teachpe.com/?p=2163); [Wikipedia "Deep Desperation"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_games)); for seniors, use the lob "to break the rhythm or catch your breath" ([Walters](https://swnewsmedia.com/lakeshore_weekly/news/opinion/columnists/commentary-tennis-tips-for-senior-players/article_1c2a8197-2623-5dd4-b459-6714ccdf3db9.html)).
- Corrective drills: D7, G6 (Triples with two up / one back).
