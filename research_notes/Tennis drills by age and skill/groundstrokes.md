# Groundstroke Drills and Faults by Age and Skill (green ball 9-11 through adult NTRP 4.5)

Research date: 2026-10-09. Scope: forehand, backhand (one- and two-handed), rally consistency, depth, direction, topspin, slice, inside-out forehand, defense; plus a fault catalogue with corrective drills. Other researchers cover serve/volley/footwork/etc.

## What drills do top coaching sources recommend for rally consistency, depth and direction change? (Drill library)

### Takeaway
Governing-body curricula (USTA Net Generation Green Ball plans, USTA Tennis 101 for Adults, Tennis Australia "Tennis Time" coaching manual, LTA) and coach drill libraries (CoachTube "Best Tennis Drills for Coaches", Sportplan, Tennis Companion) converge on the same toolkit. For consistency they use cooperative "count" rallies that start short and move back ("Rally Now", "Ten to Move On", Tempo Drill). For depth they use games with a deep target zone between service line and baseline ("Up and Down the River", "Height for Depth"). For direction they use fixed crosscourt/down-the-line patterns ("Figure 8", "Zig-Zagging", "Six by Six", "Alley Rally"). These progress into closed coach-fed patterns (Spanish/2-ball feeds) and then live-ball, scored half-court points. The 37 drills below give each one's setup precisely enough to diagram.

#### D1. Simple Forehands & Backhands (stationary hand-feed)
- **Ages / stage:** green 9-11; adult beginner. The source targets "beginners & kids".
- **Level:** beginner
- **Skill area:** forehand, backhand
- **Works on / fixes:** grip, basic swing shape, follow-through; first contact for brand-new players
- **Players & equipment:** 1 hitter + 1 feeder; basket of balls. [A] Use green ball for juniors and orange/green ball for adult beginners, matching the USTA adult plan.
- **Setup:** The hitter stands at the centre of the service line, where the centre line meets the SL. The feeder stands 4-6 ft to the side of the hitter on the same side of the net, holding the basket. The feeder demonstrates the grip and the stroke first.
- **Steps:** (1) The feeder tosses so the ball bounces waist-high in front of the hitter. (2) The hitter plays a forehand (or backhand) over the net, focusing on the follow-through. (3) Repeat on the other side. (4) Score = successful hits over the net.
- **Coaching cues:** "Start racquet at waist height"; "strong follow-through" (Tennis Companion). "Shake hands with the racquet" (Eastern grip); grip pressure "about a 3 out of 10" ([USTA forehand basics](https://www.usta.com/en/home/improve/tips-and-instruction/national/learning-the-basics--forehand.html)).
- **Progression:** D2 (feed from across the net), then D3 (side to side).
- **Regression:** start with the racquet already at the contact point, out in front, then add the finish. This is the USTA adult forehand demo sequence: "racquet in front, starting at contact point", then "racquet to side, extend to finish" ([USTA Tennis 101 for Adults](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/USTA-Tennis-101-for-Adults.pdf)).
- **Source:** [Tennis Companion – 12 Essential Tennis Drills for Beginners & Kids](https://tenniscompanion.org/12-essential-tennis-drills-for-beginners-and-kids/)
- **Video (related):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FD7kpvPTj3M — "26 Tennis Forehand Drills - Hand & Racket Feeding To Improve Technique" — Martin Kares Tennistraining

#### D2. Ball-Toss Forehands & Backhands (moving ball, fed from across the net)
- **Ages / stage:** green 9-11; adult beginner
- **Level:** beginner
- **Skill area:** forehand, backhand
- **Works on / fixes:** judging ball speed and depth; adjusting feet before the swing (helps late contact and crowding)
- **Players & equipment:** 1 hitter + 1 feeder; basket
- **Setup:** The hitter is in ready position at the centre of the SL. The feeder is on the opposite side at the centre service line, 2-3 ft from the net, with the basket.
- **Steps:** (1) The feeder gently tosses so the ball bounces about waist-high to the hitter. (2) The hitter judges speed, adjusts footwork, and hits the forehand or backhand. (3) Alternate sides after each basket.
- **Coaching cues:** "Judge the bounce, move your feet"; "turn sideways early" [A]
- **Progression:** D3 (alternate sides, unannounced)
- **Regression:** D1
- **Source:** [Tennis Companion](https://tenniscompanion.org/12-essential-tennis-drills-for-beginners-and-kids/)
- **Video (related):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hAfiW1YHyHY — "The Top Tennis Drills For Beginners - Learn To Rally From Day One" — Patrick Smith Tennis Coaching

#### D3. Side-to-Side Forehands and Backhands
- **Ages / stage:** green 9-11; yellow 11-14; adult beginner-intermediate
- **Level:** beginner to intermediate. The source calls it intermediate: it "tests anticipation and grip switching".
- **Skill area:** forehand, backhand, movement
- **Works on / fixes:** grip change between wings; lateral movement; flat feet; crowding
- **Players & equipment:** 1 hitter + 1 feeder; basket
- **Setup:** Same as D2: hitter at the centre of the SL, feeder across the net at the centre service line, 2-3 ft from the net.
- **Steps:** (1) The feeder alternates tosses to the forehand and backhand. (2) The hitter switches grip, moves, hits, and recovers to the centre. (3) Advanced: the feeder does not say which side is coming.
- **Coaching cues:** "Keep racquet head high" in the ready position (source)
- **Progression:** unannounced random feeds; [A] racquet feeds from the baseline
- **Regression:** D2 (one side per basket)
- **Source:** [Tennis Companion](https://tenniscompanion.org/12-essential-tennis-drills-for-beginners-and-kids/)
- **Video:** none verified for this exact drill (see D1 video for feeding variations)

#### D4. Forward and Backward Forehands & Backhands
- **Ages / stage:** green 9-11; adult beginner
- **Level:** beginner to intermediate
- **Skill area:** forehand, backhand, depth judgement
- **Works on / fixes:** judging depth and speed; dynamic footwork; hitting off the back foot or reaching for short balls
- **Players & equipment:** 1 hitter + 1 feeder; basket
- **Setup:** The hitter is at the centre of the SL. The feeder is on the opposite side at the centre service line, 2-3 ft from the net.
- **Steps:** (1) A moderate-depth toss that needs no movement. (2) A shorter toss, so the hitter moves forward. (3) A longer toss, so the hitter moves back. (4) The hitter returns to the SL between shots. (5) Advanced: don't announce short, neutral or long.
- **Coaching cues:** "Position yourself to catch the ball at about waist height" (source)
- **Progression:** unannounced depth; [A] racquet-feed from the opposite baseline with varied spin
- **Regression:** announce depth before each feed
- **Source:** [Tennis Companion](https://tenniscompanion.org/12-essential-tennis-drills-for-beginners-and-kids/)
- **Video:** none verified for this exact drill

#### D5. Hit and Catch
- **Ages / stage:** green 9-11 (Sportplan lists it for orange/green ball); adult beginner [A]
- **Level:** beginner
- **Skill area:** forehand, backhand, direction
- **Works on / fixes:** directing the ball to a person; control over power
- **Players & equipment:** 2 players; 1 cone used as a "catching cup"; basket
- **Setup:** The player is in ready position at the centre of the SL. The feeder/catcher stands on the opposite side on the centre line, halfway between the net and the SL, holding an upturned cone.
- **Steps:** (1) The feeder tosses to the player's forehand. (2) The player hits straight back to the feeder. (3) The feeder catches it in the cone, in the air or off the bounce. (4) Repeat on the backhand, then switch roles. (5) Score = successful hit-and-catches.
- **Coaching cues:** "Direct the shot to your partner"; "small swing, short distance" (USTA adult cue, cross-applied [A])
- **Progression:** alternate forehand and backhand without announcing it (source)
- **Regression:** catcher catches with hands [A]
- **Source:** [Tennis Companion](https://tenniscompanion.org/12-essential-tennis-drills-for-beginners-and-kids/); [Sportplan "Hit & Catch (GS)" orange/green](https://www.sportplan.net/sketches/Tennis/openSketch/10225903)
- **Video:** none verified

#### D6. Rally Now! / Build Up (step-back cooperative rally — the USTA adult beginner core)
- **Ages / stage:** adult beginner (the source is the USTA adult plan); green 9-11 [A]; senior beginner [A]
- **Level:** beginner
- **Skill area:** rally consistency, forehand, backhand
- **Works on / fixes:** first sustained rallies; matching swing size to distance
- **Players & equipment:** pairs. Red or foam ball on a 36-ft or 42-ft court, then orange ball on 60 ft, then green ball on 78 ft. Each player has a target in front of them, and boundaries are marked with chalk or TDLs.
- **Setup:** Partners face each other on opposite sides of the net, about 10 ft from the net, each with a target in front. In "Build Up", partners start in the middle of the service box on a half-court.
- **Steps:**
  - **Rally Now:** cooperative forehand/backhand rally aimed at the targets. After a rally of six shots, both players take one big step back, until they reach the SL.
  - **Build Up:** the coach sets a rally goal (for example 4). Once reached, raise the goal by 2.
  - **Later class:** after six shots with red balls, step back to the 60-ft baseline, switch to the orange ball, and aim to bounce the ball between the SL and the 60-ft baseline. Then use the green ball from the 78-ft baseline.
- **Coaching cues:** "Small swing, short distance. Distance affects technique."; "Small swing, short distance; bigger swing, greater distance."; "Ready position and react." (source)
- **Progression:** Level 2 crosscourt; Build Up levels go forehand only, then backhand only, then move to the SL. Score a point each time the ball bounces between the SL and baseline, first to 10.
- **Regression:** "Instant Rally": tap the ball up eye-high off the strings, palm up, then knuckles up. Then self-rally on a target, partner rally on a target, and partner rally over a line.
- **Source:** [USTA Tennis 101 for Adults](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/USTA-Tennis-101-for-Adults.pdf)
- **Video (related):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YQcKqXI2Jcw — "Beginner Tennis: Learn to Rally Fast!" — Coach Josh Morain

#### D7. Ten to Move On (mini-tennis to baseline)
- **Ages / stage:** yellow 11-14; teens; adult intermediate. The source says intermediate-advanced. [A] Use 5 balls per stage for green ball or adult beginners.
- **Level:** intermediate to advanced
- **Skill area:** rally consistency, depth control
- **Works on / fixes:** consistency under a target; scaling swing length as distance grows
- **Players & equipment:** 4 players (2 pairs) on one court; 1 ball per pair
- **Setup:** Each pair faces each other across the net. Everyone starts on the service line opposite their partner, one pair per half-court.
- **Steps:**
  1. Rally 10 balls kept only inside the opposite service box.
  2. Move to halfway between the SL and baseline and rally 10 balls anywhere in the half-court.
  3. Move to the baseline and rally 10.
  4. Move back to the halfway position and rally 10.
  5. Finish at the SL with 10.
  - The first pair to complete the whole sequence wins.
- **Coaching cues:** "Same rhythm, longer swing as you move back" [A]
- **Progression:** forehands only or backhands only; crosscourt only [A]
- **Regression:** 5-ball stages; let the ball bounce twice [A]
- **Source:** [CoachTube – Ten to Move On](https://coachtube.com/course_lesson/best-tennis-drills-for-coaches/ten-to-move-on-groundstroke-consistency-accuracy/19914768)
- **Video (related):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=526H8zXwp2w — "How To Develop Consistent Rally Skills At Any Level Even As A Beginner #tennis" — Patrick Smith Tennis Coaching

#### D8. Mini-Tennis Crosscourt (green warm-up)
- **Ages / stage:** green 9-11; yellow 11-14; all adults as a warm-up [A]
- **Level:** beginner to intermediate
- **Skill area:** direction, forehand/backhand, rally consistency
- **Works on / fixes:** crosscourt direction; using width
- **Players & equipment:** 2 pairs per court; green balls
- **Setup:** Each pair uses a half-court. All players stand on the service line and hit into the service box plus alley.
- **Steps:** (1) Pairs rally crosscourt with the outside stroke only (the forehand-to-forehand diagonal for two right-handers). (2) Switch sides after 2 minutes.
- **Coaching cues:** "Take advantage of extra width" (goal)
- **Progression:** (1) rally into the corners of the service box; (2) rally with more angle into the alleys. "Mini-Tennis Angles" variant: TDLs split the service box in half horizontally; alternate short sharp angles with deep balls between the TDL and the SL; play points with a bonus for the alley.
- **Regression:** straight-ahead mini-tennis [A]
- **Source:** [USTA Net Generation Green Ball plans, Green 2 ch. 1-2](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uuKtDEXCVnA — "Green ball players drills and exercises: MINI-TENNIS VARIATIONS" — Fitness Instructional Tenis

#### D9. Tempo Drill (one-minute rally count)
- **Ages / stage:** green 9-11 (source); yellow; adult beginner-intermediate [A]
- **Level:** beginner to intermediate
- **Skill area:** rally consistency, topspin
- **Works on / fixes:** consistency with spin and speed; a measurable baseline
- **Players & equipment:** 2-3 pairs per court; 1 ball per pair; timer
- **Setup:** Every player on the baseline, with pairs straight across from each other.
- **Steps:** (1) Count how many balls the pair can rally in 1 minute with one ball. (2) After a miss, restart at the same number (Green 2 version). (3) Repeat and try to beat the record.
- **Coaching cues:** "Hit with spin and speed to increase numbers". The USTA coaching tip says depth is key on the green court. Encourage players to use the lower body to start and then extend the hands all the way through. Players who just hit high, loopy topspin to keep the ball deep will struggle as the game gets more advanced.
- **Progression:** straight ahead, then crosscourt with recovery; straight ahead with one stroke; crosscourt with the outside stroke
- **Regression:** start from the SL [A]
- **Source:** [USTA Green Ball plans, Green 2 ch. 3 & Green 1 ch. 1](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)
- **Video (related):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ScaOTd2t56o — "7-6-5-4-3-2-1-Go! Tennis Coaching Drill: Rally Consistency with Students" — Cosmin | WebTennis24

#### D10. Alley Rally
- **Ages / stage:** green 9-11 (source); yellow; teens; adult intermediate [A]
- **Level:** intermediate
- **Skill area:** direction, rally consistency, topspin/slice
- **Works on / fixes:** straight-line direction control; extension through the ball
- **Players & equipment:** 2 pairs per court; 1 ball per pair
- **Setup:** All players on the baseline. Each pair stands in, and hits into, its own doubles alley (one pair per alley).
- **Steps:** (1) Rally back and forth inside the doubles alley. (2) Recover outside the alley after each shot. (3) Rally for numbers; a miss resets to zero.
- **Coaching cues:** "Stay consistent, hitting with square stance and good extension" (goal)
- **Progression:** one stroke only, then alternate strokes; topspin only, then slice only
- **Regression:** start from the SL; count misses-allowed [A]
- **Source:** [USTA Green Ball plans, Green 2 ch. 2-3](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)
- **Video (related):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ixAA_8GikaY — "How to Rally & Hit with Direction | USTA Coaching" — United States Tennis Association (USTA)

#### D11. Up and Down the River (depth game)
- **Ages / stage:** green 9-11 (source); yellow; adult beginner-intermediate leagues [A]
- **Level:** beginner to intermediate
- **Skill area:** depth, rally consistency
- **Works on / fixes:** keeping the ball deep with height, spin and speed
- **Players & equipment:** 2 pairs per court; TDLs
- **Setup:** Each pair uses a half-court. Boundaries run from the middle of the court to the doubles sideline, so alleys are in. TDLs split the court from the service line to the baseline. [A] This means extending the centre line through the backcourt so each half has its own deep zone.
- **Steps:** (1) Start points after a serve and return. (2) Both players must let the ball bounce. (3) Play to 4 points, then volley cooperatively until the last court finishes. (4) The winner moves up a court and the loser moves down.
- **Coaching cues:** "Use height, spin, and speed to keep ball deep" (goal)
- **Progression:** start points straight ahead with one stroke; play crosscourt with the outside stroke only. "Depth Games" variant: every ball must bounce between the SL and baseline.
- **Regression:** cooperative count of balls landing past the SL before playing points [A]
- **Source:** [USTA Green Ball plans, Green 2 ch. 1](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)
- **Video (related):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DVRyIjzJ3kI — "Complete Green Ball Class: Rally, Volley, & Point Play" — New Rochelle Racquet Club

#### D12. Height for Depth
- **Ages / stage:** yellow 11-14; teens; adult intermediate. The source does not give a level; it appears intermediate.
- **Level:** intermediate
- **Skill area:** depth, topspin, rally consistency
- **Works on / fixes:** netting; short balls; low net clearance
- **Players & equipment:** pairs; spare racquets
- **Setup:** Pairs on opposite baselines rally up and down the middle of the court. Racquets are placed in the net to force a higher trajectory. [A] Stand them along the net tape so the effective net height rises.
- **Steps:** (1) Cooperative rally for the longest possible exchange. (2) Every shot must land beyond the service box. (3) The rally restarts if the ball lands long, wide, short, or in the net. (4) Pairs compete for the longest rally in a set time.
- **Coaching cues:** keep proper striking technique and don't just push for consistency ("players can sometimes start to push the ball too much"); "hit through the ball"
- **Progression:** a deeper zone, for example 3/4 court [A]; crosscourt only [A]
- **Regression:** remove the racquets; count balls past the SL only [A]
- **Source:** [Sportplan – Height for Depth (TA00050)](https://www.sportplan.net/drills/Tennis/Rally-Drills/Height-for-Depth-TA00050.jsp)
- **Video (related):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n9tDwKz1x64 — "Tennis Drill: More Power And Depth On Your Forehand" — Online Tennis Instruction

#### D13. Net-Clearance Crosscourt
- **Ages / stage:** teens; adult intermediate-advanced [A]
- **Level:** intermediate
- **Skill area:** depth, topspin, forehand, rally consistency
- **Works on / fixes:** abbreviated finish / decelerating; netted balls
- **Players & equipment:** 2 players; [A] a rope or string above the net, or just a visual target
- **Setup:** [A] Two players on opposite baselines on the deuce diagonal, rallying forehands crosscourt.
- **Steps:** Rally crosscourt forehands with a height goal of 3-4 ft over the net (PlayYourCourt). The LTA similarly recommends rallies aimed at targets a few feet above the net cord, to avoid netting and gain depth.
- **Coaching cues:** "Finish over the non-dominant shoulder"; "swing as if hitting three stacked balls" (PlayYourCourt)
- **Progression:** add a deep target zone past the SL [A]; backhand diagonal
- **Regression:** start at the SL with half swings [A]
- **Source:** [PlayYourCourt – Why your forehand has no power (2026)](https://www.playyourcourt.com/news/why-your-forehand-has-no-power-and-how-to-fix-it-for-good/); [LTA – How to play better tennis rallies](https://www.lta.org.uk/play/tennis-tips-and-techniques/how-to-play-better-tennis-rallies/) (from search summary, not fetched)
- **Video (related):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zEz9vrm3QYw — "My Top 3 Drills For Rally Quality - Classroom Tennis Lesson (Ep2)" — The Tennis Mentor

#### D14. Call the Bounce
- **Ages / stage:** green 9-11 (source); adult beginner [A]
- **Level:** beginner to intermediate
- **Skill area:** rally consistency, depth reading, movement
- **Works on / fixes:** flat feet; late reading of depth; hitting off the back foot
- **Players & equipment:** 2-3 pairs per court
- **Setup:** Pairs use a half-court, with all players spread along the baseline.
- **Steps:** (1) Rally straight ahead. (2) As the ball comes back, call "up", "back" or "stay", then move to that spot to return it. (3) Follow a really short "up" ball to the net, finish the rally, then move back.
- **Coaching cues:** "Call location and move to ball as it crosses net" (goal)
- **Progression:** rally crosscourt; any stroke, then one stroke, then alternate strokes
- **Regression:** coach calls the depth [A]
- **Source:** [USTA Green Ball plans, Green 2 ch. 1](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)
- **Video (related, split-step timing):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XR83yXLla8w — "Tennis Split Step Timing, Execution And Drills" — Feel Tennis Instruction

#### D15. X Drill (deep roll + short angle)
- **Ages / stage:** green 9-11 (source); yellow 11-14; teens [A]
- **Level:** intermediate
- **Skill area:** topspin, depth, defense, movement
- **Works on / fixes:** moving diagonally up and back; heavy topspin from deep; attacking a short ball
- **Players & equipment:** groups of 3 (feeder + 2 hitters). Green 1 version: pairs. Targets: cones or spots.
- **Setup:** One hitter stands at the centre of the baseline, with the other waiting behind at a safe distance. The feeder stands mid-court, between the baseline and SL, on the same side as the hitters, with balls. Targets go in the deep and short corners on the other side. Green 1 version: hitters stand mid-baseline of a half-court and the tosser stands in the service box on the same side.
- **Steps:** (1) The feeder tosses high and deep to one side. The hitter backs up, returns high and deep crosscourt with heavy topspin, and recovers to the middle. (2) The second feed is high and short to the opposite side. The hitter plays a short angle (Green 1: an aggressive ball to the corner) and recovers. (3) Repeat to the opposite sides. (4) Do 2 sequences (Green 1: 4), then switch hitters and later the feeder.
- **Coaching cues:** "Move in diagonals up and back using appropriate stances"; "drop-step and cross moving to deep ball, increased racquet speed on high loop" (goals)
- **Progression:** hand-feed, then racquet-feed from the other side of the net, then the coach feeds faster; short angle or rip to the corner; play competitively as teams
- **Regression:** feed to one wing only, at walking pace [A]
- **Source:** [USTA Green Ball plans, Green 2 ch. 1 & Green 1 ch. 1](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)
- **Video:** none verified for this drill

#### D16. Crazy Feed (heavy-topspin attack game)
- **Ages / stage:** green 9-11 (source); yellow 11-14 [A]
- **Level:** intermediate
- **Skill area:** topspin, depth, defense
- **Works on / fixes:** high, heavy, deep topspin to the weaker wing; balls that go long because they are hit flat
- **Players & equipment:** teams of 3-4; full court
- **Setup:** Teams on opposite baselines; 2 players play at a time.
- **Steps:** (1) The attacker self-feeds a deep ball from behind the baseline, hitting high and deep to the opponent's weakness. (2) Play out the point. The attacker can't come to the net off the feed. (3) Rotate every point. (4) Attackers score 1 for a won point and defenders score 2. First to 7, then change opponents.
- **Coaching cues:** "Take advantage of increased length and width by rolling balls deep to corners" (goal)
- **Progression:** self-feed forehands, then backhands. The defender feeds a high, deep ball with little spin, and the attacker hits back with heavy topspin.
- **Regression:** cooperative "roll it deep" count [A]
- **Source:** [USTA Green Ball plans, Green 2 ch. 1](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)
- **Video:** none verified

#### D17. Figure 8 (cooperative change of direction)
- **Ages / stage:** green 9-11 (USTA Green 1 warm-up); yellow; teens; adult intermediate-advanced (Tennis Australia uses a fed "Figure 8" for squads)
- **Level:** intermediate
- **Skill area:** direction (crosscourt / down the line), rally consistency
- **Works on / fixes:** controlled change of direction; footwork between wings
- **Players & equipment:** 2 pairs per court (USTA). TA version: coach-fed.
- **Setup:** Each pair uses a half-court (USTA warm-up). One player hits only down the line and the other only crosscourt, so the ball traces a figure 8. Start from the SL, then move to the baseline.
- **Steps:** (1) Rally cooperatively, with one side hitting down the line and the other crosscourt. (2) Move the partner a few feet right and left using the outside stroke. (3) After each side has hit both directions, repeat from the baseline. TA lists "Figure 8 (one hit, focus on footwork)" and "Figure 8 (two hits, focus on footwork)" among its 2-ball coach-fed drills.
- **Coaching cues:** "Find a rally pace you can control with appropriate speed and spin" (USTA goal)
- **Progression:** a set number of shots before switching; slice and topspin from the SL; one side hits all forehands
- **Regression:** from the SL only, with a set number of shots [A]
- **Source:** [USTA Green Ball plans, Green 1 ch. 1](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf); [Tennis Australia – Tennis Time Coaching Manual (Geoff Quinlan), "Drills and Activities"](https://www.tennis.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2010/08/Drills-and-Activities.pdf)

#### D18. Centre Third, Outside Third

#### D29. Slice Backhand Progression Ladder
- **Ages / stage:** yellow 11-14; teens; adult intermediate-advanced; senior [A]. The source says "all levels". USTA green plans already have 9-11s play slice points with a continental grip and one-handed backhands.
- **Level:** beginner to advanced (staged)
- **Skill area:** slice
- **Works on / fixes:** floating slice; no backspin; wrist flick
- **Players & equipment:** coach/partner; basket; target zones

## What are the common forehand/backhand faults, their causes, fix cues and corrective drills? (Fault catalogue)

### Takeaway
The faults that recur across sources come from preparation and body use, not the arm alone. They are: arming / no unit turn, late contact, poor spacing, back-foot hitting, and deceleration. Two-handed backhands mostly fail through dominant-arm takeover; one-handers mostly fail through an elbow lead. Most fixes are simple constraint drills: catch-then-hit, freeze finishes, non-dominant-hand-only swings, the OK-sign grip, net-clearance targets, and depth zones.

### Cited Findings

#### F1. Arming the ball / no unit turn
- **What it looks like / ball result:** The body stays square to the net and the arm generates the stroke; shoulders barely rotate; legs and hips are static. The ball "floats back like a beach ball", with little pace or penetration and inconsistent direction. — [PlayYourCourt 2026](https://www.playyourcourt.com/news/why-your-forehand-has-no-power-and-how-to-fix-it-for-good/)
- **Likely cause:** Skipping the body's biggest force producers. Late or no coil, where the player only "moves the racquet back". — [PlayYourCourt 2026](https://www.playyourcourt.com/news/why-your-forehand-has-no-power-and-how-to-fix-it-for-good/); [USTA, Gladstone 2018](https://www.usta.com/en/home/improve/tips-and-instruction/national/improve-your-tennis-game--get-your-forehand-flowing.html)
- **Fix cues:**
  - "Unit turn": hips and upper body rotate as one unit. — [USTA](https://www.usta.com/en/home/improve/tips-and-instruction/national/improve-your-tennis-game--get-your-forehand-flowing.html)
  - Pivot the outside foot on the split step; the non-dominant hand points at the incoming ball; strings face the side fence; "arm is the messenger, not the engine". — [PlayYourCourt 2026](https://www.playyourcourt.com/news/why-your-forehand-has-no-power-and-how-to-fix-it-for-good/)
  - Start the turn before or as the ball bounces. — [PlayYourCourt (Baxter 2019)](https://www.playyourcourt.com/news/forehand-killer-part-2/)
- **Corrective drills:** D33 Unit-Turn Catch then Hit; shadow swings (D33 regression); D2
- **Videos:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MWVBsnwv7dM — "How to Fix the Most Common Forehand Power Mistake (Step-by-Step)" — Essential Tennis - Lessons and Instruction for Passionate Players; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=du5uOZfKArw — "Why The Split Step & Unit Turn Are CRUCIAL For Your Tennis Game" — Gaby Paz - Vantage Tennis

#### F2. Late contact
- **What it looks like / ball result:** The ball is struck beside or behind the body rather than in front. The result is a loss of control and power. — [Tennis Uni](https://tennis-uni.com/en/tennis-forehand/); [PlayYourCourt 2026](https://www.playyourcourt.com/news/why-your-forehand-has-no-power-and-how-to-fix-it-for-good/)
- **Likely cause:** Hitting too close to the body and late preparation (Tennis Uni). Rushed "spastic late motion" with no separated takeback (Baxter). On one-handers, an elbow lead (see F9).
- **Fix cues:**
  - "Strike ball further forward"; contact in front with the racket face parallel to the net. — [Tennis Uni](https://tennis-uni.com/en/tennis-forehand/)
  - Ball "a little bit in front of you" (backhand). — [USTA basics, Pettit 2017](https://www.usta.com/en/home/improve/tips-and-instruction/national/learning-the-basics--backhand.html)
  - Prepare before the bounce. — [PlayYourCourt 2019](https://www.playyourcourt.com/news/forehand-killer-part-2/)
- **Corrective drills:** D33, D34, D2 / D3, D4
- **Videos:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NsRlIN9See4 — "LATE Forehand Fix - tennis lesson" — Essential Tennis - Lessons and Instruction for Passionate Players; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c5SqRb9i020 — "Forehand Contact Point Correction | 3.5 NTRP Tennis Lesson" — Intuitive Tennis

#### F3. Hitting off the back foot / no weight transfer
- **What it looks like / ball result:** Flat feet, standing tall, no load on the outside leg, weight staying centred or falling backward. The ball lands short with weak pace. — [PlayYourCourt 2026](https://www.playyourcourt.com/news/why-your-forehand-has-no-power-and-how-to-fix-it-for-good/)
- **Likely cause:** Inadequate weight transfer. — [Tennis Uni](https://tennis-uni.com/en/tennis-forehand/). Poor depth reading of the incoming ball [A].
- **Fix cues:** "Shift weight to front foot during forward swing" (Tennis Uni). Knees bent with weight on the balls of the feet, load the outside leg during the turn, "push the ground away with your back foot", and use a semi-open or open stance (PlayYourCourt 2026).
- **Corrective drills:** D4 Forward and Backward; D14 Call the Bounce; D34 Drop-Hit Freeze-Finish
- **Video:** none verified that is specific to this fault. See the general https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BuBaV8zjo78 — "7 Tennis Forehand Mistakes EVERY Player Makes (And How to Fix Them)" — Patrik Broddfelt (content not confirmed).

#### F4. Wristy flip (and its opposite, the locked death-grip)
- **What it looks like / ball result:** A wrist snap or flick at contact gives unpredictable results. On slice, a loose wrist gives a floater that invites attack. — [Sportplan Apr 2026](https://www.sportplan.net/drills/Tennis/trends/slice-backhand-tactical-weapon-2025-04.jsp). The opposite fault is a death grip with a rigid wrist, which slows racquet speed and loses the whip. — [PlayYourCourt 2026](https://www.playyourcourt.com/news/why-your-forehand-has-no-power-and-how-to-fix-it-for-good/)
- **Likely cause:** Independent hand action replacing body rotation (flip); excess tension (lock).
- **Fix cues:**
  - Shadow swings holding the racquet throat with the non-hitting hand until the forward swing begins, to reinforce a one-unit turn; firm wrist through slice contact. — [Sportplan](https://www.sportplan.net/drills/Tennis/trends/slice-backhand-tactical-weapon-2025-04.jsp)
  - Grip pressure "about a 3" out of 10. — [USTA basics](https://www.usta.com/en/home/improve/tips-and-instruction/national/learning-the-basics--backhand.html)
  - Relaxed grip with controlled lag. — [PlayYourCourt 2026](https://www.playyourcourt.com/news/why-your-forehand-has-no-power-and-how-to-fix-it-for-good/)
- **Corrective drills:** D33 (non-hitting hand on the throat during the turn), D29 (slice firm wrist), D34
- **Video (related):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8kDLHFtVT5c — "5 Drills To Improve Your Forehand FAST I TENNIS LESSON" — Tennis Evolution. Its description mentions wrist control.

#### F5. No follow-through / abbreviated finish
- **What it looks like / ball result:** A sudden stop at contact; a "jab"; jerky deceleration; leaning away. Energy doesn't transfer, so the ball is weak and short. — [PlayYourCourt 2026](https://www.playyourcourt.com/news/why-your-forehand-has-no-power-and-how-to-fix-it-for-good/)
- **Likely cause:** Braking the racquet before or right after impact, often from fear of hitting long [A].
- **Fix cues:**
  - Finish over the non-dominant shoulder; "swing as if hitting three stacked balls". — [PlayYourCourt 2026](https://www.playyourcourt.com/news/why-your-forehand-has-no-power-and-how-to-fix-it-for-good/)
  - Follow-through over the left shoulder with the upper body rotating (right-hander). — [Tennis Uni](https://tennis-uni.com/en/tennis-forehand/)
  - Two-hander: finish with the non-dominant elbow pointing across the net and "airing the armpits". — [USTA](https://www.usta.com/en/home/improve/tips-and-instruction/national/improve-your-tennis-game--the-driving-force-in-your-two-handed-b.html)
  - "Strong follow-through". — [Tennis Companion](https://tenniscompanion.org/12-essential-tennis-drills-for-beginners-and-kids/)
- **Corrective drills:** D13 Net-Clearance Crosscourt; D34 freeze-finish; D1
- **Video (related):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BuBaV8zjo78 — "7 Tennis Forehand Mistakes EVERY Player Makes (And How to Fix Them)" — Patrik Broddfelt

#### F6. Ball goes long (missing low-to-high / too much pace for level)
- **What it looks like / ball result:** Balls sail past the baseline. — [USTA forehand basics](https://www.usta.com/en/home/improve/tips-and-instruction/national/learning-the-basics--forehand.html), from search summary: "The harder you swing, the farther the ball is going to go."
- **Likely cause:** A flat swing path without topspin brush, or swing speed beyond the player's control. USTA says beginners' "number 1 priority" is keeping the ball on court. — [USTA forehand basics](https://www.usta.com/en/home/improve/tips-and-instruction/national/learning-the-basics--forehand.html)
- **Fix cues:**
  - Brush low to high, "6 o'clock to 12 o'clock". — [USTA backhand basics](https://www.usta.com/en/home/improve/tips-and-instruction/national/learning-the-basics--backhand.html)
  - "Small swing, short distance; bigger swing, greater distance". — [USTA Tennis 101 for Adults](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/USTA-Tennis-101-for-Adults.pdf)
  - "Use height, spin, and speed to keep ball deep". — [USTA Green plans](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)
- **Corrective drills:** D9 Tempo (spin for consistency), D11 Up and Down the River, D12 Height for Depth, D16 Crazy Feed (heavy topspin)
- **Video:** none verified that is specific to topspin-for-control

#### F7. Poor spacing / crowding the ball
- **What it looks like / ball result:** Crowded position; contact level with or behind the hips; no room to extend. The contact is off-centre and the shot feels like it has "no power". — [PlayYourCourt 2026](https://www.playyourcourt.com/news/why-your-forehand-has-no-power-and-how-to-fix-it-for-good/)
- **Likely cause:** Incorrect footwork or positioning, with the feet adjusted too late. — [Tennis Uni](https://tennis-uni.com/en/tennis-forehand/)
- **Fix cues:** Contact slightly in front of the front hip at waist-to-chest height, with a comfortably extended arm and about 12-18 in of space (PlayYourCourt 2026). "Adjust feet early" (Tennis Uni).
- **Corrective drills:** D34 Drop-Hit Freeze; D3 Side-to-Side; D4 Forward/Backward
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8kDLHFtVT5c — "5 Drills To Improve Your Forehand FAST I TENNIS LESSON" — Tennis Evolution. Its search description mentions "proper spacing, extension".

#### F8. Two-handed backhand: dominant-arm takeover
- **What it looks like / ball result:** For right-handers, the stronger right arm dominates, so the player "pull[s] the racquet instead of driving it" and the shot is weakened. — [Tennis.com 2010](https://tennis.com/news/articles/tip-remember-the-other-hand)
- **Likely cause:** The bottom (dominant) hand powers the swing and the top hand is passive.
- **Fix cues:** "A left-handed forehand with the right hand along for the ride" ([Tennis.com](https://tennis.com/news/articles/tip-remember-the-other-hand)). Top hand = "driver", bottom hand = "passenger"; finish with the non-dominant elbow pointing across the net ([USTA, Davies 2018](https://www.usta.com/en/home/improve/tips-and-instruction/national/improve-your-tennis-game--the-driving-force-in-your-two-handed-b.html)).
- **Corrective drills:** D31 non-dominant-hand forehands; D32 OK-sign grip
- **Videos:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xn-Bdd74F5c — "Top 3 Drills For Two Handed Backhand In Tennis - Transform Your Tennis Backhand" — Top Tennis Training - Pro Tennis Lessons; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M0aIRntFvcE — "How to immediately improve your two handed tennis backhand | + Drills!" — Meike Babel Tennis

#### F9. One-handed backhand: elbow leading
- **What it looks like / ball result:** The arm and elbow move ahead of the racquet. A bent elbow needs time to straighten; if it doesn't, contact is late and control is lost. — [Tennisplayer forum: Elbow lead on 1HB](https://forum.tennisplayer.net/forum/tennisplayer/1046-elbow-lead-on-1hb); [TennisOne](https://tennisone.tennisplayer.net/content/Theories/theories.Yandell.p.5.php). These come from search summaries, and the forum is a lower-tier source.
- **Likely cause:** Not turning, or turning very late. The shoulders should reach 90 degrees to the net before the arm moves.
- **Fix cues:** A straight arm at the bottom of the backswing before the ball bounces; a straight arm and locked wrist at contact; minimal elbow bend on the takeback; the upper arm close to the chest at the start of the forward swing. — [TennisOne – Murphy](https://tennisone.tennisplayer.net/content/Lessons/murphy/back/prepback2.php)
- **Corrective drills:** D35 One-Hander Straight-Arm Feed; D33-style catch turn on the backhand side [A]
- **Videos:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nuRh27czEy8 — "3 DEADLIEST ONE-HANDED Backhand Mistakes (And How to Fix Them!)" — VS Tennis; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bdU0SFj7ToE — "You NEED to FIX Your One-Handed Backhand! Drills for Power and Control + PRO Tips and Technique!" — Chris Lewit; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zniT2qyiN4Q — "Do this One Handed Backhand drill to Fix your Rotation." — Total Tennis Domination

#### F10. Wrong grip for the intended spin
- **What it looks like / ball result:**
  - Eastern forehand: hard to handle very high balls. — [PlayYourCourt – Eastern vs Western](https://www.playyourcourt.com/news/eastern-vs-western-grip-tennis/?amp=1)
  - Eastern gives flat, hard trajectories; Western gives topspin but less power and is difficult to execute. — [Tennis Uni](https://tennis-uni.com/en/tennis-forehand/)
  - On slice, an Eastern backhand grip "produces flat, floating results". — [Sportplan 2026](https://www.sportplan.net/drills/Tennis/trends/slice-backhand-tactical-weapon-2025-04.jsp)
- **Likely cause:** The grip doesn't match the spin or contact height the player needs.
- **Fix cues:** Semi-western for most juniors, with Eastern as a stepping stone ([PlayYourCourt](https://www.playyourcourt.com/news/eastern-vs-western-grip-tennis/?amp=1)). Eastern "shake hands" grip for adult beginners ([USTA basics](https://www.usta.com/en/home/improve/tips-and-instruction/national/learning-the-basics--forehand.html)). Continental for slice ([Sportplan](https://www.sportplan.net/drills/Tennis/trends/slice-backhand-tactical-weapon-2025-04.jsp)).
- **Corrective drills:** D16 Crazy Feed and D9 Tempo (topspin), D29 (slice grip)
- **Video:** none verified that is specific to grip

#### F11. Flat feet / no (or mistimed) split step
- **What it looks like / ball result:** Without a split step, the feet are flat and the player is "always a step behind". Heel-down players are flat-footed at the opponent's contact. — [Tennis Nation footwork guide](https://www.tennisnation.com/blog/technique/ultimate-tennis-footwork-guide/); [MyTennisHQ split-step guide](https://mytennishq.com/tennis-split-step-a-comprehensive-guide-with-videos/). Both come from search summaries.
- **Likely cause:** Splitting at contact, which is too late at higher levels, or not at all.
- **Fix cues:** Start the split early so you are in the air as the ball leaves the opponent's racquet. Land wider than shoulder width, knees bent, weight equal on both feet. (Same sources.)
- **Corrective drills:** D14 Call the Bounce; D3 Side-to-Side; D15 X Drill; D28 Defend and Recover (split step with the left foot on the centre mark)
- **Videos:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XR83yXLla8w — "Tennis Split Step Timing, Execution And Drills" — Feel Tennis Instruction; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J1UhPl1UrYs — "How To Master The Split Step in 5 Minutes: Beginner to Pro" — Top Tennis Training - Pro Tennis Lessons; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AYe0uBf_Zis — "How to Master the Tennis Split Step | Timing, Footwork & Movement with Vin Baccelli" — Coachlife - Tennis

#### F12. Opening up too early
- **What it looks like / ball result:** The chest and shoulders rotate to face the net before the forward swing has started. It is named as a common flaw on both the serve and the forehand. — [PlayYourCourt, search summary](https://www.playyourcourt.com/news/?p=9454). Ball result: no sourced description [A].
- **Likely cause:** An incomplete unit turn. — [Tennis Uni](https://tennis-uni.com/en/tennis-forehand/)
- **Fix cues:** "Keep shoulders closed longer during swing" (Tennis Uni). Separate the stroke into turn/takeback, then drop-and-swing ([PlayYourCourt 2019](https://www.playyourcourt.com/news/forehand-killer-part-2/)).
- **Corrective drills:** D33 Unit-Turn Catch; D17 Figure 8. The down-the-line hitter must stay turned longer [A].
- **Video (related):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BuBaV8zjo78 — "7 Tennis Forehand Mistakes EVERY Player Makes (And How to Fix Them)" — Patrik Broddfelt

#### F13. Floating slice (bonus)
- **What it looks like / ball result:** A high, slow slice that sits up for the opponent; or no backspin at all. — [Sportplan 2026](https://www.sportplan.net/drills/Tennis/trends/slice-backhand-tactical-weapon-2025-04.jsp)
- **Likely cause:** A face opened too much plus a high-to-low swing with no forward extension (floater). A face perpendicular to the ground at contact (no spin). An Eastern backhand grip.
- **Fix cues:** Push forward toward the target after contact, not just chop down; face 10-15 degrees open; continental grip; firm wrist; use the ball-on-strings tilt check.
- **Corrective drills:** D29; D30
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zw-1nzGwhrA — "The Backhand Slice Explained: Stop Floating the Ball (Fix This Mistake!)" — Coachlife - Tennis

#### F14. Disjointed vs rushed one-motion forehand (bonus)
- **What it looks like / ball result:** Either a disjointed stroke that breaks tempo, or an overcorrection into "one continuous motion" with no hold after the unit turn. The rushed version loses control because joint positions can't be held. — [PlayYourCourt, Scott Baxter, June 20 2019](https://www.playyourcourt.com/news/forehand-killer-part-2/)
- **Likely cause:** Poor timing of preparation relative to the bounce.
- **Fix cues:** Finish the unit turn and racquet preparation before the ball bounces; a "slight pause" at the top; two segments, turn/takeback then drop-and-swing.
- **Corrective drills:** D33; D2
- **Video:** none verified that is specific to this fault

### Inferences
- Faults F1, F2, F7, F12 and F14 are linked: late or absent unit turn leads to late contact and poor spacing, then to compensations with the arm or wrist. A coach should therefore check preparation timing (turn by the bounce) before correcting the arm [A].
- Constraint drills (D31/D32 for the two-hander, D33 for the turn, D13 for the finish, D12 for depth) fit every age group because they change the task, not the explanation. This suits green-ball kids, who respond to games, and seniors with limited range alike [A].

### Gaps
- No high-quality source was found that describes the ball-flight signature of each fault, for example the direction a late forehand typically misses. Ball results are given only where a source stated them.
- The "wristy flip" on the forehand had no dedicated reputable source; the fix draws on a slice article and grip-pressure guidance.
- Essential Tennis and Fuzzy Yellow Balls written articles were not fetched. Their content is represented only by verified video titles.
- The two-hander claim that dominant-arm pulling also causes "missing wide and lack of depth" appeared only in an unattributed search summary that mixed pickleball sites. It is excluded from the findings.

## What are the classic feed-ball vs live-ball progressions, and how should drills be adapted for adults vs juniors?

### Takeaway
Every curriculum moves from closed (self-feed, hand-feed, racquet-feed) to open (cooperative live ball, then scored live points). Tennis Australia's squad manual states this as a rule. USTA green plans build it into each drill (hand-feed, then racquet-feed from across the net, then faster coach feeds, then competitive). A 2026 Sportplan slice plan adds a 70%-success gate before moving up a step. For adults, the USTA uses the same progressive-ball pathway as for children (red/foam on 36-42 ft, orange on 60 ft, green on 78 ft) with step-back rallies. Juniors get more game-based formats and gradual one-on-one competition.

### Cited Findings
- "Drills/Activities move from closed to open where applicable." Score most activities first to 3/5/7. "80% of shots – player only moves 3m or less. Design drills and activities accordingly." "Worst mistake is hitting into the net." Group players on ability, not necessarily age. Plan every session with a contingency to "skill up" or "skill down". — [Tennis Australia – Tennis Time Coaching Manual (Geoff Quinlan)](https://www.tennis.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2010/08/Drills-and-Activities.pdf)
- TA's manual pairs closed coach-fed blocks ("2 Ball" and "Spanish" feeding drills, 8 balls each, then rotate with roles drilling, shadowing, picking up and recovering) with "Game Based Drills": crosscourt Swedish, Change of Direction, Beat the Champ, and crosscourt points to 11. — [TA manual](https://www.tennis.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2010/08/Drills-and-Activities.pdf)
- USTA green X Drill progression: hand-feed, then racquet-feed from the other side of the net, then the coach feeds to increase tempo and difficulty, then play competitively. — [USTA Green plans](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)
- USTA activity types are labelled Individual, Team, Cooperative, Competitive and Hand/Racquet-Feed. The ball "can be player or coach" fed. — [USTA Green plans](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)
- USTA green 90-minute template: warm-up 6-8 min; character 3 min; skills (athletic + tennis) 30-35 min; games 25-30 min; play-on-your-own review 3 min. Use each plan twice before moving on. — [USTA Green plans](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)
- Transition to the 78-ft court: "Most players' overall skill level will drop, and that should be expected… the most important thing to work on, initially, is court coverage." Use the right racquet size and move up to 27 in as control improves. "At this age and stage, you should slowly introduce one-on-one competition." — [USTA Green plans](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)
- USTA adult beginners follow this sequence:
  - Instant Rally tap-ups, then self-rally on a target, then partner rally on a target, then over a line (red/foam ball, 42-ft court).
  - Rally Now from 10 ft, stepping back after 6 shots.
  - Orange ball on 60 ft, then green ball on 78 ft, aiming between the SL and baseline.
  - Drop-hit-and-trap sequences are used before full rallies. Cue: "Small swing, short distance; bigger swing, greater distance."
  - — [USTA Tennis 101 for Adults](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/USTA-Tennis-101-for-Adults.pdf)
- Sportplan's 2026 slice progression: shadow and self-feed, then coach/partner feed (target depth, then width, about 70% success before progressing), then rally integration, then point play. — [Sportplan](https://www.sportplan.net/drills/Tennis/trends/slice-backhand-tactical-weapon-2025-04.jsp)
- Staged progression for juniors: start rallying just beyond the service box; once players regularly hit a 10-shot rally, move further back. Drills work across colour stages by changing the ball, resizing the court and adjusting the feed. — [Sportplan – 15 Tennis Drills for Junior Players](https://www.sportplan.net/drills/Tennis/guides/tennis-drills-for-juniors.jsp) (search summary)
- The LTA modifies the court size for beginners (service box or half-court), then moves to baseline play as confidence builds. — [LTA](https://www.lta.org.uk/play/tennis-tips-and-techniques/tennis-drills-to-help-build-consistency-in-your-game/)
- USTA level guidance for the backhand: beginners focus on racquet-face control and keeping the ball in; intermediates use longer, faster swings with body rotation and experiment with spin; advanced players win points through offense. — [USTA basics backhand (Pettit 2017)](https://www.usta.com/en/home/improve/tips-and-instruction/national/learning-the-basics--backhand.html)
- Green-ball age bands differ by source:
  - USTA green clinic listings describe green as ages 9-13 transitioning to full court. — [USTA PlayTennis Green Ball course](https://playtennis.usta.com/PlayTennisLV/Coaching/Course/5a8237f6-c976-44c6-9763-fcb5c5e7b9ef) (search summary)
  - Another program describes green as 11-13. — [Park City](https://parkcity.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/62031/638017058051400000) (search summary)
  - LTA-style "Mini Green" is 9-10. — [Liverpool Tennis Centre](https://lifestyles.liverpool.gov.uk/centres/liverpool-tennis-centre/tennis-coaching/) (search summary)
- Video showing the ball-stage framework: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KgRE3CLBV8k — "USTA Florida Go Pro Tip: Red, Orange, Green, & Yellow Tennis Balls" — USTA Florida

### Inferences
- **Junior vs. adult adaptation [A]:**
  - Juniors (green/yellow) get game-based, competitive formats such as Up and Down the River and Crazy Feed, which have built-in rotation and rewards. Feeds should be high and deep to build heavy topspin and court coverage.
  - Adult beginners and seniors get cooperative counting rallies (D6, D36, D37), slower balls (orange/green) and shorter courts, with more closed feeding repetition before live ball. This follows USTA's own adult plan.
  - Adult intermediate-advanced players (NTRP 3.5-4.5) benefit most from coach-fed patterns (D22-D24) that mimic match situations, then scored crosscourt points (D25).
- A practical gate for moving from feed to live ball, drawn from the sources: about 70% success on the fed pattern (Sportplan), or a 10-ball rally at the current distance (Ten to Move On; Sportplan juniors guide), before advancing.

### Gaps
- No PTR/USPTA or ITF primary document defining adult vs. junior drill adaptation was retrieved.
- No sourced guidance was found specifically for senior players' groundstroke drills (load management, court size). Senior assignments are inferences.

## What grip/technique standards do modern coaches teach at each level?

### Takeaway
USTA teaches adult beginners the Eastern ("shake hands") forehand with light grip pressure (3/10) and a low-to-high "6 to 12 o'clock" brush. Coaching sources say semi-western is the grip most commonly taught to juniors today, with Eastern as a stepping stone. On the two-handed backhand, the non-dominant top hand drives, typically with an eastern/semi-western top-hand grip over a continental bottom hand, and the finish has the non-dominant elbow pointing across the net. The slice uses a continental grip, a 45-degree high-to-low path and a face 10-15 degrees open. USTA green plans introduce continental-grip slice and one-handed chip shots alongside topspin groundstrokes.

### Cited Findings
- **Forehand, beginner (USTA):** Eastern forehand grip, "shaking hands with the racquet". Find it by holding the throat with the non-dominant hand, face perpendicular to the ground, and sliding the dominant palm down to the handle. Grip pressure is about 3/10. The swing goes low to high; topspin means brushing up "6 o'clock to 12 o'clock". — [USTA – Tennis 101: The Forehand](https://www.usta.com/en/home/improve/tips-and-instruction/national/learning-the-basics--forehand.html) (search summary)
- **Forehand, juniors:** semi-western is "most commonly taught to juniors at present" and is the most common grip on the pro tour; switch to Eastern if semi-western isn't clicking and work toward semi-western. Eastern is easy to learn and generates power but struggles with very high balls. — [PlayYourCourt – Eastern vs Western grip](https://www.playyourcourt.com/news/eastern-vs-western-grip-tennis/?amp=1) (search summary)
- **Forehand standards:**
  - Unit turn with the left shoulder pointing at the ball (right-hander).
  - Split step as a small hop before the opponent strikes.
  - Contact in front with the face parallel to the net.
  - Drop the racquet, then accelerate up with a loose wrist.
  - Follow through over the left shoulder.
  - Semi-western is "most popular", Eastern is flat/power, Western is topspin but harder.
  - — [Tennis Uni (2020)](https://tennis-uni.com/en/tennis-forehand/)
- **Two-handed backhand (USTA):** dominant hand at the bottom of the handle, non-dominant on top; choose whatever gives the most racquet-face control; grip pressure about 3/10. Topspin path 6 to 12 o'clock, underspin 12 to 6. — [USTA basics backhand, Elliott Pettit 2017](https://www.usta.com/en/home/improve/tips-and-instruction/national/learning-the-basics--backhand.html). The top hand is the "driver", with the non-dominant elbow pointing across the net at the finish. — [USTA, Karl Davies 2018](https://www.usta.com/en/home/improve/tips-and-instruction/national/improve-your-tennis-game--the-driving-force-in-your-two-handed-b.html)
- **Two-handed backhand grips:** a continental bottom hand combined with an Eastern or mild semi-western top hand; the top-hand grip helps generate power and topspin. — [Mouratoglou](https://www.mouratoglou.com/?p=11469); [TennisOne two-hand backhand lesson](https://tennisone.tennisplayer.net/club/lessons/eng/2hand-bh/bh-1.php) (search summaries)
- **Two-handed backhand biomechanics:** an eastern (vs continental) non-dominant grip produced greater peak racket-head angular velocity and post-impact ball speed, with similar accuracy. — [PubMed 35730365](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35730365/) (search summary; full paper not read)
- **Coach practice:** a survey found 58.5% of coaches primarily teach double-handed backhand grip positions in the 7-10 age group. — [Dunn, Sheffield Hallam (SHURA)](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/29030/1/Dunn-PerspectivesPracticesAccredited%28VoR%29.pdf) (search summary; wording not verified in the full text)
- **Slice:**
  - Continental grip (index base knuckle on bevel 2).
  - High-to-low at about 45 degrees, with the face open 10-15 degrees.
  - Contact in front of the lead hip with the arm nearly extended; the non-hitting arm extends back.
  - Forward-and-down finish; firm wrist; closed stance for teaching.
  - — [Sportplan, Apr 2026](https://www.sportplan.net/drills/Tennis/trends/slice-backhand-tactical-weapon-2025-04.jsp)
- **Green ball:** USTA green plans have players "play all shots with continental grip and slice; backhands are one-handed" in slice mini-tennis. Absorb and Rip requires a continental grip on the defensive "juggle" ball. Chip-lob rallies use "continental grip and one-handed backhand". — [USTA Green plans](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf). A green-ball program description says the focus is "developing topspin groundstrokes and using the continental grip on volleys, overheads and serves". — [USTA PlayTennis Champions Tennis – Green Ball](https://playtennis.usta.com/championstennis/EventsV2/GreenBall) / [Park City](https://parkcity.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/62031/638017058051400000) (search summary; exact page not confirmed)
- **Level progression (USTA):** beginners prioritise keeping the ball on court with a controlled swing. Intermediates lengthen and speed up the swing and add underspin and topspin through face-angle changes. Advanced players play strategically and offensively. — [USTA basics backhand](https://www.usta.com/en/home/improve/tips-and-instruction/national/learning-the-basics--backhand.html)

### Inferences
- **Practical grip standard by stage [A]:**
  - **Green 9-11:** semi-western or Eastern forehand with a topspin emphasis; two-handed backhand for most players; continental one-handed slice introduced as a touch or defensive shot (per USTA green plans).
  - **Yellow 11-14 and teens:** semi-western forehand; two-hander with an eastern top hand, or a one-hander for committed players; continental slice.
  - **Adult beginner:** Eastern forehand ("shake hands") with grip pressure 3/10.
  - **Adult intermediate-advanced (NTRP 3.5-4.5):** move toward semi-western for topspin height and add a reliable continental slice backhand.
- Grip pressure "3 out of 10" appears in USTA guidance for both wings and is a universal cue across ages.

### Gaps
- No PTR or USPTA official grip-standard document was retrieved, and no LTA or Tennis Australia technique-standard document by stage was retrieved.
- The biomechanics study (PubMed 35730365) and the coach survey (Dunn, SHU) were seen only through search summaries; sample sizes and exact findings are unverified.
- No source tied grip choices explicitly to NTRP levels.
