## 1. What throwing progressions do federations and top coaches use to build the serve for kids and adult beginners?

### Takeaway
Federations and coaches all start the serve as an **overarm throw**. Kids throw at targets and to set distances, then do a "hand-to-racket" push-down from above the head, then serve from close in and move back line by line. The USTA adds football (quarterback) and over-the-fence throws for older juniors. Adult-beginner methods (Feel Tennis) follow the same order: a two-ball toss-and-throw, then continental-grip familiarity, then pronation, then a serve split into two parts. A kneeling or sitting serve at the T is a widely used bridge that teaches the player to hit **up** on the ball.

#### Drill library: throwing foundations and beginner serve build-up

**S1. Cone Flip Throw to Hand-to-Racket Push-Down**
- **Ages / stage:** Red 5-8, Orange 8-10 (the source is a red-ball school lesson for "Over 8s")
- **Level:** Beginner
- **Skill area:** Serve (overarm foundation; also an overhead foundation)
- **Works on / fixes:** Overarm throwing pattern, sideways start, hitting down from above the head. Also helps kids who push the ball or serve underarm (F5, F8).
- **Players & equipment:** Pairs; red balls; 21-inch rackets; 3 markers per pair; several saucer cones per player.
- **Setup:** Partners face each other behind markers 2 m apart, with a third marker halfway between to make a "micro court". Saucer cones are placed upside down in front of each player. *(added: run on the red-court service area, several pairs side by side along the net.)*
- **Steps:** 1) Throw overarm and try to hit the partner's cones: 1 point for a hit, 10 points if the cone flips. 2) Hand to racket: stand sideways with the racket hand at the back, racket above the head in front of the body, ball resting on the strings; push the ball downward toward the partner's cones. The partner catches and repeats. 3) Racket to racket: one player serves into the target area, the other returns; the pair scores a team point for each serve-return-catch set.
- **Coaching cues:** "Side-on", "Racket above head", "Push down from up high", *(added)* "Elbow up, throw at the cone".
- **Progression:** Throw the ball from shoulder height up to the racket and strike down from above the head, then from waist height. Then make the target area smaller. **Regression:** Non-dominant-hand throws are listed as a *variation*. *(added: easier version is a bigger target, shorter distance, or a foam ball.)*
- **Source:** [Tennis Ireland, Primary School Over 8s (PDF), Lesson 4](https://www.tennisireland.ie/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Primary-School-Over-8s.pdf)
- **Video (related, not drill-identical):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hXLeq30tGAc — "How To Teach Kids Tennis (Ep 1) - The Serve" — The Tennis Mentor

**S2. Three-Level Throw**
- **Ages / stage:** Orange 8-10, Green 9-11 (source: Green Ball 2)
- **Level:** Beginner
- **Skill area:** Serve (throwing foundation, distance and direction)
- **Works on / fixes:** A full throwing action with leg drive and a high release. Fixes "arm-only" serving and missing short into the net (F6, F8).
- **Players & equipment:** 3-6 players per court; balls. Green balls are fine; *(added: tennis balls or bean-bag balls for throwing)*.
- **Setup:** All players on one side of the net in two lines behind the baseline. One line throws from the deuce side and one from the ad side, so two players throw at a time.
- **Steps:** 1) Throw into the diagonal service box twice, then rotate. 2) Throw so the ball lands between the opposite service line and baseline, twice, then rotate. 3) Throw so it lands between the opposite baseline and the fence.
- **Coaching cues:** *(added)* "Side-on, throw up and out", "Front arm points up", "Push off the back foot".
- **Progression:** (1) Three in a row: box, past service line, past baseline. (2) Split the box and court into two halves and throw to a named half. (3) "See who can hit opposite fence." **Regression:** *(added)* Move up to the service line, or use only the first level.
- **Source:** [USTA Net Generation Practice Plan Green, Ch.05 "Serve to Spots" (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)
- **Video (related):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NON_ZSYEhew — "How To Teach Kids The Tennis Serve" — My Tennis Coaching

**S3. Quarterback Throw Progression (football or tennis ball)**
- **Ages / stage:** Yellow juniors 11-14, Teens; also suitable for Adult beginners *(added)*
- **Level:** Beginner
- **Skill area:** Serve (throwing foundation and trophy position)
- **Works on / fixes:** Sideways turn, arm positions in the trophy, and the ball "tip" pointing back behind the head. This becomes the racquet-drop and pronation pattern. Fixes no turn and a pushing arm (F5, F7, F8).
- **Players & equipment:** Pairs, up to 3 pairs per side; one football per pair (a tennis ball if there are not enough).
- **Setup:** Partners stand on opposite sidelines of the court.
- **Steps (each stage only once the previous one is done well):** 1) Play catch, throwing a spiral. 2) Add distance. 3) Add quarterback movement: face forward, turn and run back, set, throw. 4) Start facing forward with both hands on the ball, then turn sideways with the throwing arm up and to the side, bent at 90°. 5) Turn sideways with both hands on the ball and separate: front arm bent and parallel to the ground; back arm at 90° with the ball behind the head. 6) Same preparation, but point the tip of the ball backward behind the head.
- **Coaching cues:** "Turn sideways", "Front arm parallel", "Ball behind head", "Tip points back".
- **Progression:** The source lists stages 1-5 as progressions. **Regression:** Use a tennis ball. *(added: shorter distance.)*
- **Source:** [USTA Net Generation Practice Plan Yellow, "Quarterback" (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Yellow.pdf)
- **Video (related throwing-to-pronation drills):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-9cIObcQyME — "7 Serve Pronation Drills For A Better Tennis Serve" — Feel Tennis Instruction

**S4. Fence Throws**
- **Ages / stage:** Green 9-11, Yellow juniors 11-14 *(added: works for adult beginners too)*
- **Level:** Beginner
- **Skill area:** Serve (upward throwing trajectory)
- **Works on / fixes:** Throwing **up** rather than forward, which fixes low or flat contact and serves into the net (F6, F8).
- **Players & equipment:** Pairs, 2 balls per pair, a court fence.
- **Setup:** One partner outside the fence and one inside, each about 10 ft from the fence.
- **Steps:** Throw the ball over the fence so the partner can catch it without moving far. Throw two balls; the partner catches and throws them back.
- **Coaching cues:** *(added)* "Up and over", "Long arm at release".
- **Progression:** Start with the ball on the ground behind the back foot (pick up, load, throw). *(added: step farther from the fence.)* **Regression:** *(added)* Move closer to the fence.
- **Source:** [USTA Net Generation Practice Plan Yellow, "Fence Throws" (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Yellow.pdf)
- **Video:** No verified drill-specific video found.

**S5. Two-Ball Toss-and-Throw**
- **Ages / stage:** Adult beginner, Senior, Teens *(source is aimed at beginners in general)*; Orange and Green as well *(added)*
- **Level:** Beginner
- **Skill area:** Serve (coordinating the toss arm and hitting arm, rhythm)
- **Works on / fixes:** Each arm's role, toss accuracy, balance. Fixes an inconsistent toss and arms working out of sync (F3, F4).
- **Players & equipment:** Individual; 2 balls; *(added: a chalk or throw-down square on the court for toss accuracy)*.
- **Setup:** Stand at the baseline in serving stance with a ball in each hand, arms in front and balls touching. *(added: mark a small square in front of the lead foot and inside the baseline.)*
- **Steps:** Toss one ball straight up with the non-dominant arm while swinging the dominant arm back. Throw the second ball forward as far as possible. Do 20-30 reps.
- **Coaching cues:** "Toss with the non-dominant arm, throw with the dominant arm". Toss accuracy "within a marked square"; keep balance.
- **Progression:** *(added)* Throw over the net to land in the diagonal box, then go to S7 (serve in two parts). **Regression:** *(added)* Toss only and let the ball land in the square.
- **Source:** [Feel Tennis, Serve Lesson for Beginners, Step 1](https://www.feeltennis.net/serve-lesson-for-beginners/)
- **Video (related, beginner serve lesson):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m4UQBa1HtiQ — "Serve Lesson for Beginners: How to Hit a Tennis Serve" — Essential Tennis - Lessons and Instruction for Passionate Players

**S6. Kneeling or Sitting Serve at the T**
- **Ages / stage:** Green 9-11 to Adult advanced. *(added: kneeling is a common bridge for kids, but no kid-specific source was found.)*
- **Level:** Beginner to Intermediate (flat or slice); Advanced for the kick version (see S16)
- **Skill area:** Serve
- **Works on / fixes:** Hitting **up** and extending into the ball; isolates the upper body. Fixes letting the toss drop, pushing the ball, and serving into the net (F5, F8).
- **Players & equipment:** Individual or a coach; a basket of balls; *(added: a knee pad, mat or racket cover under the knee)*.
- **Setup:** Kneel or sit on the baseline **or** the service line next to the center mark (T), aiming into the diagonal service box.
- **Steps:** Toss and serve from the knees or seated, swinging "up and into the ball before swinging out" to the service box. *(added: 10-15 balls per set, then 5 standing serves to transfer the feel.)*
- **Coaching cues:** "Extend up into the ball", "Same toss spot every time", "Exhale through the stroke".
- **Progression:** *(added)* Kneel at the baseline instead of the service line; aim at a target cone; go to S16 (kneeling kick). **Regression:** Kneel at the service line *(source gives both positions)*.
- **Source:** [Human Kinetics, *The Tennis Drill Book*, 2nd ed. excerpt](https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/thrust-launch-serve-technique)
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SexL4O0LaZw — "Tennis Kick Serve Drill - Serve From Your Knee For A Better Kick Serve" — Total Tennis Domination

**S7. Serve in Two Parts (toss, catch, trophy check)**
- **Ages / stage:** Orange 8-10 to Senior *(source: beginners)*
- **Level:** Beginner
- **Skill area:** Serve (rhythm, toss, trophy position)
- **Works on / fixes:** A consistent toss, getting into the trophy position, and timing the swing to the toss (F3, F4, F7).
- **Players & equipment:** Individual; balls; racquet in continental grip.
- **Setup:** Behind the baseline in serving stance *(added: or at the service line for kids)*.
- **Steps:** 1) Toss and **catch** the ball in the tossing hand, checking the trophy position. 2) Repeat the toss and serve. Alternate catch and serve.
- **Coaching cues:** "Toss, freeze in trophy, catch", "Guide the body toward a complete serve; it won't be smooth yet".
- **Progression:** Gradually blend into a full serve motion. **Regression:** *(added)* Do toss-and-catch with no racquet.
- **Source:** [Feel Tennis, Serve Lesson for Beginners, Step 5](https://www.feeltennis.net/serve-lesson-for-beginners/)
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZFy99x_fMXc — "Tennis Serve - Serve Step 2 - Toss and Backswing" — Fuzzy Yellow Balls

**S8. Three-Level Serve (service line to baseline to fence)**
- **Ages / stage:** Green 9-11, Yellow juniors 11-14 (source: Yellow Ball 2), Teens, Adult beginner/intermediate *(added)*
- **Level:** Beginner to Intermediate
- **Skill area:** Serve (racquet-head speed, hitting "up and out")
- **Works on / fixes:** Racquet-head speed and an upward swing path. Fixes decelerating or "guiding" serves (F5, F8, F11).
- **Players & equipment:** 3-6 players per court; basket of balls.
- **Setup:** All players on the baseline on one side of the net, half on the deuce side and half on the ad side. Two serve at a time, one from each side.
- **Steps:** 1) Warm-up serves into the service box, two each, then rotate. 2) Serve so the ball lands between the opposite service line and baseline, twice, then rotate. 3) Serve so it lands between the opposite baseline and the fence.
- **Coaching cues:** "Racquet-head speed", "Hit up and out on the ball".
- **Progression:** (1) Serve three in a row: box, past service line, past baseline. (2) Split the box and court into two sides and serve to a named area. **Regression:** *(added)* Start from the service line (the USTA orange "Serve Plus One" starts at the service line).
- **Source:** [USTA Net Generation Practice Plan Yellow, "Three-Level Serve" (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Yellow.pdf)
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5a-ryZnrmh8 — "Tennis Serve - 3 Drills To Improve Power and Consistency" — Top Tennis Training - Pro Tennis Lessons

### Inferences
- Every source follows the same pattern: **throw, then hit down from above the head, then serve close in, then move back.** The order fits this library: red/orange use S1, S2 and the orange "Serve Plus One" from the service line; green/yellow use S2-S4 and S8; adults use S5-S7 and S6.
- The USTA Quarterback stages describe the trophy position (front arm parallel, back arm at 90°, "tip points back"). Coaches can say "football throw" as a cue for adult beginners who already know how to throw.

## 2. What drills build toss consistency, continental grip, pronation, rhythm, kick/slice serve, and serve placement (T, body, wide)?

### Takeaway
Experts agree on the order: **grip and edge first** (swing "on edge", continental tapping), **then pronation** (ground-smack and throw-and-let-go drills), **then rhythm** (USTA "Drag Serve"), **then spin and targets**. Placement is trained with zoned service boxes (USTA "Serve Index") and cones. The kick serve is built from kneeling and raised-net drills plus a modified toss. Emory's tennis-medicine program advises **delaying the kick serve until after age 13** and hitting flat serves before adding spin.

### Cited Findings
- Feel Tennis says the grip "has to be changed to continental before doing any corrective exercises." The waiter's tray happens when the player faces the court too early or carries forehand-grip muscle memory — [Feel Tennis, Correcting the Waiter's Serve](https://www.feeltennis.net/correcting-the-waiters-serve/)
- Feel Tennis's continental familiarity drill: tap the ball downward with the face "parallel to your body and not away from you" for 2-3 minutes. Comfort with the grip typically takes "3-6 months" — [Feel Tennis, Serve Lesson for Beginners](https://www.feeltennis.net/serve-lesson-for-beginners/)
- Feel Tennis's seven pronation drills: throw balls and let go; lead with the edge and finish behind the back; lead with the edge and finish in front; slice to the ad court with late pronation; serve with no body rotation facing the net post; smack the ball on the ground; hit the ball backwards — [Feel Tennis, The Serve Pronation Technique And 7 Drills](https://www.feeltennis.net/serve-pronation/)
- USTA "Drag Serve": start with the racquet pointing straight down slightly behind the back leg. Toss first, then move the hitting arm when the ball reaches its peak. The teaching tip says it keeps the palm down in the backswing, "which simulates a throwing motion" — [USTA Green (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)
- USTA "Serve Index": throw-down lines split each box into 2 zones. Count the balls needed to hit all 4 zones, and serves count only "if you use same rituals before each serve". The yellow progression asks right-handers to slice wide on the deuce side and down the T on the ad side — [USTA Green (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf); [USTA Yellow (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Yellow.pdf)
- Kneeling kick drill (video description via search): "Place one knee on a mat or racquet cover and practice hitting over the higher net towards the cone," finishing "hand behind" the back to help pronation — [YouTube: Be Gr8 at Tennis - Rob Cherry](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QyMIKPF1FH0)
- Thrust-launch leg drive: step the back foot up next to the lead foot during the toss, bend both knees, "launch up and out with the legs together," strike at the peak, land on the lead foot. Cue: "place the toss in the same location every time" — [Human Kinetics excerpt](https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/thrust-launch-serve-technique)
- "Delay kick serve until after age 13 to reduce stressful loads"; "Hit flat serves before adding spin"; "Increase the amount of knee flexion (knee bend) to reduce shoulder and elbow torque" — [Emory Healthcare Tennis Medicine Program, Wellness](https://www.emoryhealthcare.org/centers-programs/sports-medicine-center/tennis-medicine-program/wellness)

#### Drill library: toss, grip, pronation, rhythm, spin and placement

**S9. Toss Target ("Same Spot" Toss)**
- **Ages / stage:** Orange 8-10 to Senior
- **Level:** Beginner to Intermediate
- **Skill area:** Serve (toss)
- **Works on / fixes:** Toss consistency and toss too far behind, in front or to the side (F3, F4). Also helps keep the tossing arm up.
- **Players & equipment:** Individual; balls; *(added: a target such as a hoop, racquet face, towel or throw-down square)*.
- **Setup:** Serving stance at the baseline. *(added: the target sits just inside the baseline, in front of and slightly to the hitting side of the lead foot, for flat or slice; for kick see S16.)*
- **Steps:** Toss to full height with no swing and let the ball fall; it should land on or in the target. Then add the trophy position, then the swing *(added: 10 tosses per set; score the hits out of 10)*.
- **Coaching cues:** "Place the toss in the same location every time" (Human Kinetics); toss accuracy "within a marked square" (Feel Tennis); *(added)* "Lift, don't flick", "Release at eye level or above".
- **Progression:** *(added)* Smaller target; toss with eyes on the contact point; add the full serve and catch any bad toss. **Regression:** *(added)* Lower toss, bigger target.
- **Source:** [Feel Tennis, Serve Lesson for Beginners (toss into marked square)](https://www.feeltennis.net/serve-lesson-for-beginners/); [Human Kinetics excerpt (same location every time)](https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/thrust-launch-serve-technique)
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UJ3xNfw7G3Y — "Tame Your Serve Toss With The "Keep Lifting" Drill" — Feel Tennis Instruction. Also https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9YxgjV1Dtls — "Tennis Serve Progressions Step 3: Toss" — Fuzzy Yellow Balls; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jmYsWY36XHU — "3 Tennis Drills for Perfect Serve Toss" — Cosmin | WebTennis24

**S10. Continental Edge Tap (tapping the ball down)**
- **Ages / stage:** Orange 8-10 to Senior (good for adult beginners moving from a forehand grip)
- **Level:** Beginner
- **Skill area:** Serve (continental grip)
- **Works on / fixes:** Comfort with the continental grip; the face sits parallel to the body. Fixes the frying-pan grip (F1).
- **Players & equipment:** Individual; 1 ball; racquet.
- **Setup:** Anywhere on court. Hold the racquet in continental grip, choked up toward the throat.
- **Steps:** Gently tap the ball downward (bounce dribble) for 2-3 minutes, keeping the face parallel to the body.
- **Coaching cues:** "Racket angled parallel to your body, not away from you".
- **Progression:** *(added)* Move the hand to the end of the grip; alternate up and down taps on the same edge; walk while tapping. **Regression:** *(added)* Choke up more; use a red or orange ball.
- **Source:** [Feel Tennis, Serve Lesson for Beginners, Step 2](https://www.feeltennis.net/serve-lesson-for-beginners/)
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=quUydRpf96Q — "Fix The Most Common Error On The Serve - Continental Grip Drills That Work!!" — Patrick Smith Tennis Coaching. Also https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=85TTLdfvzPk — "How To Serve With A Continental Grip" — 2MinuteTennis

**S11. Swing on Edge / Lead With the Edge (shadow, no ball)**
- **Ages / stage:** Green 9-11 to Senior
- **Level:** Beginner to Intermediate
- **Skill area:** Serve (grip, swing path, set-up for pronation)
- **Works on / fixes:** Waiter's tray and an open face in the backswing (F2); forehand grip (F1).
- **Players & equipment:** Individual; racquet; no ball.
- **Setup:** Serving stance at the baseline *(added: side-on to the net, facing the side fence)*.
- **Steps:** Lead with the **bottom edge** into the backswing, the **top edge** into the loop behind the back, then the **bottom edge** out toward an imaginary ball, finishing on the right side (right-hander). Then hold the racquet with only thumb and index finger for more freedom. A related drill: three-finger figure-8 loops while rotating the body and lifting the heel (Feel Tennis beginner Step 4).
- **Coaching cues:** "Edge leads", "Stay sideways longer", "Smooth loops".
- **Progression:** Thumb-and-index-finger hold. Then add the ball and serve "with the edge" (S13). **Regression:** *(added)* Do it in slow motion in front of a mirror or the fence.
- **Source:** [Feel Tennis, Correcting the Waiter's Serve, Drill 1](https://www.feeltennis.net/correcting-the-waiters-serve/); [Feel Tennis beginner lesson, Step 4](https://www.feeltennis.net/serve-lesson-for-beginners/)
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9QLLwCrQKLI — "Tennis Serve Progressions Step 1: Swing on Edge" — Fuzzy Yellow Balls

**S12. Ground-Smack Pronation**
- **Ages / stage:** Yellow juniors 11-14 to Senior *(added: gentle reps only for seniors with shoulder history)*
- **Level:** Beginner to Intermediate
- **Skill area:** Serve (pronation)
- **Works on / fixes:** Pronation with internal shoulder rotation. Fixes pushing the ball or no pronation (F8).
- **Players & equipment:** Individual; balls; racquet in continental grip.
- **Setup:** *(added: stand near the fence or the back of the court)*. Racquet above the shoulder in a "hammer" position, edge facing the ball.
- **Steps:** Toss or drop the ball and let it bounce. Gently hit it into the ground while pronating, keeping the racquet on the dominant side at the finish. Do 30-50 controlled reps. Variation: hit the ball "backwards" after the bounce with a same-side follow-through.
- **Coaching cues:** "Start smooth; add power just before contact"; don't force the movement ("avoid forcing movements to prevent injury").
- **Progression:** Pronation drills 4-5: slice to the ad court with late pronation (S14); flat serve facing the net post with forearm only. **Regression:** *(added)* Shadow swings with no ball.
- **Source:** [Feel Tennis, Serve Lesson for Beginners, Step 3](https://www.feeltennis.net/serve-lesson-for-beginners/); [Feel Tennis, Serve Pronation, drills 6-7](https://www.feeltennis.net/serve-pronation/)

**S13. Serve With the Edge, then Fence Serve**
- **Ages / stage:** Teens, Adult beginner to advanced, Senior
- **Level:** Intermediate
- **Skill area:** Serve
- **Works on / fixes:** Waiter's tray and early opening (F2); turning to face the court too early.
- **Players & equipment:** Individual; basket of balls; a court fence.
- **Setup:** Part A: baseline, normal serving position. Part B: stand about **2 ft from the fence**, side-on as in serving stance, so the swing path runs along the fence.
- **Steps:** A) Hit 20-30 balls keeping the racquet edge-aligned the whole way through ("technique over placement"). Then "change your mind late": pronate at the last moment so the strings meet the ball. B) Serve near the fence. If the racquet hits the fence, the face opened too early or you rotated forward too soon.
- **Coaching cues:** "Edge to the ball, strings at the last moment", "Stay sideways", "Expect resistance from the old pattern".
- **Progression:** Slice to the ad court (S14), then full serves to targets (S15). **Regression:** *(added)* Shadow at the fence with no ball.
- **Source:** [Feel Tennis, Correcting the Waiter's Serve, Drills 4-6](https://www.feeltennis.net/correcting-the-waiters-serve/)

**S14. Slice to the Ad Court ("pronate late")**
- **Ages / stage:** Yellow juniors 11-14, Teens, Adult intermediate/advanced, Senior
- **Level:** Intermediate
- **Skill area:** Serve (slice) and serve targets
- **Works on / fixes:** Slice spin, staying sideways, pronation timing. Fixes waiter's tray (F2) and opening up early. It is a safer second serve for seniors *(added)*.
- **Players & equipment:** Individual; basket of balls.
- **Setup:** Serve from the deuce side *(added: the source's "toward ad court" means sending the ball left of the body line, which keeps you sideways; a right-hander can also serve from the ad side to the wide target)*.
- **Steps:** Serve slowly with the slice grip. Lead with the edge, brush the slice, and pronate **after** contact. At first "pronate too late", then gradually shorten the delay.
- **Coaching cues:** "Lead with edge", "Pronate too late at first", "Stay sideways".
- **Progression:** Feel Tennis pronation drill 5: serve facing the net post with no body rotation. *(added: aim at the S15 wide zone.)* **Regression:** *(added)* Do it from the service line.
- **Source:** [Feel Tennis, Serve Pronation, drill 4](https://www.feeltennis.net/serve-pronation/); [Feel Tennis, Correcting the Waiter's Serve, drill 3](https://www.feeltennis.net/correcting-the-waiters-serve/)

**S15. Serve Index (zone targets: T, body, wide)**
- **Ages / stage:** Green 9-11 (Green 2), Yellow juniors 11-14 (Yellow 2), Teens, all Adults, Senior *(added)*
- **Level:** Beginner to Advanced (scale the zones)
- **Skill area:** Serve targets; second serve and routine
- **Works on / fixes:** Direction, a repeatable pre-serve ritual, using spin for control. Fixes double faults under pressure (F11) and having no placement plan.
- **Players & equipment:** Up to 4 per court; throw-down lines (TDLs), cones or poly-spots; extra players act as returners.
- **Setup:** All players on one side; two serve at once, one from deuce and one from ad. TDLs split **each** service box vertically into 2 zones (a "T half" and a "wide half"), making 4 zones across both boxes. *(added: for adults, use 3 zones per box — wide, body, T — about 1 m wide each, with cones at the back of each zone.)*
- **Steps:** Count how many balls it takes to serve successfully into all 4 zones; that number is your "serve index". Serves count only if the same ritual is used before each one. After everyone finishes, start again and chase a personal best. Yellow team version: add players' indexes together for a team serve index and aim for the lowest.
- **Coaching cues:** "Same ritual every serve", "Use spin to add control".
- **Progression:** All serves must have spin. Yellow: right-handers must **slice wide on the deuce side and down the T on the ad side** (the reverse for lefties). *(added: second serves only; returner calls in/out.)* **Regression:** *(added)* Bigger zones (whole half-box); serve from the service line.
- **Source:** [USTA Green, Ch.05 "Serve Index" (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf); [USTA Yellow, "Serve Index" (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Yellow.pdf)

**S16. Kneeling Kick Serve Over a Raised Net**
- **Ages / stage:** Teens 14-18, Adult intermediate/advanced. **Not before age 13** (Emory guidance on the kick serve).
- **Level:** Intermediate to Advanced
- **Skill area:** Second serve / kick
- **Works on / fixes:** Brushing low to high, net clearance, pronation finishing behind the back. Fixes a flat, low second serve and double faults (F11).
- **Players & equipment:** Individual; basket; mat or racquet cover for the knee; a cone target; a way to raise the net *(added: a rope or string tied about 1-1.5 m above the net tape between poles, or two tall cones with a string)*.
- **Setup:** Kneel on one knee at the baseline next to the center mark. *(added: put the cone deep in the diagonal box near the service line, middle or wide.)*
- **Steps:** Serve kick over the higher "net" toward the cone, finishing with the arm behind the back.
- **Coaching cues:** "Brush up", "Finish arm behind back", *(added)* "Hit up to the sky; spin brings it down".
- **Progression:** *(added)* Stand up and serve over the raised net; add a kick-specific toss (commonly taught as more over the head than the flat toss; see the FYB kick-toss video); Chris Lewit cone drill. **Regression:** *(added)* Do it from the service line.

**S17. Drag Serve (rhythm and relaxed arm)**
- **Ages / stage:** Green 9-11 (Green 2), Yellow, Teens, Adults
- **Level:** Beginner to Intermediate
- **Skill area:** Serve (rhythm, racquet drop, racquet-head speed)
- **Works on / fixes:** Toss before arm; relaxed hitting arm; palm down in the backswing. Fixes a rushed swing, an early or missing racquet drop, arm tension, and swinging before the toss peaks (F7).
- **Players & equipment:** Pairs, 2 pairs per court.
- **Setup:** Partners on the baselines, diagonally across the net from each other.
- **Steps:** Start with the racquet pointing straight down, slightly behind the back leg. **Toss first**, and move the hitting arm only when the ball reaches its peak. Serve twice; the partner catches and serves back.
- **Coaching cues:** "Toss first", "Arm goes at the peak", "Relaxed arm", "Palm down like a throw".
- **Progression:** Drag serve on one, full motion on the next. **Regression:** *(added)* From the service line; toss-and-catch only.
- **Source:** [USTA Green, Ch.05 "Drag Serve" (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)

**S18. Thrust Launch (leg drive)**
- **Ages / stage:** Teens 14-18, Adult intermediate/advanced *(added: not recommended for seniors with knee or back issues)*
- **Level:** Intermediate to Advanced
- **Skill area:** Serve (power from the legs)
- **Works on / fixes:** Knee bend and leg drive; landing on the lead foot. Fixes no knee bend (F6) and falling sideways (F9).
- **Players & equipment:** Individual; basket.
- **Setup:** Baseline or service-line center (T), in the normal service ready position.
- **Steps:** Shift weight forward as in flat-serve footwork. Step up with the back foot as you toss and place it next to the lead foot. Bend both knees together, "launch up and out with the legs together", strike at the peak of the toss, then land on the lead foot and follow through.
- **Coaching cues:** "Load both knees", "Launch up and out", "Land on the front foot", "Exhale".
- **Progression:** *(added)* Move to the baseline and serve to S15 zones. **Regression:** Kneeling serve (S6) to teach "up", then the service line.
- **Source:** [Human Kinetics, *The Tennis Drill Book*, "Thrust Launch Serve"](https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/thrust-launch-serve-technique)
- **Video (related):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6aPfX-hIOqc — "How To Tennis Serve Step By Step" — Tennis Evolution; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_XO7IEHDkl0 — "Serve- Hip Flexor Snap Towel Drill" — Patrick Dougherty

**S19. Team Defender (one-serve pressure game)**
- **Ages / stage:** Green 9-11 (Green 2 version), Yellow juniors 11-14 (Yellow 2 version), Teens, Adults *(added)*
- **Level:** Intermediate
- **Skill area:** Serve (holding serve, second serve under pressure) and return
- **Works on / fixes:** Using the serve and first ball offensively under scoring pressure. Fixes double faults under pressure (F11).
- **Players & equipment:** At least 6 players and 2 courts; teams of 3-4.
- **Setup:** Each team is assigned a court to "defend". Each team sends one player to challenge another team's court; the rest stay to defend.
- **Steps (Yellow 2):** Defenders serve to the challenger and play one point, then rotate out. Only the serving side can score; the defender can only stop the serving team scoring. The first team to a set number wins the round. **Green 2 variant ("Attack second serve"):** the challenger gets **one serve**; the defender must return and come in.
- **Coaching cues:** "Serve plus first ball", *(added)* "Same ritual", "Commit to the spin".
- **Progression:** The server must hit a forehand on the first ball after the serve; 2 points for a volley or overhead winner. Green: return to the open court; serve to a set spot. **Regression:** *(added)* Two serves allowed; serve from the service line.
- **Source:** [USTA Yellow, "Team Defender" (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Yellow.pdf); [USTA Green, "Team Defender" (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)

## 3. What return drills (split-step timing, short backswing, block returns, return-to-target games) and overhead drills (lob-feed progressions, scissor kick, footwork, bounce overhead) are recommended?

### Takeaway
USTA Net Generation plans give ready-to-run **return games**: Serve Plus One, First Four, Serve Pyramids, Return the Cannon, Up and Down the River, Two-on-One, and Step Up. Across them the goals repeat: "split-step", "take return on the rise", "compact backswing", "load from legs". **Overhead** progressions run from a cooperative drop-hit lob warm-up (USTA), to coach-fed lobs with shadowing (Sportplan), to "smash and touch the net" repeats (CoachTube), to movement-back throwing (USTA Three Ball Throw), to lob games. Scissor-kick material is mostly video-based.

### Cited Findings
- USTA Yellow "First Four Team" goal: "Work on serve and split-step; return and split-step with proper recovery" — [USTA Yellow (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Yellow.pdf)
- USTA Green coaching tip: green-ball players "need at least two types of returns": a rally return from behind the baseline, and "an attacking return on the rise when the serve is more predictable" — [USTA Green (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)

#### Drill library: return of serve

**R1. Serve Plus One**
- **Ages / stage:** Orange 8-10 (Orange 3), Yellow juniors 11-14 (Yellow 2 version from the baseline)
- **Level:** Beginner
- **Skill area:** Return (and serve)
- **Works on / fixes:** Serve and return direction and consistency; continental grip and correct foot position on the serve.
- **Players & equipment:** Two pairs per court; orange balls (orange) or yellow balls.
- **Setup:** Each pair hits diagonally across the net (crosscourt boxes). **Orange: players start on the service line.** Yellow: full court, start on the baseline.
- **Steps:** Serve, return, server hits one groundstroke (yellow: to the open court), and the returner traps the ball. The returner then serves and repeats. Each player does 4 sequences, then switch sides (deuce to ad). After a full rotation, play it as a game: the pair scores 1 point per three-ball sequence, plus a bonus point if the server's groundstroke lands past the service line.
- **Coaching cues:** "Continental on the serve", "Feet in position", *(added)* "Split as the server tosses".
- **Progression:** Start on the baseline; serve to a set half of the box. **Regression:** *(added)* Drop-hit serve; returner catches and throws.
- **Source:** [USTA Orange Sample, "Serve Plus One" (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/curriculum-center/coach-samples/assets/Net-Generation-Coaches-Curriculum-Orange-Sample.pdf); [USTA Yellow (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Yellow.pdf)
- **Video:** No verified drill-specific video found.

**R2. First Four Team (serve, return, 3rd and 4th balls)**
- **Ages / stage:** Yellow juniors 11-14 (Yellow 1), Teens, Adult beginner/intermediate *(added)*
- **Level:** Beginner to Intermediate
- **Skill area:** Return (split-step and recovery)
- **Works on / fixes:** Split-step timing for both server and returner, and recovery. Fixes a returner who stands still (F12).
- **Players & equipment:** Two pairs per court.
- **Setup:** Pairs diagonally across the net from each other; players start on the baseline.
- **Steps:** Serve, return, then one groundstroke each; the server catches the 4th ball and starts again. 1 point per successful four-ball sequence. Play court vs court. After 4 attempts switch servers; switch sides after a full rotation.
- **Coaching cues:** "Serve and split-step", "Return and split-step, then recover".
- **Progression:** Serve to a set half of the box; all four balls to set sides. **Regression:** *(added)* Second-serve pace only; returner may catch the serve on the first rep.
- **Source:** [USTA Yellow, Ch.02, "First Four Team" (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Yellow.pdf)
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XR83yXLla8w — "Tennis Split Step Timing, Execution And Drills" — Feel Tennis Instruction. Also https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hcwNlrTKB5c — "Split-Step and Return of Serve Footwork" — TPA tennis

**R3. Serve Pyramids (aggressive return)**
- **Ages / stage:** Green 9-11 (Green 2), Yellow, Teens
- **Level:** Intermediate
- **Skill area:** Return (aggressive / on the rise, return to target)
- **Works on / fixes:** Moving in and loading from the legs. Fixes a passive returner (F12).
- **Players & equipment:** Two teams of 2-3, half-court; 4 balls per team for the pyramid.
- **Setup:** Half-court singles. One player serves, the other returns. Each team has a set area behind the baseline where it builds a four-ball pyramid.
- **Steps:** The server gets **one** serve; the returner gets **two chances**. The returner moves in, drives the ball to the open court, and plays out the point. The team adds a ball to its pyramid for each point won. First complete pyramid wins; start a new game and switch the serving team.
- **Coaching cues:** "Take it on the rise", "Load from the legs", "Drive to the open court".
- **Progression:** Returner may hit to either side; must hit a forehand return; must come in. **Regression:** *(added)* Returner starts at the baseline and any return in counts.
- **Source:** [USTA Green, Ch.07 "Serve Pyramids" (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_pS0otk2560 — "Top 3 Drills For Perfect Return of Serve in Tennis" — Top Tennis Training - Pro Tennis Lessons

**R4. Return the Cannon (block return vs. big serve)**
- **Ages / stage:** Green 9-11 (Green 2), Yellow, Teens, Adult intermediate/advanced *(added)*
- **Level:** Intermediate
- **Skill area:** Return (block or short-backswing return), plus serve speed
- **Works on / fixes:** Returning pace with a compact swing. Fixes a big backswing on the return (F13). The server gets racquet-head speed.
- **Players & equipment:** Two pairs per court, half-court; TDLs.
- **Setup:** TDLs split the court in half; all points crosscourt. **The server stands on the service line**; the returner is in the normal return position *(added: about on the baseline in the diagonal half)*.
- **Steps:** The server tries to hit an unreturnable serve. The server wins the point if the returner can't return crosscourt; the returner wins by returning crosscourt. Play to 4, switch roles, then switch sides.
- **Coaching cues:** *(added)* "Short turn, firm hand", "Block it back crosscourt", "Use the server's pace".
- **Progression:** Play out the point on the half-court, with the returner scoring 2 for winning the rally. The server serves from between the service line and the baseline. **Regression:** *(added)* Server at three-quarter pace; returner starts a step back.
- **Source:** [USTA Green, Ch.05 "Return the Cannon" (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3TxZTZEEg44 — "TENNIS RETURN | Block Your Return Of Serve With This Grip" — Tennis Evolution

**R5. Team Up and Down the River (return inside the baseline)**
- **Ages / stage:** Yellow juniors 11-14 (Yellow 1), Teens, Adults *(added)*
- **Level:** Intermediate to Advanced
- **Skill area:** Return (on the rise, return to target)
- **Works on / fixes:** Taking the return early and moving the server. Fixes a passive returner who stands deep (F12).
- **Players & equipment:** Teams of two on opposite baselines; multiple courts.
- **Setup:** Full-court singles points. **The returner stands inside the baseline.**
- **Steps:** Each team serves two points in turn. The server gets **one serve**; the returner drives the ball to a corner. The winning player stays in for an extra point and the loser rotates out. On "time", the winning team moves up a court and the losing team moves down (partners split).
- **Coaching cues:** "Take the return on the rise", "Drive it to a corner".
- **Progression:** Returner must hit a forehand; return and come to the net. **Regression:** "Serve underhand or with drop hit."
- **Source:** [USTA Yellow, Ch.02, "Team Up and Down the River" (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Yellow.pdf)
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9xlqYxbSnA4 — "Dominate The Return Of Serve | 4 Easy Tennis Tips" — 2MinuteTennis

**R6. Pinch + Step Up (compact backswing, second-serve attack)**
- **Ages / stage:** Green 9-11, Yellow, Teens, Adult intermediate/advanced
- **Level:** Intermediate
- **Skill area:** Return (short backswing, early contact)
- **Works on / fixes:** Taking the ball early with a compact backswing; attacking second serves. Fixes a big backswing (F13) and a passive returner (F12).
- **Players & equipment:** Pairs; 2-3 pairs per court.
- **Setup:** Part A, Pinch: rally with **toes on the service line** and no stepping backward, then back up and rally with toes on the baseline. Part B, Step Up: in points, the returner stands **right behind the service line** to return second serves.
- **Steps:** A) Rally any groundstrokes for up to 2 minutes per line, taking deep balls on the rise and volleying anything in the air. B) Play points; on every second serve the returner steps in behind the service line.
- **Coaching cues:** "Don't step back", "Prepare early", "Compact backswing".
- **Progression:** Straight ahead, then crosscourt, then one stroke crosscourt, then alternate strokes. "Return and come to net on all second serves" (Yellow "Return and Come In"). **Regression:** *(added)* Do Pinch from mid-court with slower balls.
- **Source:** [USTA Green, Ch.07 "Pinch" and "Step Up" (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf); [USTA Yellow, "Return and Come In" (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Yellow.pdf)
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PWSOJuufdpE — "Return of Serve Drill - Coaching the Advanced Tennis Player" — PlaySportsTV

**R7. Two-on-One Attack (return and come in)**
- **Ages / stage:** Green 9-11 (Green 2), Yellow, Teens, Adult advanced (doubles) *(added)*
- **Level:** Intermediate to Advanced
- **Skill area:** Return (chip and charge, return and approach)
- **Works on / fixes:** Taking the return early and moving forward with a hop step. Fixes a passive returner (F12).
- **Players & equipment:** 4-5 players per court.
- **Setup:** One side has a net player and a baseliner covering the doubles court, with extra players lined up behind the baseliner. On the other side a single "champ" stands on the baseline covering a half-court (middle to the doubles line).
- **Steps:** The champ gets **one serve**; the returner comes to the net and the point is played two on one. The champ can't lob the first two balls. After each point rotate: baseliner to net, net player to the back of the line. After 7 points, bring on a new champ.
- **Coaching cues:** "Take return early", "Hop move to approach".
- **Progression:** Champ must lob within the first two shots; returner must use a forehand. **Regression:** *(added)* Champ serves second-serve pace.
- **Source:** [USTA Green, Ch.07 "Two-on-One Attack" (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Dl_vM5TnDY4 — "Tennis Lesson - Return of Serve Video #3 - Ready Hop/Split Step - Insight Tennis" — Insight Tennis

#### Drill library: overhead

**O1. Overhead Warm-Up (cooperative drop-hit lobs)**
- **Ages / stage:** Green 9-11, Yellow juniors 11-14 (Yellow 2), Teens, Adults, Senior *(added)*
- **Level:** Beginner
- **Skill area:** Overhead
- **Works on / fixes:** Turning and lifting both arms together, controlled direction. Fixes no turn / no pointing hand (F15) and letting the ball drop (F14).
- **Players & equipment:** Pairs; 2 pairs per court on half-courts.
- **Setup:** One player at the net and one at the baseline of the same half-court.
- **Steps:** The baseliner drop-hits a lob; the net player smashes it back to the baseliner, who traps or stops it and repeats. After 6 successful overheads, switch.
- **Coaching cues:** "Both arms up and turn shoulders at the same time", *(added)* "Point at the ball", "Hit it at full reach".
- **Progression:** Lob one short, then one deep; play a cooperative rally and keep the ball going. **Regression:** The baseliner moves to the **60-foot baseline** (closer, orange-court length).
- **Source:** [USTA Yellow, "Overhead Warm-Up" (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Yellow.pdf)
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=msN27A6QiAk — "Improve your tennis game: Overhead drill" — United States Tennis Association (USTA)

**O2. Shadow Smash Rotation (coach-fed lobs)**
- **Ages / stage:** Orange 8-10 to Senior (group lessons)
- **Level:** Beginner to Intermediate
- **Skill area:** Overhead
- **Works on / fixes:** Technique through repetition and watching others; control over power. Fixes swinging too early (F16) and no turn (F15).
- **Players & equipment:** 4+ players and a coach feeder; basket.
- **Setup:** Two hitters at the net (*added:* one in each service box, about 2 m off the net). Two "shadow" players stand behind or beside them and mirror them. Others wait in the alley. The coach feeds from the opposite side *(added: from the baseline center)*.
- **Steps:** The coach lobs to hitter 1, who smashes; hitter 2 and both shadows mirror the movement. Then a lob to hitter 2. Each hitter smashes twice, then goes to the end of the line; the shadow becomes the hitter and a waiting player becomes the shadow.
- **Coaching cues:** "Smashes should be hit cross-court, away from teacher"; control and movement over power.
- **Progression:** Related Sportplan drills: "Run and Smash", "Approach and Smash", "Touch and Smash". **Regression:** *(added)* Lower, shorter lobs; let the ball bounce first (bounce overhead).
- **Source:** [Sportplan, Overheads](https://www.sportplan.net/drills/Tennis/Smash-Drills/Overheads-t400111.jsp)
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NOMh7eK5HY4 — "Tennis Overhead Smash Technique - How To Never Miss An Overhead Again" — PlayYourCourt.com

**O3. Smash and Touch the Net (10-in-a-row)**
- **Ages / stage:** Yellow juniors 11-14, Teens, Adult intermediate/advanced
- **Level:** Intermediate
- **Skill area:** Overhead (footwork, recovery)
- **Works on / fixes:** Moving back with a turn, then recovering forward. Fixes late feet and letting the ball drop (F14).
- **Players & equipment:** 1-3 players per coach; basket.
- **Setup:** Player at the net *(added: in the middle of a service box)*; the coach feeds lobs from the opposite baseline.
- **Steps:** The coach feeds alternating lobs. After each overhead the player runs up to touch the net, then moves back for the next. Count successful overheads to 10.
- **Coaching cues:** *(added)* "Turn first, then move back", "Side-shuffle or crossover", "Point at the ball".
- **Progression:** *(added)* Deeper lobs that require a scissor kick (O5); a target cone. **Regression:** *(added)* Short lobs; allow a bounce.
- **Source:** [CoachTube, Best Tennis Drills for Coaches – "Over Overhead" (search snippet; not fetched)](https://coachtube.com/course_lesson/best-tennis-drills-for-coaches/over-overhead/19915772)
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yBtXMU7hpwY — "TENNIS OVERHEAD SMASH | Footwork For The Tennis Overhead" — Tennis Evolution

**O4. Three Ball Throw (move back and explode up)**
- **Ages / stage:** Orange 8-10 *(added)*, Green, Yellow juniors 11-14 (Yellow 2)
- **Level:** Beginner
- **Skill area:** Overhead (footwork) and serve (leg drive)
- **Works on / fixes:** Drop-step or crossover back, loading the back leg, driving up while throwing. Fixes no leg drive (F6) and poor overhead footwork (F14, F15).
- **Players & equipment:** Pairs on opposite sides of the net, up to 3 pairs per court; 3 balls per player.
- **Setup:** On one side, players start at the net (one at each sideline and one in the middle). On the same side, each player has 3 balls in a straight line behind them: middle of the service box, on the service line, and on the baseline.
- **Steps:** Starting at the net, turn, jog to the first ball, bend, grab it and throw it high over the net in one motion. Turn back, jog to the second ball, and so on. The partners catch all three, place them in the same spots, and switch roles.
- **Coaching cues:** "Drop-step/crossover", "Load on back leg", "Drive up with legs as you throw".
- **Progression:** *(added)* Throw at a target zone; add a shadow overhead swing. **Regression:** "Pick up ball, pause, then throw."
- **Source:** [USTA Yellow, "Three Ball Throw" (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Yellow.pdf)
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ub2tLbOZvFg — "Overhead Smash Tennis | Footwork (Step by Step) 2018" — CLUTCH Tennis

**O5. Scissor-Kick Overhead Drill**
- **Ages / stage:** Teens 14-18, Adult advanced *(added: not for seniors or beginners)*
- **Level:** Advanced
- **Skill area:** Overhead (scissor kick vs deep lobs)
- **Works on / fixes:** Attacking deep lobs: jump off the back leg, legs switch in the air, land on the front leg. Fixes letting deep lobs drop and backpedalling (F14).
- **Players & equipment:** Player and feeder; basket.
- **Setup:** *(added)* Player starts at the net in the center; feeder at the opposite baseline lobs deep (landing around the hitter's service line or deeper).
- **Steps:** *(added, from the drill format described in the source)* Feed a deep lob; the player turns, crossover-steps back, jumps off the back leg and scissors the legs while hitting, then recovers forward to the net for the next lob ("grinder" repeats, as a cardio drill).
- **Coaching cues:** *(added)* "Turn and point", "Push off the back leg", "Legs switch in the air", "Land on the front leg".
- **Progression:** *(added)* Alternate deep and short lobs; finish to a target. **Regression:** *(added)* Shadow scissor kicks with no ball; let deep lobs bounce.
- **Source:** [YouTube: "Tennis Scissors Kick Overhead Drill" — Topspin Tennis (search description: "Grinder Overhead Scissors Kick Drill… a cardio drill")](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h2RPMEbPvoU)

**O6. Lob and Play (overhead in doubles context)**
- **Ages / stage:** Yellow juniors 11-14 (Yellow 2), Teens, Adults (doubles)
- **Level:** Intermediate
- **Skill area:** Overhead (game) and return (lob return)
- **Works on / fixes:** Handling lobs, communication, finishing overheads in live play (F14, F15, F16).
- **Players & equipment:** Two teams of 2-4 per court.
- **Setup:** Teams start in the one-up, one-back doubles position.
- **Steps:** Play no-ad doubles games where the returner **must lob** the return. Rotate players every two points and switch opponents every two games.
- **Coaching cues:** "Lob down the line over the net player", "Communicate on every lob".
- **Progression:** Lob down the line; the returner comes to the net if the lob is deep. **Regression:** Start the point with a drop-hit lob down the line.
- **Source:** [USTA Yellow, "Lob and Play" (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Yellow.pdf)

**O7. Copy Cat Overhead Shadow (net to baseline)**
- **Ages / stage:** Orange 8-10 *(added)*, Green 9-11 (Green 2), Yellow
- **Level:** Beginner
- **Skill area:** Overhead (footwork pattern)
- **Works on / fixes:** Moving back while turned sideways. Fixes backpedalling while facing the net and not turning (F15).
- **Players & equipment:** Up to 4 players per side; no balls.
- **Setup:** Players line up across the baseline.
- **Steps:** The coach demonstrates and players copy: three approaches (hop move) to the net, then **three shadow overheads moving from the net back to the baseline**.
- **Coaching cues:** *(added)* "Turn, point, shuffle back", "Swing up at full reach".
- **Progression:** Add a coach-tossed or fed ball (go to O1 or O2). **Regression:** *(added)* One overhead shadow only, standing still.
- **Source:** [USTA Green, Ch.05 "Copy Cat" progression 2 (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Green.pdf)

### Inferences
- Across USTA levels the return goals move from consistency and direction (orange) to split-step and recovery (yellow 1), then to taking it on the rise and moving in (green and yellow), then to coming in (green 2 and yellow). That gives a natural return curriculum by age.
- A **bounce overhead** is easiest to teach as a regression of O2 or O3: let a very high or deep lob bounce, then smash.

## 4. Injury and volume considerations: serve counts for juniors, shoulder care for adults/seniors

### Takeaway
I found **no federation-published "serve count" table for juniors**, nothing comparable to baseball pitch counts. The best-sourced guidance is: train fewer hours per week than your age; play fewer than 40 USTA singles matches a year; delay the kick serve until after 13; hit flat before spin; bend the knees more to reduce shoulder and elbow torque; do a 10-minute dynamic warm-up (Emory). Research shows elite players hit about 40 serves per set, and a volume-based return-to-serve program never raises volume and intensity together (Myers et al. 2016). For older adults, the abbreviated serve showed **no difference** in shoulder muscle activation from the traditional serve.

### Cited Findings
- "Consider training fewer hours in a week than your age… a 12-year-old player should train 12 hours or less per week"; juniors should play fewer than 40 USTA singles matches a year; elite players should not exceed 3 consecutive competitive weeks; up to two-thirds of tennis injuries are attributed to overuse — [Emory Healthcare Tennis Medicine, Wellness](https://www.emoryhealthcare.org/centers-programs/sports-medicine-center/tennis-medicine-program/wellness)
- "Delay kick serve until after age 13"; "Hit flat serves before adding spin"; more knee flexion reduces shoulder and elbow torque; "Tennis 10+" is a 10-minute, 10-step dynamic warm-up and cool-down — [Emory Healthcare Tennis Medicine, Wellness](https://www.emoryhealthcare.org/centers-programs/sports-medicine-center/tennis-medicine-program/wellness)
- Myers, Sciascia, Kibler & Uhl (2016), *Sports Health* 8(6): a typical set has about **40 serves** at a 3:1 first-to-second ratio. Professional men serve 63 more per match than junior men (best-of-5 vs best-of-3). Players can reach about **5,400 serves a season**. Upper-extremity injuries are 20-49% of tennis injuries. The interval program uses 21 steps and does not raise volume and intensity at the same time — [PMC5089352](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5089352)
- The abbreviated serve (racquet head up at the toss) showed **no significant differences** in shoulder muscle activation from the traditional serve in any phase — [University of Kentucky, "A comparison of muscle activations during traditional and abbreviated tennis serves"](https://scholars.uky.edu/en/publications/a-comparison-of-muscle-activations-during-traditional-and-abbrevi/)
- Feel Tennis warns against forcing pronation drills: "avoid forcing movements to prevent injury" — [Feel Tennis beginner lesson](https://www.feeltennis.net/serve-lesson-for-beginners/)
- Unverified search snippets, flagged as low confidence: "shoulder… volume increases above 25% from an average week" raises susceptibility; overuse injury rate in the dominant shoulder of 8.2 per 1,000 match hours (15.9% of overuse injuries) in high-performance juniors — snippets from [SportsEdTV blog](https://sportsedtv.com/blog/Injury-Proof-the-Shoulder-tennis) and [Frontiers in Sports and Active Living (2022)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2022.798255/epub). Neither page was opened, so the attribution of each figure is uncertain.
- Verified warm-up and shoulder videos: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3VqhWLbmxi8 — "Tennis Warm Up: Shoulders & Upper Body" — intosport; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y4e5F2RQvaI — "Shoulder Warm-Up to Reduce Injury  [5 Simple Exercises]" — Next Level Tennis; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DZP1RF7uyIs — "Shoulder Exercises for Tennis Players" — Orthopedic One
- Verified senior serve videos: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JqT_5pSYE7c — "Senior Tennis Serve" — Joseph MacDonald; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FMKy-ZbATYw — "Tennis Serve -- Key Points Especially for Senior players" — Col Nehru (No Frills Academy)

### Inferences
- Practical volume rule from these sources: count serves in practice and keep weekly serve volume steady instead of spiking it. Never raise both the number of serves and their intensity (kick, max-pace) in the same week (taken from the Myers 2016 interval logic). Juniors under 13 should keep second serves to slice or flat-with-spin, not kick (Emory).
- For seniors, an abbreviated or simplified take-back looks no worse for shoulder muscle load, and slice second serves (S14) reduce the lumbar extension a kick needs. That second point is my inference; no study was found on it.
- The leg-drive drills (S18, O4) and the knee-flexion cue double as **shoulder protection**, per Emory's knee-flexion statement.

## 5. Common serve, return and overhead faults, with fixes and corrective drills

### Takeaway
Most serve faults trace back to **grip (forehand/"frying pan")**, **facing the court too early**, **toss placement** or **missing leg drive**. Feel Tennis documents four causes of the waiter's tray. Return faults are mostly about **timing (split-step)** and **backswing size**. Overhead faults come from **late turning and footwork**. Every fault below links to drills in sections 1-3.

### Cited Findings
- Waiter's tray causes: (1) mental uncertainty about making contact, (2) not trusting pronation, (3) a subconscious fear of hitting your own head, worse with poor shoulder turn, (4) forehand-grip muscle memory — [Feel Tennis](https://www.feeltennis.net/correcting-the-waiters-serve/)
- Foot fault, ITF Rule 18 (via search snippet; rules PDF not opened): during the service motion the server shall not change position by walking or running, touch the baseline or court with either foot, touch outside the imaginary extension of the sideline, or touch the imaginary extension of the centre mark — [Tennis Companion, "What is a fault in tennis"](https://tenniscompanion.org/what-is-a-fault-in-tennis/); [USTA "What's the call: can the server cross the center line?"](https://www.usta.com/en/home/improve/tennis-rules/national/what-s-the-call--can-the-server-cross-the-center-line-.html)
- "Land on lead foot" ends the thrust-launch serve — [Human Kinetics](https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/thrust-launch-serve-technique)
- Overhead: "Move both arms up and turn shoulder simultaneously" — [USTA Yellow (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Yellow.pdf)
- Return: "Use compact backswing when taking ball on the rise"; "return and split-step with proper recovery" — [USTA Yellow (PDF)](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Yellow.pdf)

#### Fault catalogue

**F1. Frying-pan / forehand grip on the serve**
- **What it looks like / ball result:** The palm sits behind the handle and the strings face the target from the start. Serves are flat and pushed with little spin; it is hard to get power or spin, and second serves "pat".
- **Likely cause:** Learning the serve with the forehand grip; muscle memory persists after switching ([Feel Tennis](https://www.feeltennis.net/correcting-the-waiters-serve/)).
- **Fix cues:** "Continental: shake hands with the edge", "Racket parallel to your body" ([Feel Tennis beginner](https://www.feeltennis.net/serve-lesson-for-beginners/)), "Expect 3-6 months to feel comfortable".
- **Corrective drills:** S10 Continental Edge Tap → S11 Swing on Edge → S13 Serve With the Edge → S14 Slice to Ad.
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kcxwqOJZV9g — "How To Switch From Forehand To Continental Grip On The Serve | Tennis Serve Lesson" — Performance-Plus Tennis

**F2. Waiter's tray (racquet face open in the backswing / trophy)**
- **What it looks like / ball result:** In the backswing the strings face the sky like a waiter carrying a tray. The ball is pushed, short and spinless; there is little power, and the player often faces the net early.
- **Likely cause:** The four Feel Tennis causes above, especially facing the court too early and forehand-grip memory.
- **Fix cues:** "Lead with the edge", "Stay sideways longer", "Strings at the last moment" ("change your mind late").
- **Corrective drills:** S11, S13 (edge serve, then fence serve), S14, S3 (Quarterback "tip points back").
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bc3VkS8tAxY — "Transform Your Tennis Serve Technique - Fix The Waiter's Tray with these simple steps" — Meike Babel Tennis. Also https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=evv2WkBOAvE — "Tennis SERVE -  Correcting The Waiter's Tray" — Feel Tennis Instruction; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O9zc6hXfPR8 — "How To Fix Your Tennis Waiter Tray Serve In Tennis (With Just 2 Fingers)" — Total Tennis Domination; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EMJKMuUxTQU — "Fix The Waiter's Tray Tennis Serve With One Simple Trick" — Meike Babel Tennis

**F3. Inconsistent toss**
- **What it looks like / ball result:** The toss lands in a different place each time and the player chases it. Results vary between net, long and mishits; often there are many catch-and-retoss moments.
- **Likely cause:** Releasing the ball low or flicking the wrist; the toss arm bending or dropping early; the toss height changing *(cause list is coaching consensus from the toss videos below, not a federation text)*.
- **Fix cues:** "Place the toss in the same location every time" ([Human Kinetics](https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/thrust-launch-serve-technique)), "Keep lifting", *(added)* "Straight arm, release high".
- **Corrective drills:** S9 Toss Target, S5 Two-Ball Toss-and-Throw, S7 Serve in Two Parts.
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UJ3xNfw7G3Y — "Tame Your Serve Toss With The "Keep Lifting" Drill" — Feel Tennis Instruction. Also https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nmDO2mdNyZU — "Tennis Serve Toss Tips and Drills" — Tom Avery Tennis - CTW Academy; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XO5W7vCf3hc — "Improve your tennis serve with these 3 drills for a consistent ball toss" — Meike Babel Tennis; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HsnXfnsy3C8 — "5 Tips To Fix Your Serve Toss..." — Total Tennis Domination; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ta8J7tHZXkw — "Tennis Serve Toss and Follow-Through Drills With Harsh Mankad " — Tenicity

**F4. Toss too far behind or too far in front (or too far left/right)**
- **What it looks like / ball result:** Too far behind: the back arches, the ball goes long or is shanked, and it puts strain on the back. Too far in front: the player falls forward, the ball is hit down into the net, and it is hard to hit up. *(Ball results are coaching consensus; no federation text was found.)*
- **Likely cause:** A toss arm path that is not aligned with the target (for example swinging across the body), or releasing too early or late. For kick serves, players often confuse the kick toss with the flat or slice toss.
- **Fix cues:** *(added)* "Toss in front and to the hitting side for flat or slice", "Let it drop: it should land just inside the baseline, in front of the front foot", "For kick, toss more over the head".
- **Corrective drills:** S9 Toss Target (with a target placed per serve type), S7, S16 (kick toss).
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iYpl8PbxAWc — "Fix Your Toss With This Simple Tip (Tennis Serve Technique)" — 2MinuteTennis. Also https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TkAj6MmwxHw — "Tennis Kick Serve - Kick Serve Step 1 - Toss" — Fuzzy Yellow Balls

**F5. Tossing arm drops early / no "pointing" tossing arm**
- **What it looks like / ball result:** The tossing arm falls before the hitting arm rises. The shoulders open early, the player loses height and power, and the ball often goes into the net.
- **Likely cause:** Rushing; not coordinating the two arms ("toss with one, throw with the other").
- **Fix cues:** "Keep the tossing arm up longer", "Toss arm ahead of hitting arm" (USTA Drag Serve "Mission").
- **Corrective drills:** S5, S17 Drag Serve, S3 Quarterback (front arm parallel).
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IHcHQQzDKLU — "How To Keep Your Tossing Arm Up Longer (Tennis Serve Drill)" — 2MinuteTennis. Also https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G5S6lMBwzXU — "Tossing Arm Serve Drill To Improve Your Technique" — Tennis Evolution

**F6. No knee bend / no leg drive**
- **What it looks like / ball result:** Straight legs and all arm. There is little pace, and the shoulder and elbow carry the load.
- **Likely cause:** No load phase; feet static; often fear of losing balance.
- **Fix cues:** "Bend the knees", "Launch up and out", "Drive up with legs as you throw". Emory: more knee flexion **reduces shoulder and elbow torque** ([Emory](https://www.emoryhealthcare.org/centers-programs/sports-medicine-center/tennis-medicine-program/wellness)).
- **Corrective drills:** S18 Thrust Launch, O4 Three Ball Throw, S2 Three-Level Throw, S8 Three-Level Serve.
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_XO7IEHDkl0 — "Serve- Hip Flexor Snap Towel Drill" — Patrick Dougherty *(related; hip action)*; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=upcyHOegDLI — "The Tennis Serve Simplified - 6 Steps To An Advanced Serve" — PlayYourCourt.com

**F7. Dropping the racquet head early / no "racquet drop"**
- **What it looks like / ball result:** The racquet falls behind the back before the toss peaks (early drop), or never drops at all (a stiff, pushing arm). Rhythm is off, racquet-head speed is low, and contact is often late or low.
- **Likely cause:** Swinging before the toss reaches its peak; a tense arm.
- **Fix cues:** "Toss first; arm moves at the peak", "Relaxed arm", "Palm down like a throw" (USTA Drag Serve).
- **Corrective drills:** S17 Drag Serve, S7 Serve in Two Parts, S3 Quarterback.
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uYguiaDTgH8 — "Tennis Serve: Racquet Drop (HUGE Mistake to Avoid & Drills To-Do)" — Tennis Evolution. Also https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S7t0gV1Hu10 — "Tennis Serve Tip: Improve Your Racket Drop" — Online Tennis Instruction

**F8. Pushing the ball / no pronation**
- **What it looks like / ball result:** The strings meet the ball flat from early on and the arm "pushes" toward the target. Serves are slow and short, there is no snap, and the finish lands on the wrong side.
- **Likely cause:** Not trusting pronation; a forehand grip (F1); facing the net too early ([Feel Tennis](https://www.feeltennis.net/correcting-the-waiters-serve/)).
- **Fix cues:** "Lead with edge, pronate", "Finish on your dominant side first (ground smack), then across", "Accelerate naturally and pronate your forearm" ([Feel Tennis pronation](https://www.feeltennis.net/serve-pronation/)).
- **Corrective drills:** S12 Ground-Smack Pronation, S14, S6 Kneeling Serve, S1/S3 throwing.
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=urCqh8-W5EM — "TENNIS SERVE LESSON: How To Master Pronation On Your Serve (Pronation Drills)" — Tennis Evolution. Also https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CJlW6tOT4Ho — "5 Drills For EASY Serve Pronation! Tennis Serve Lesson" — Performance-Plus Tennis

**F9. Landing on the wrong foot / falling sideways**
- **What it looks like / ball result:** The player lands on the back foot or falls toward the side fence. The serve sprays sideways, recovery is slow, and the server is out of position for the return.
- **Likely cause:** The toss is too far to the side or behind (F4); no forward weight transfer; legs drive sideways instead of up and in.
- **Fix cues:** "Land on lead foot" ([Human Kinetics](https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/thrust-launch-serve-technique)), *(added)* "Toss into the court", "Balance and hold the finish for 2 seconds".
- **Corrective drills:** S18, S9 (toss into the court), S5 (balance in the two-ball throw).
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n274VekfB1U — "Serve Drill: Toss and Balance | Tennis" — SIKANA English

**F10. Foot faults**
- **What it looks like / ball result:** Stepping on or over the baseline before contact, walking or running into the serve, or crossing the centre-mark or sideline extension. It is a fault and gets called in officiated play.
- **Likely cause:** A front foot that slides or creeps during the toss or leg drive; standing too close to the line; the back foot stepping through early (pinpoint stance).
- **Fix cues:** Rule 18 basics (no walking or running; don't touch the baseline, the sideline extension or the centre-mark extension), *(added)* "Start a ball-width behind the line", "Front foot stays planted until contact".
- **Corrective drills:** *(added)* S18 done with a throw-down line placed just behind the baseline; S15 with a "foot-fault = lost zone" rule.
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vBnjtbNGQno — "How To Stop Foot Faulting on the Serve | Tennis Technique" — Intuitive Tennis

**F11. Double faults under pressure**
- **What it looks like / ball result:** Second serves into the net or long at key scores; the player slows the arm down ("guides" it).
- **Likely cause:** No reliable spin second serve; no routine; decelerating.
- **Fix cues:** "Same ritual every serve", "Use spin to add control" (USTA Serve Index); "Racquet-head speed, hit up and out" (Three-Level Serve).
- **Corrective drills:** S15 Serve Index (with ritual), S19 Team Defender (one serve), S8, S16 (kick, 13+), S14 (slice).
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HCrpSKYvLoo — "Kick Serve Drills THAT WORK!!! | INSANE KICK" — Supreme Tennis. Also https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x6toBHiG8Z0 — "Kick Serve Secret (Revealed) | TENNIS SERVE LESSON" — Tennis Evolution

**F12. Returner too passive / standing still**
- **What it looks like / ball result:** No split-step and flat feet. Late, weak or short returns; the returner gets aced or jammed.
- **Likely cause:** Split-step mistimed (too early or late) or missing; standing too deep.
- **Fix cues:** "Split-step as the server makes contact", *(added)* "Land your split as you read the serve"; "Return and split-step with recovery" (USTA First Four); "Take return early" (USTA Two-on-One).
- **Corrective drills:** R2 First Four, R3 Serve Pyramids, R5 Up and Down the River, R6 Step Up, R7 Two-on-One.
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Dl_vM5TnDY4 — "Tennis Lesson - Return of Serve Video #3 - Ready Hop/Split Step - Insight Tennis" — Insight Tennis. Also https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XR83yXLla8w — "Tennis Split Step Timing, Execution And Drills" — Feel Tennis Instruction; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z8CNYjf-Uyk — "THE SPLIT STEP: ESSENTIAL DRILLS FOR ADVANCED CONDITIONING AND MOVEMENT I TENNIS ON DEMAND" — tennisondemand

**F13. Big backswing on the return**
- **What it looks like / ball result:** A full groundstroke backswing against a fast serve. The return is late, framed or sprayed wide.
- **Likely cause:** Treating the return like a rally ball; late unit turn.
- **Fix cues:** "Compact backswing" (USTA), *(added)* "Shoulder turn, not arm swing", "Block and use the server's pace".
- **Corrective drills:** R4 Return the Cannon, R6 Pinch + Step Up.
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3TxZTZEEg44 — "TENNIS RETURN | Block Your Return Of Serve With This Grip" — Tennis Evolution. Also https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_pS0otk2560 — "Top 3 Drills For Perfect Return of Serve in Tennis" — Top Tennis Training - Pro Tennis Lessons

**F14. Overhead: letting the ball drop too low**
- **What it looks like / ball result:** Contact at head height or below. The smash goes into the net or becomes a weak push, and is often framed.
- **Likely cause:** Late movement back; not getting behind the ball; standing still.
- **Fix cues:** *(added)* "Turn and move early", "Hit it at full reach, like a serve", "Get behind the ball"; *(added)* on very high lobs, let it bounce.
- **Corrective drills:** O3 Smash and Touch the Net, O5 Scissor Kick, O1, O4.
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NOMh7eK5HY4 — "Tennis Overhead Smash Technique - How To Never Miss An Overhead Again" — PlayYourCourt.com

**F15. Overhead: no turn / no pointing (tracking) hand**
- **What it looks like / ball result:** Square to the net, backpedalling, hitting arm only. Timing and direction are poor and the player risks falling backward.
- **Likely cause:** Footwork not trained; arms and shoulders not coordinated.
- **Fix cues:** "Move both arms up and turn shoulder simultaneously" ([USTA Yellow](https://preview.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Practice-Plan-Yellow.pdf)), *(added)* "Point at the ball", "Shuffle or crossover back, never backpedal".
- **Corrective drills:** O7 Copy Cat shadow, O1 Overhead Warm-Up, O4.
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yBtXMU7hpwY — "TENNIS OVERHEAD SMASH | Footwork For The Tennis Overhead" — Tennis Evolution. Also https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ub2tLbOZvFg — "Overhead Smash Tennis | Footwork (Step by Step) 2018" — CLUTCH Tennis

**F16. Overhead: swinging too early**
- **What it looks like / ball result:** The racquet swings before the ball arrives, so contact is late or low on the frame. Mishits and balls into the net.
- **Likely cause:** The racquet starts already behind the back with no "trophy" pause; no tracking hand to time the ball.
- **Fix cues:** *(added)* "Racquet up early, then wait", "Track with the non-hitting hand", "Swing when the ball reaches the top of your reach".
- **Corrective drills:** O2 Shadow Smash (control over power), O1 (short and deep lobs), O6.
- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=msN27A6QiAk — "Improve your tennis game: Overhead drill" — United States Tennis Association (USTA)

### Inferences
- Many faults share one root: **a forehand grip combined with facing the court early** produces F1, F2 and F8 together. The grip-and-edge chain (S10 → S11 → S13 → S14) is the main fix for all three.
- **Leg drive (F6) and landing (F9)** connect to injury guidance: Emory links more knee flexion to lower shoulder and elbow torque. Coaches can present S18 and O4 as both power and health drills.
- Return faults (F12, F13) are best fixed through **games** (USTA uses one-serve and returner-inside-baseline constraints) rather than isolated technique drills.

### Gaps
- Fault "ball result" and "likely cause" lines for F3, F4, F9 and F14-F16 rely on coaching consensus and video titles more than on a federation or peer-reviewed text. Those parts are marked as added or consensus.
- I found no peer-reviewed source on how common each fault is by level (for example the percentage of 3.0 players who use a forehand grip). The claim that the waiter's tray is the "#1" recreational serve mistake appeared only in a search-result summary of YouTube descriptions, so it is excluded as unverified.
- The ITF Rules of Tennis text (Rule 18) was taken from secondary pages (Tennis Companion and a USTA "What's the call" page via search snippet). The ITF rules PDF itself was not opened.
