## Q1. What are the signature games in USTA Net Generation, ITF Play and Stay / Tennis10s, Tennis Australia Hot Shots, LTA Youth (and Tennis Canada Progressive Tennis), and what does a ready-to-run library look like?

### Takeaway
The richest openly downloadable, fully specified activity libraries are USTA Net Generation's Red and Orange Community Practice & Play Plans and Tennis Canada's Learn to Play curriculum; together with LTA Youth Go! and Judy Murray's Education Scotland resource they supply 50 named, ready-to-run games below (tots → red → orange), each with setup, rules, progressions and cues. Tennis Australia Hot Shots and ITF Tennis10s lesson documents could not be opened (403 / not retrieved), so their contribution here is limited to programme structure.

### Cited Findings

#### A. Programme context: ball stages, courts, racquets (for drawing court diagrams)
- ITF 10-and-under competition: Red court is 36-42 ft (10.97-12.80 m) long × 14-20 ft (4.27-6.10 m) wide, net 31.5-33 in (0.800-0.838 m); Orange court 58-60 ft (17.68-18.29 m) × 20-27 ft (6.10-8.23 m), net 31.5-36 in. Red (Stage 3) ball recommended for players up to 8 with racquet up to 23 in; Orange (Stage 2) for 8-10 with 23-25 in racquet; Green (Stage 1) for advanced 9-10 with 25-26 in. — [ITF-Rules](https://itftennis.com/media/7221/2025-rules-of-tennis-english.pdf)
- The red ball (foam or felt) is described as 75% slower than a regular yellow ball and aimed at ages 5-8 (2012 ITF rule-change coverage). — [Tennis Australia news, 3 Jan 2012](https://www.tennis.com.au/news/2012/01/03/itf-makes-changes-to-rules-of-tennis-for-10u-comp) (via search snippet)
- Tennis Canada Progressive Tennis: Red (6+) court 36 ft (11 m) × 18 ft (5.5 m), net 80 cm (31.5 in), 19 or 21 in racquet, oversize foam or low-compression felt ball; Orange (8+) court 60 ft (18 m) × 21 ft singles / 27 ft doubles, net 80 cm, 23 or 25 in racquet. Red courts are officially set up sideways across a regulation court (up to 6 per court); a rope tied from the regulation net to the fence and propped on chairs can serve as a red net. — [TC-LTP](https://assets.tenniscanada.com/production/PDFs/LearnToPlay_ENG.pdf)
- USTA Net Generation Red equipment: playground balls, red felt and foam balls (two of each per player), racquets 23 in and smaller, poly-spots/throw-down lines (TDLs), one 9-in cone per two players, 18-ft pop-up nets for 36-ft courts (or barrier tape/rope/pennants strung between fences, carts, chairs), clothespins (200 per class) for scorekeeping, two packs of playing cards. Community 1 can run on any flat surface (gym, parking lot); Community 2-3 on a tennis court. — [NG-Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Red.pdf)
- Net Generation Red levels: Community 1 = 5-8-year-old first-time beginners; Community 2 = completed C1 or 7-9-year-old beginners; Community 3 = completed C2 or some experience, target age 8-10. — [NG-Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Red.pdf)
- Net Generation Orange: 25-in racquets, orange balls; first two Orange 1 chapters use a 42-ft court (regulation service line used as baseline), the rest use the 60-ft orange court (9 ft inside the regulation baseline, 3 ft inside the singles sidelines; 21 ft wide singles, 27 ft doubles). NOTE: the Community Orange manual's recommended age is 11-14-year-old beginners or graduates of Red Community 3 — i.e., it is aimed older than the 8-10 orange band in this brief. — [NG-Orange](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Orange.pdf)
- LTA Youth Red: ages 5-8, learn to serve, rally and score; looks more like traditional tennis (overarm serves, rallies, volleys); relaxed competition introduced; racquets typically 54-60 cm (21-23 in). Task Cards per sub-stage earn a roadmap sticker and certificate (Rally Awards). — [LTA Youth Ages (club page)](https://clubspark.lta.org.uk/kinetictennis/Coaching/LTAYouthAges); [LTA Youth Red (club page)](https://clubspark.lta.org.uk/TriplettsCommunityTennisCentre/Coaching/LTAYouthRed); [Rally Awards Stage 2](https://clubspark.lta.org.uk/CarlDouglasCoaching/RALLYAWARDS/STAGE2) (search snippets; club sites, not LTA HQ)
- Tennis Australia Hot Shots: programme for ages 3-12 using modified equipment; Red stage uses 19-23 in racquets and game-based activities; Hot Shots Red Awards target ages 4-8 with regular rewards, and within each of four levels players are tested on four skills: Rally, Serve, Come to the net, Score and Compete. — [Hot Shots coach page](https://play.tennis.com.au/DPTennisCoaching/Coaching/Junior/HotShotsCoach); [Tennis Australia – About Hot Shots](https://www.tennis.com.au/play/kids-tennis/hot-shots/about-hot-shots); [Hot Shots Red Ball Rally Awards](https://morningsidetennis.com.au/?p=2900) (search snippets; the Morningside page returned 404 when fetched)
- LTA Tennis at Home series names: High 5 Rallies, Floor Tennis, Switch Catch, Double Trouble; "Tennisables" character challenges (Ace, Bounce, Dash, Slice, Smash, Spin); downloadable activity cards for ages 4-7 and 8-11. — [LTA Tennis at Home](https://www.lta.org.uk/play-compete/lta-youth/tennis-at-home/)
- Tots (3-5) club programmes under LTA: 30-minute sessions; sponge or mini red balls; 17 or 19 in racquets; activities built on balance, coordination, agility, movement, racquet-and-ball skills; balloons, beach balls, footballs, beanbags used alongside racquets. — [Kinetic Tennis Tots (LTA ClubSpark)](https://clubspark.lta.org.uk/KineticTennisCoaching/Coaching/Junior/TotsTennis) (search snippet)

#### B. The library (50 ready-to-run games/drills)

Level tags (first-timer / developing / ready to move up) are my mapping of the source level: NG Community 1 / Tennis Canada Red 1 → first-timer; NG Community 2 / Red 2 → developing; NG Community 3 and Orange → ready to move up (see Inferences). Distances are from the source unless marked **[coach suggestion]**. Cues are paraphrased from the source unless marked **[added]** (common coaching language I supplied; not in the cited source).

##### 1. Balloon Tennis
- **Ages / stage:** Tots 3-5 (see balloon-safety gap) → early Red
- **Level:** First-timer
- **Skill area:** Coordination (tracking, sending & receiving), first rally
- **Works on / fixes:** Gives every child success because balloons move slowly; tracking at all heights, both sides of body; rallying with a partner before a ball is introduced.
- **Players & equipment:** 1-2+ players; round balloons inflated medium-to-low so they float a little; a 3-5 m string/ribbon; tape; paper plates + paint-stir sticks taped on as "racquets" (or real racquets / hands).
- **Setup:** String hung horizontally about 1 m off the floor between two chairs/shelves = net. One child each side, roughly 1-2 m from the string **[coach suggestion]**.
- **Steps / rules:** Hit the balloon back and forth over the string. If it touches the floor on your side, the other player scores. First to 5, then restart. Racquets may not cross over the net. Multiple touches on your own side are allowed.
- **Coaching cues:** Watch the balloon all the way onto the plate; "pancake hand" (flat palm under the balloon) **[added]**.
- **Progression:** Solo keep-ups counting taps → partner rallies → light tennis balls for older children → JM: use left and right hands, forehand/backhand side of hand, then racquet.
- **Regression:** Hands only; catch-and-throw the balloon instead of hitting.
- **Source:** Net Generation at-home balloon tennis instructions as summarised in search results from [USTA Florida](https://www.ustaflorida.com/tennis-tip-thursday-balloon-tennis-edgies-fast-feet/) and [Active for Life](https://activeforlife.com/activity/balloon-tennis/) (both pages returned 403 to direct fetch); [JM](https://education.gov.scot/media/tunlek1v/jm-tennis-resource-primary.pdf); [Premier Education](https://www.premier-education.com/news/tennis-games-for-kids/) (ages 4-7 variant, count taps)
- **Video (Exact):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8i5I3z0lgz0 — "Net Generation: Balloon Tennis Pt. 1" — United States Tennis Association (USTA)

##### 2. Beanbag Racket Rally (Judy Murray progression)
- **Ages / stage:** Tots 3-5 → Red first-timers
- **Level:** First-timer
- **Skill area:** Coordination; racquet-head control; forehand/backhand shape
- **Works on / fixes:** Children who cannot yet control a racquet; beanbags don't roll away and are easier to trap than a ball (good for big groups).
- **Players & equipment:** Pairs; beanbags; one racquet each, later two racquets each.
- **Setup:** Pairs in small spaces facing each other, starting close (about 1-2 m) **[coach suggestion]**; hoops can mark target areas.
- **Steps / rules:** (1) Start with the hand: mimic the shape of the shot sending the beanbag to partner. (2) Add the racquet when the child is succeeding. (3) Paired beanbag exchanges with one beanbag, then add a second, then increase distance, working towards a "mini rally". (4) "Double racket" exercise (a racquet in each hand) mirrors forehand and backhand.
- **Coaching cues:** Start statically, then add movement; work both left and right sides (tennis is two-sided). "Shake hands with the racquet" for grip **[added]**.
- **Progression:** Second beanbag; more distance; switch to fluff ball, then ball on the floor, then bouncing ball.
- **Regression:** Hand only; partner closer; balance the beanbag on the strings and walk.
- **Source:** [JM](https://education.gov.scot/media/tunlek1v/jm-tennis-resource-primary.pdf)
- **Video (Related, same programme):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lYuzwTnQYUc — "Primary Racket Skills" — Education Scotland. Also related: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Istx1NxbRuc — "Stay and Play - Let's play Tennis Racket Bean Bag Toss with Stacy" — Portland Parks & Recreation

##### 3. Pizza Delivery
- **Ages / stage:** Red 5-8 (NG C1); easily run with tots
- **Level:** First-timer
- **Skill area:** Coordination / balance / movement (warm-up)
- **Works on / fixes:** Body awareness, keeping a flat palm (precursor to balancing the ball on strings), moving while controlling an object.
- **Players & equipment:** Whole group; one "pizza" per child (poly-spot, paper plate, or a ball on an open hand); chalk/TDLs for boundary.
- **Setup:** Defined area marked with chalk/TDLs; players spread out.
- **Steps / rules:** Carry the pizza flat in one palm around the area; walk fast, no running. Try to gently knock down other players' pizzas or chase them so they drop it. Dropped pizza → go to the sideline and cheer, or do five reps of a fun exercise (e.g., "I'm a Star" jumps, donkey kicks); coach decides re-entry.
- **Coaching cues:** Stay low, be aware, keep moving.
- **Progression:** Carry the "pizza" on the racquet strings (see #5); switch hands.
- **Regression:** No knocking — just walk the pizza through cones.
- **Source:** [NG-Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Red.pdf) (C1 Ch.2 Station 1; C2 Ch.5 warm-up)
- **Video:** None verified for this exact activity.

##### 4. Bungee Jump (and Bungee Flips)
- **Ages / stage:** Red 5-8 (NG C1-C3); tots with a large foam ball
- **Level:** First-timer → developing
- **Skill area:** Coordination (racquet control); forehand/backhand face awareness
- **Works on / fixes:** Watching the ball onto the strings, soft hands, steady head and wrist; introduces palm-up (forehand face) and knuckles-up (backhand face).
- **Players & equipment:** Individuals; racquet + red ball each.
- **Setup:** Each player in their own space (spread on both sides of the net if on court).
- **Steps / rules:** Palm up, ball in centre of strings; tilt the racquet so the ball rolls off; let it bounce; catch it back on the strings. Reposition with free hand if needed. *Bungee Flips:* catch each bounce on the opposite face (palm up, then knuckles up).
- **Coaching cues:** Pause with the ball on the strings and keep your head still; firm grip, slow and steady; use your knees to get down to the ball, keep the wrist still.
- **Progression:** Hold the ball on the strings for less time → knuckles-up → continuous tap-ups → do bungees while moving around the court.
- **Regression:** Free hand helps reposition; bigger ball.
- **Source:** [NG-Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Red.pdf) (C1 Ch.2, Ch.5; C2 Ch.3; C3 Ch.1-2)
- **Video:** None verified for this exact activity.

##### 5. Start Your Engines (with Traffic Lights / "Red Light, Green Light" calls)
- **Ages / stage:** Tots 3-5 / Red 5-8
- **Level:** First-timer
- **Skill area:** Coordination + movement; listening/stop-start control
- **Works on / fixes:** Balancing the ball on the strings while moving; stopping under control.
- **Players & equipment:** Individuals or teams; racquet + red ball each; chalk/TDLs for two "sidelines".
- **Setup:** Two lines marked (any width the group can cover — e.g., a red-court width of 18 ft **[coach suggestion]**).
- **Steps / rules (NG):** Do two bungee jumps at the start line, then walk to the opposite line and back with the ball balanced on the strings. Level 2: three then four bungees first. Level 3: continuous bungees line to line. Level 4: alternate palm up and knuckles up.
- **Traffic-lights overlay (generic PE game, tennis adaptation is mine):** Red = stop, amber = walk, green = run (or move faster); extra calls in some versions: roundabout (turn round once), motorway (sprint), speed bump (jump). Applying these calls while carrying the ball on the strings is my adaptation — not documented by a tennis federation in sources I could access.
- **Coaching cues:** Eyes on the ball; flat strings ("pizza tray") **[added]**.
- **Progression:** Faster calls; tap the ball up while moving.
- **Regression:** Carry a beanbag or poly-spot instead of a ball.
- **Source:** [NG-Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Red.pdf) (C1 Ch.2 Station 3); Traffic Lights rules from [Perform](https://www.perform.org.uk/child-development/drama-games/traffic-lights) and [Scouts – Red light green light](https://scouts.org.uk/activities/red-light-green-light) (search snippets; non-tennis sources)
- **Video:** None verified.

##### 6. Self-Toss ("Bounce-Catch")
- **Ages / stage:** Tots / Red (NG C1)
- **Level:** First-timer
- **Skill area:** Coordination / tracking
- **Works on / fixes:** Judging the bounce; two-hand then one-hand catching.
- **Players & equipment:** Individuals; one red ball each; chalk/TDLs boundary.
- **Setup:** Players spread out in a defined space.
- **Steps / rules:** Toss the ball with one hand, palm up, above head height; let it bounce; catch with both hands. Home version: toss to a wall, let it bounce, catch; low, medium, high tosses.
- **Coaching cues:** Palm up to the sky on the toss; "bounce — catch!" said aloud **[added]**.
- **Progression:** Toss higher → toss farther away → catch with one hand.
- **Regression:** Bigger (playground) ball; two bounces allowed.
- **Source:** [NG-Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Red.pdf) (C1 Ch.1 Station 1, Play at Home)
- **Video (Related):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lOpIXxHtwp4 — "20 Tennis exercises for groups of 3 4 year olds" — Tenniscoachblog

##### 7. Ice Cream Cones
- **Ages / stage:** Red 5-8 (NG C2); tots can do it
- **Level:** First-timer → developing
- **Skill area:** Coordination / tracking
- **Works on / fixes:** Waiting for the ball to drop and catching it as it falls (counters rushing in too close).
- **Players & equipment:** Individuals; red ball + upturned cone each.
- **Setup:** Spread around the court.
- **Steps / rules:** Toss, let it bounce, catch it in the upturned cone. Call out a different ice-cream flavour after each catch.
- **Coaching cues:** Bend your knees and catch it on the way down **[added]**.
- **Progression:** Alternate catching after the bounce and in the air; count "scoops" in a row.
- **Regression:** Two-hand catch without the cone; LTA Go also recommends "ice cream" cones as a catching aid.
- **Source:** [NG-Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Red.pdf) (C2 Ch.2); [LTA-Go](https://lta.org.uk/490853/siteassets/roles/activators/file/lta-youth-go---net-game-lesson-plans.pdf)
- **Video:** None verified.

##### 8. One Bounce, Two Bounce
- **Ages / stage:** Red 5-8 (NG C2-C3)
- **Level:** Developing
- **Skill area:** Tracking / receiving
- **Works on / fixes:** Reading the bounce; patience (not grabbing early).
- **Players & equipment:** Pairs; one red ball; a line, tape/rope, or object as a "net".
- **Setup:** Partners on opposite sides of the line about 8 ft apart.
- **Steps / rules:** Take turns tossing. The tosser calls "one" or "two" = number of bounces allowed before the catch.
- **Coaching cues:** Ready position before each toss **[added]**.
- **Progression:** Toss with non-dominant hand; one-hand catch with an orange ball; toss and trap with the racquet.
- **Regression:** Bigger ball; reduce distance.
- **Source:** [NG-Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Red.pdf) (C2 Ch.3, Ch.5; C3 Ch.4-5)
- **Video:** None verified.

##### 9. Tunnel Ball
- **Ages / stage:** Red 5-8 (NG C1-C3)
- **Level:** First-timer → developing
- **Skill area:** Movement / tracking
- **Works on / fixes:** Turning and chasing a moving ball; reacting to a call.
- **Players & equipment:** Pairs; one red ball; a sideline at each end.
- **Setup:** Roller with both feet on a sideline; partner about 5 ft away, feet shoulder-width apart, in ready position, back to the far sideline.
- **Steps / rules:** Roll the ball between the partner's legs at medium speed; partner turns, chases it down before it reaches the opposite sideline and tosses it back. Four successful, then swap.
- **Coaching cues:** Quick turn, quick feet **[added]**.
- **Progression:** Toss over partner's head to catch before the far sideline; tosser shouts 1, 2 or 3 = bounces allowed before catching.
- **Regression:** Slower roll; shorter distance to far line.
- **Source:** [NG-Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Red.pdf) (C1 Ch.4; C2 Ch.4, Ch.6; C3 Ch.1-2)
- **Video:** None verified.

##### 10. Ball Drop
- **Ages / stage:** Red 7-10 (NG C3); Orange
- **Level:** Ready to move up
- **Skill area:** Movement / reaction / tracking
- **Works on / fixes:** Reaction speed, first step, catching to the side (not in front of the face).
- **Players & equipment:** Pairs; two red or two orange balls (orange is easier to catch one-handed).
- **Setup:** Pairs about 4 ft apart. Dropper holds both balls at shoulder height, arms in a "V", palms down.
- **Steps / rules:** Drop one ball; partner catches it after the bounce with one hand — right hand on the right side, left hand on the left. Four each side, then switch roles.
- **Coaching cues:** Ready position, eyes on the hands **[added]**.
- **Progression:** Alternate left/right; dropper's arms straight out to the side; step back.
- **Regression:** Two-hand catch from the side with a red ball.
- **Source:** [NG-Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Red.pdf) (C3 Ch.2-3)
- **Video (Exact):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZORZOfQgS2A — "Net Generation: Ball Drop" — United States Tennis Association (USTA)

##### 11. Shake-Hands Toss
- **Ages / stage:** Red 7-10 (NG C3)
- **Level:** Ready to move up
- **Skill area:** Tracking / cooperative movement
- **Works on / fixes:** One-hand catching; moving the feet together to adjust to a bad toss.
- **Players & equipment:** Pairs; one orange ball.
- **Setup:** Partners shake hands and keep holding — their joined arms form a "net".
- **Steps / rules:** With the free hand, gently toss over the arm-net, let it bounce, catch one-handed; repeat. Move feet together to adjust. Four each, then switch hands.
- **Coaching cues:** Move together, then catch.
- **Progression:** Higher and wider tosses; catch in the air.
- **Regression:** Two bounces allowed.
- **Source:** [NG-Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Red.pdf) (C3 Ch.3-4)
- **Video:** None verified.

##### 12. Fire Ants
- **Ages / stage:** Red 5-10 (NG C2-C3); tots love it
- **Level:** First-timer
- **Skill area:** Movement (fast feet)
- **Works on / fixes:** Flat-footed, static players; ready position.
- **Players & equipment:** Whole group; no equipment.
- **Setup:** Spread around the court in ready position.
- **Steps / rules:** On "go", stomp as many imaginary fire ants as possible on the spot for a set time.
- **Coaching cues:** Stay on your toes; tiny fast steps **[added]**.
- **Progression:** Skip around the court and stomp when the coach shouts "fire ants!"; side-shuffle and stomp.
- **Regression:** Shorter bursts.
- **Source:** [NG-Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Red.pdf) (C2 Ch.1; C3 Ch.1, Ch.6)
- **Video:** None verified.

##### 13. Messy Bedroom / Clean Your Room
- **Ages / stage:** Tots (with soft balls) / Red / Orange
- **Level:** First-timer
- **Skill area:** Serve foundation (overarm throwing); team game
- **Works on / fixes:** Overarm throwing pattern for the serve; energy and team spirit.
- **Players & equipment:** Two teams of 3-5; bucket of red balls per side; net, pop-up net or barrier tape.
- **Setup:** Teams on opposite sides of a net/barrier tape (NG prefers balls on the side away from the fence). Coach tips a bucket of balls out on each side.
- **Steps / rules:** NG: throw balls overhand one at a time to the other side for ≤1 minute (adjust to ball count); afterwards each team collects and counts the balls it threw and tries to beat its team record. LTA Go variant: aim is to have the fewest balls on your side after 60 s; if an opponent catches your throw before it bounces, the thrower is out. Tennis Canada variant: each player starts with a ball; team keeping its end clearest wins.
- **Coaching cues:** Side-on, elbow up, throw high over the net **[added]**.
- **Progression:** Throw with non-dominant hand; must land in a service box/designated area.
- **Regression:** Two-hand throws; lower net/tape.
- **Source:** [NG-Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Red.pdf) (C1 Ch.3; C2 Ch.1; C3 Ch.3); [LTA-Go](https://lta.org.uk/490853/siteassets/roles/activators/file/lta-youth-go---net-game-lesson-plans.pdf); [TC-LTP](https://assets.tenniscanada.com/production/PDFs/LearnToPlay_ENG.pdf)
- **Video:** None verified for this exact game.

##### 14. Hits
- **Ages / stage:** Red 6-10 (NG C2-C3)
- **Level:** Developing
- **Skill area:** Sending / aiming (warm-up)
- **Works on / fixes:** Aiming at a moving target; rolling then throwing accuracy.
- **Players & equipment:** Teams of 4-6; basket of red balls per team; coach has two playground balls; tape/rope or no net.
- **Setup:** Teams spread on opposite service lines; coach at the side of the court at the centre.
- **Steps / rules:** Coach calls "hits!" and rolls a playground ball down the centre of the court; each player rolls a red ball trying to hit it. Teams total their hits; coach rolls a second ball. Repeat for a "team best".
- **Coaching cues:** Step and roll toward where the big ball is going **[added]**.
- **Progression:** Move back to the baseline; throw at the playground ball.
- **Regression:** Coach rolls slower; players closer.
- **Source:** [NG-Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Red.pdf) (C2 Ch.2; C3 Ch.2)
- **Video:** None verified.

##### 15. Walk the Dog (and Zig-Zag)
- **Ages / stage:** Red 5-8 (NG C1-C2); tots with a big ball
- **Level:** First-timer
- **Skill area:** Forehand and backhand (swing path on the ground)
- **Works on / fixes:** Racquet face control, contact to the side of the body, follow-through toward the target, early racquet preparation via footwork.
- **Players & equipment:** Individuals or pairs; racquet + red ball; poly-spots/paper plates/chalk targets.
- **Setup:** Players on a sideline; a spot 15-20 ft away (or on the opposite sideline). Zig-zag: four spots in a zig-zag between two sidelines; 3 players per pattern (next goes when previous reaches spot 2).
- **Steps / rules:** Roll the ball with the edge of the racquet to the spot, stop it on the spot, roll it back and stop it again. Partner version: send it back along the ground to your partner, who stops it and repeats. Zig-zag: forehand to spot 1, backhand to spot 2, alternating.
- **Coaching cues:** Racquet edge touches the ground, strings stay on the ball; ball to the side of your body, follow through to the target; forehand = palm forward, backhand = knuckles forward; use your feet to get the racquet in position.
- **Progression:** Backhand only → zig-zag both sides → bungees on each spot → drop-hit back to partner.
- **Regression:** Big ball; smaller racquet; both hands on the racquet.
- **Source:** [NG-Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Red.pdf) (C1 Ch.1, 3-6; C2 Ch.2)

##### 16. Keep It Moving / Floor Rally in the Alley (cooperative rolling rally)
- **Ages / stage:** Red 5-10
- **Level:** First-timer → developing
- **Skill area:** Rally (forehand/backhand along the ground)
- **Works on / fixes:** Over-hitting; facing the net; contact in front; first continuous rally.
- **Players & equipment:** Pairs; racquets; one red ball; chalk/TDLs.
- **Setup:** NG: 2-3 pairs per side of the net, partners facing each other with a court line as the "net"; players mark their own boundaries. Tennis Canada: pair works in the doubles alley, each player's feet on a sideline (singles or doubles line).

##### 17. Touchdown

##### 19. Jacks (Onesies to Fivesies) and Splat
- **Ages / stage:** Red 5-9 (NG C1-C2)

##### 20. Lobster Trap / Half-Pound Lobster Trap

##### 22. Survivor (the Island)
- **Ages / stage:** Red 5-10 (NG C1-C2)
- **Level:** First-timer → developing
- **Skill area:** Forehand / backhand (fed); team game
- **Works on / fixes:** Consistency under light pressure; extension toward the target.

##### 23. Feed, Hit & Catch
- **Ages / stage:** Red 6-8 (TC Red 1)
- **Level:** First-timer

##### 24. Big-Ball Tennis / Throw Rally
- **Ages / stage:** Tots (big ball) / Red 5-10
- **Level:** First-timer → developing
- **Skill area:** Rally pattern without racquet; tracking; recovery

##### 29. Serve Pyramids

##### 32. No Bounce → Volley Ball (LTA Go volley progression)
- **Ages / stage:** Red 5-8 → Orange
- **Level:** First-timer (No Bounce) → developing (Volley Ball)
- **Skill area:** Volley
- **Works on / fixes:** Swinging at volleys; grip changes (one grip for both sides); catching to the side at waist height.
- **Players & equipment:** Pairs; tennis balls; racquets; TDLs, cones, barrier tape net; "ice cream" cones as catching aids.
- **Setup:** Pairs facing each other (No Bounce in open space; Volley Ball over a net/tape).
- **Steps / rules:** *No Bounce:* two-hand throw to partner; partner claps once, then catches before the ball bounces. *Volley Ball:* one player throws underarm to partner's forehand; hitter volleys it over the net for the thrower to catch; 10 each, swap; repeat on the backhand side; third set the thrower may go to either side but must call it before releasing.
- **Coaching cues:** Catch one-handed, to the side, about waist high (mimics a volley); hold the racquet like a hammer or magic wand so one grip works both sides; the ball goes where the strings point.
- **Progression:** One-hand same-side catches; thrower moves left/right after throwing and the volleyer redirects to the new spot.
- **Regression:** Solo throw-clap-catch (add claps); hold the racquet head in two hands in front of the face and just block the ball back (target = strings); "Different": catch with two cones and send back; pat the ball back with a palm-up high-five.

##### 35. Up-and-Back Volley
- **Ages / stage:** Orange 8-10 (NG Orange C2; manual aimed at older beginners)
- **Level:** Ready to move up
- **Skill area:** Volley / approach / split step
- **Works on / fixes:** Coming forward after an approach; split-stepping on partner's hit.
- **Players & equipment:** Pairs, two per court; racquets; orange balls.
- **Setup:** Half-court; partners start on opposite service lines.
- **Steps / rules:** P1 drop-hits an approach to P2, moves in, split-steps. P2 hits back; P1 volleys back; P2 lets it bounce and traps. P1 retreats to the service line; P2 now drop-hits an approach and comes in. Repeat.
- **Coaching cues:** Split step when your partner hits.
- **Progression:** Rally every ball without traps; play the point out once each player has been to the net.
- **Regression:** Feed by hand; volleyer starts closer.
- **Source:** [NG-Orange](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Orange.pdf) (C2 Ch.2, Ch.4)
- **Video:** None verified.

##### 36. Make 'Em Move
- **Ages / stage:** Orange 8-10 (NG Orange C2); works for Red 7+
- **Level:** Developing
- **Skill area:** Movement / recovery
- **Works on / fixes:** "Chasing the ball with feet planted" and not recovering — the catcher must move to the ball and return to base every time.
- **Players & equipment:** Pairs; one orange ball; one TDL per pair; up to four pairs per side.
- **Setup:** Partners on the same side of the net, 4-6 ft apart; catcher's TDL = "home base".
- **Steps / rules:** Feeder tosses to one side of the catcher with enough height to reach; catcher catches one-handed (right hand on right, left on left), tosses back, recovers to home base. Switch every minute.
- **Coaching cues:** Recover to home base after every catch; same-side hand.
- **Progression:** Vary high/low and deep/short; increase distance; on high balls catch with opposite foot and hand forward.
- **Regression:** Two-hand catch; slower, higher tosses.
- **Source:** [NG-Orange](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Orange.pdf) (C2 Ch.1, Ch.4)
- **Video (Related):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OuHTPCK3EpE — "4 Best Drills to BOOST Movement for Advanced Kids Under 10 | Tennis Lesson" — Tennis With Ema

##### 37. Floor Rally with Recovery
- **Ages / stage:** Red 6-10 (TC)
- **Level:** Developing
- **Skill area:** Movement + rally direction (crosscourt / down the line)
- **Works on / fixes:** Recovering with shuffle steps; set up sideways; contact in front.
- **Players & equipment:** Pairs; racquets; red ball; home-base markers.
- **Setup:** Red court; each player starts on a home-base marker (centre recovery spot).
- **Steps / rules:** Push along the ground: one player alternates crosscourt then down the line, the other always pushes back to the same corner. Continuous; rally ends if the ball goes out or leaves the ground. Longest rally wins.
- **Coaching cues:** Shuffle back to the centre after every push; sideways to send and receive; contact in front of the leading foot, racquet facing the target.
- **Progression:** One player always crosscourt, the other always down the line; count passes in 2 minutes.
- **Regression:** One direction only; allow stopping the ball before pushing.
- **Source:** [TC-LTP](https://assets.tenniscanada.com/production/PDFs/LearnToPlay_ENG.pdf) (Practice Activity 4)
- **Video (Related):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qX07RV8JFaY — "Ball Control Tennis Drill Kids Ages 6-9 Years" — Elite Sports Clubs

##### 38. Triangles
- **Ages / stage:** Orange 8-10 (NG Orange C2)
- **Level:** Ready to move up
- **Skill area:** Forehand / backhand direction
- **Works on / fixes:** Shoulder turn; changing direction.
- **Players & equipment:** Groups of three (or four); racquets; orange balls; half-court per group if two groups share.
- **Setup:** Two feeders on one side in the middle of the service box(es); hitter on the opposite service line.
- **Steps / rules:** Feeder 1 tosses to the hitter's forehand; hitter sends it to Feeder 2, who catches and tosses back; hitter changes direction each time. After three sequences rotate one spot. With four, two hitters alternate after two sequences.
- **Coaching cues:** Turn the shoulder on each groundstroke and reach to the target.
- **Progression:** Backhand; feeders drop-hit and trap; all three rally cooperatively.
- **Regression:** Hitter self-rallies first.
- **Source:** [NG-Orange](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Orange.pdf) (C2 Ch.1)
- **Video:** None verified.

##### 39. Build Up (crosscourt rally goals)
- **Ages / stage:** Orange 8-10 (NG Orange C2)
- **Level:** Ready to move up
- **Skill area:** Rally (crosscourt consistency)
- **Works on / fixes:** Recovering to the middle; consistent crosscourt.
- **Players & equipment:** Pairs, two per court diagonal; orange balls.
- **Setup:** Both players start on the service line, diagonal; for right-handers, forehands on the deuce side and backhands on the ad side.
- **Steps / rules:** Coach sets a rally number (e.g., 4); once reached, the goal rises by 2.
- **Coaching cues:** Recover to the middle, ready for partner's next shot.
- **Progression:** Move to the baseline, any stroke; one stroke only.
- **Regression:** Lower starting goal; service-line distance.
- **Source:** [NG-Orange](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Orange.pdf) (C2 Ch.1)
- **Video:** None verified.

##### 40. In To Net (LTA Go warm-up)
- **Ages / stage:** Tots (simplified) / Red / Orange
- **Level:** First-timer
- **Skill area:** Movement / reaction
- **Works on / fixes:** Athletic stance; moving forward and back quickly; listening.
- **Players & equipment:** Pairs; TDLs.
- **Setup:** Partners face each other 2+ m apart.
- **Steps / rules:** Coach calls a body part; partners move forward and connect (right-hand high five, left hands, both hands, right feet, left feet, both feet), then return to the start.
- **Coaching cues:** "Goalie stance" to get switched on; when sidestepping, feet wider than shoulders and head well lowered.
- **Progression:** Number codes (1 = right hands … 6 = both feet) for cognitive challenge; combine numbers.
- **Regression:** Fewer commands (hands only or feet only).
- **Source:** [LTA-Go](https://lta.org.uk/490853/siteassets/roles/activators/file/lta-youth-go---net-game-lesson-plans.pdf)
- **Video (Related, LTA official):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DBVEku_UX2E — "Tennis at home - Double Trouble (8-11 years old)" — LTA (an LTA at-home activity video; not the same game)

##### 41. Tag-Team Singles
- **Ages / stage:** Red 7-10 (NG C2-C3)
- **Level:** Developing → ready to move up
- **Skill area:** First point play / rally with rotation
- **Works on / fixes:** Getting many turns; continuous play; team record.
- **Players & equipment:** Two teams of 4-6 per court; racquets; red balls.
- **Setup:** Teams on opposite sides, each in a line behind the service line; points played at the centre, halfway between service line and net (C3: middle of service box).
- **Steps / rules:** First player drop-hits over and runs to the back of the line; receiver traps and drop-hits back (C3: rallies it back) and goes to the back; continue until a miss. Try for an overall team record.
- **Coaching cues:** Hit and move to the back of the line quickly **[added]**.
- **Progression:** Courts compete for the longest rally; play competitively.
- **Regression:** Bump up and hit; allow multiple bounces.
- **Source:** [NG-Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Red.pdf) (C2 Ch.6; C3 Ch.2, Ch.5). Tennis Canada "Tag Team Tennis" (G7) is a competitive cousin: pairs, each partner plays 2 points then high-fives the partner in, continuous score to 7 or 10 — [TC-LTP](https://assets.tenniscanada.com/production/PDFs/LearnToPlay_ENG.pdf)
- **Video (Related):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lkwawy09alg — "Net Generation- Red Ball Team Challenge - USTA Middle States" — USTAMiddleStates

##### 42. Endless Bounce
- **Ages / stage:** Red 7-10 (NG C3)
- **Level:** Ready to move up (first competitive points)
- **Skill area:** Point play
- **Works on / fixes:** Playing points before strokes are perfect — any number of bounces keeps the ball alive.
- **Players & equipment:** Teams of 3-4, two teams per court; racquets; red balls.
- **Setup:** Teams on opposite sides; service boxes = boundaries; players spread out.
- **Steps / rules:** Start with a drop-hit forehand. Any number of bounces is allowed; players may bump the ball up or pass to a teammate. Point ends when the ball starts rolling or hits the net/fence. First to 4, then restart; after two games switch opponents or rebalance teams.
- **Coaching cues:** Keep it alive — bump, bounce, send **[added]**.
- **Progression:** Backhands only; maximum two bounces.
- **Regression:** Rolling rally.
- **Source:** [NG-Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Red.pdf) (C3 Ch.1 game)
- **Video:** None verified.

##### 43. Capture the Cone
- **Ages / stage:** Red 8-10 (NG C3)
- **Level:** Ready to move up
- **Skill area:** Serve/return; point play
- **Works on / fixes:** Serve-and-return under a scoring incentive.
- **Players & equipment:** Two teams of 2-3 per court (1v1 matches); 5-7 cones per team; TDLs/chalk.
- **Setup:** Each team's cones lined up on its own side, outside the court near the net. Points from the middle of the service box.
- **Steps / rules:** Serve (drop-hit or out of the air). Not returned → server wins; returned → returner wins. Winner runs to the opponent's side and claims a cone. Play by time: most cones (or all cones) wins. Switch serving team each game.
- **Coaching cues:** Turned to the side for the serve (NG serve cue).
- **Progression:** Play out the whole point; serve to a designated half of the box.
- **Regression:** Toss the serve; trap and drop-hit the return.
- **Source:** [NG-Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Red.pdf) (C3 Ch.4, Ch.6)
- **Video:** None verified.

##### 44. Throw Tennis
- **Ages / stage:** Tots (big ball) / Red 6-8 (TC)
- **Level:** First-timer
- **Skill area:** Point play (serve, rally, score without a racquet)
- **Works on / fixes:** Understanding serve-rally-score and moving an opponent before racquet skills are reliable.
- **Players & equipment:** Pairs; ball; red court.
- **Setup:** Opponents on either side of a red-court net.
- **Steps / rules:** Server starts with an overhead throw anywhere in the area; returner catches after one bounce and throws back underhand anywhere. Point ends on a double bounce, ball out or in the net, or a catch in the air.
- **Coaching cues:** Coach evaluates teaching points but keeps play flowing.
- **Progression:** One hand; catch in the air; one player crosscourt only, other down the line.
- **Regression:** Two bounces before catching; two-hand catches.
- **Source:** [TC-LTP](https://assets.tenniscanada.com/production/PDFs/LearnToPlay_ENG.pdf) (Play Activity G1)
- **Video:** None verified.

##### 45. Floor Tennis
- **Ages / stage:** Red 5-8 (TC; LTA at-home)
- **Level:** First-timer
- **Skill area:** Point play (rolling)
- **Works on / fixes:** Competing on the ground before over-the-net hitting; moving the opponent.
- **Players & equipment:** Pairs; racquets; red ball.
- **Setup:** Both players on the same side of the net with a marked playing area.
- **Steps / rules:** Serve by pushing the ball along the ground into the opponent's area; opponent stops it and pushes it back anywhere. Point ends if the ball leaves the ground or goes out.
- **Coaching cues:** Stop it, aim it, push it **[added]**.
- **Progression:** Alternate FH/BH; push without stopping; bigger area; one player rolls crosscourt, the other down the line.
- **Regression:** FH or BH only; smaller area.
- **Source:** [TC-LTP](https://assets.tenniscanada.com/production/PDFs/LearnToPlay_ENG.pdf) (Play Activity G2); LTA lists a "Floor Tennis" at-home video — [LTA Tennis at Home](https://www.lta.org.uk/play-compete/lta-youth/tennis-at-home/)
- **Video:** LTA Floor Tennis video exists per the LTA page but its YouTube ID was not retrieved/verified.

##### 46. Stop Tennis
- **Ages / stage:** Red 7-10 / Orange (TC)
- **Level:** Ready to move up
- **Skill area:** Point play / scoring
- **Works on / fixes:** Short matches with frequent wins; keeping score out loud.
- **Players & equipment:** Pairs on multiple red/orange courts.
- **Setup:** One pair per mini-court.
- **Steps / rules:** Regular points; spin for serve; alternate server every 2 points. All matches start on "GO"; the first player anywhere to reach the target score (5, 7 or 9) shouts "STOP" and all matches end. Whoever leads in each match gets a win; draws give both a win. Rotate and replay.
- **Coaching cues:** Call the score out loud (TC "Good Sport" behaviour).
- **Progression:** Serve to opponent's backhand; win points at the net.
- **Regression:** Drop-bounce-hit serve; self-rally allowed; smaller area.
- **Source:** [TC-LTP](https://assets.tenniscanada.com/production/PDFs/LearnToPlay_ENG.pdf) (Play Activity G6)
- **Video:** None verified.

##### 47. Team Cones (and Up & Down)
- **Ages / stage:** Red 7-10 / Orange (TC)
- **Level:** Ready to move up
- **Skill area:** Point play / team competition
- **Works on / fixes:** Individual matches that feed a team score (reduces pressure of losing).
- **Players & equipment:** Two teams; one large base cone per team at the side of the court; small cones.
- **Setup:** 1v1 matches across available courts; team base cones at the side.
- **Steps / rules:** Spin for serve; play to 5 or 7 (alternate serve every 2). Winner puts a small cone on the team base; wait for a new opponent from the other team. Timed (10/15/20 min); most cones wins. *Up & Down:* court split into four positions with two players each; winners move up a position, losers down; 4-minute matches; tie → one deciding point.
- **Coaching cues:** Shake hands after each game (TC Good Sport behaviour).
- **Progression:** Win points from the net; hit to open space.
- **Regression:** Self-rally allowed; smaller area.
- **Source:** [TC-LTP](https://assets.tenniscanada.com/production/PDFs/LearnToPlay_ENG.pdf) (Play Activities G5, G8)
- **Video:** None verified.

##### 48. Four-Square
- **Ages / stage:** Orange 8-10 (NG Orange C2)
- **Level:** Ready to move up
- **Skill area:** Point play / tactics (placement)
- **Works on / fixes:** Hitting to space instead of back to the opponent.
- **Players & equipment:** Two teams of 2-3 per court; orange balls; TDLs/poly-spots.
- **Setup:** Full 60-ft orange court; each side divided into four equal squares; the short/deep dividing line about 5 ft in front of the service line.
- **Steps / rules:** Singles points starting with serve and return. After the serve, a player may not hit into the same square twice in a row. Players struggling may drop-hit the second serve.
- **Coaching cues:** Find a new square every shot **[added]**.
- **Progression:** Each player names a "black hole" square the opponent may not hit into.
- **Regression:** Play inside the service boxes.
- **Source:** [NG-Orange](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Orange.pdf) (C2 Ch.1 game)
- **Video (Related, orange-stage overview):** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Dh5oKAey0A — "The INs and OUTs of Orange Ball Tennis" — Kristin W

##### 49. Gladiator
- **Ages / stage:** Orange 8-10+ (NG Orange C2)
- **Level:** Ready to move up
- **Skill area:** Point play (doubles, "king of the court")
- **Works on / fixes:** Serving and playing points under fun pressure; net play.
- **Players & equipment:** Pairs, up to five per court; orange balls.
- **Setup:** 60-ft doubles court (sidelines = regulation singles lines); one-up/one-back formation. One team = Champs on one side; Challengers lined up behind the opposite service line.
- **Steps / rules:** Challengers serve to the Champs and play the point, then rotate to the back of the line. Challengers keep their own score; at 2 points, before serving, they must shout "Gladiator" — win that point and they take the Champs' side (no shout = no takeover). New Champs: challengers count down 3-2-1 before serving.
- **Coaching cues:** Keep the ball deep crosscourt, away from the net person.
- **Progression:** Any outright winner takes the Champs spot; a winning volley after approaching takes it.
- **Regression:** Singles; play inside the service boxes.
- **Source:** [NG-Orange](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Orange.pdf) (C2 Ch.2 game)
- **Video:** None verified.

##### 50. Positive-Negative
- **Ages / stage:** Orange 8-10 (NG Orange C2)
- **Level:** Ready to move up
- **Skill area:** Rally → scoring bridge
- **Works on / fixes:** Consistency first, then competition.
- **Players & equipment:** Two pairs per court (half-court); orange balls.
- **Setup:** Half-court singles.
- **Steps / rules:** Coach sets a rally number (e.g., 6 or 8). Rally starts with a serve; both players start at 0. If the rally breaks before the number, the player who erred goes −1; once past the number, whoever didn't make the error gets +1. Two-minute rounds, then switch opponents.
- **Coaching cues:** Make one more ball than your partner **[added]**.
- **Progression:** Crosscourt with one stroke; full court; serve-and-return games to 3 or 4.
- **Regression:** Lower target number.
- **Source:** [NG-Orange](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Orange.pdf) (C2 Ch.3 game)
- **Video:** None verified.

## Q2. How do coaches keep 4-8-year-olds engaged (themes, stories, scoring, short rounds, rotation)?

### Takeaway
Federations engineer engagement through very short activities (Tennis Canada: practice ≤ average age + 2 minutes), "play first" lesson design, imaginative themes and story roles (pizzas, lobsters, fire ants, islands), cooperative "beat your record" scoring with props (clothespins, playing cards, ball pyramids), and frequent rotation/stations.

### Cited Findings
- Tennis Canada lesson structure: Meet & Greet → Warm-up → Play-Practice-Play → Wrap-up; each lesson starts and ends with an "I Can Play Points" activity, rejecting "technique first". Play activities run 15-20 min; practice activities 8-12 min. Rule of thumb: run a practice activity no more than 2 minutes over the group's average age (average age 7 → ≤9 min). — [TC-LTP](https://assets.tenniscanada.com/production/PDFs/LearnToPlay_ENG.pdf)
- Tennis Canada "I am a Good Sport" behaviours: hug the racquet when the instructor talks, cooperate with a partner, pick up balls quickly, keep score out loud, fair line calls, compliment opponents, shake hands after each game. — [TC-LTP](https://assets.tenniscanada.com/production/PDFs/LearnToPlay_ENG.pdf)
- Tennis Canada: players typically repeat a level 2-3 times; stress gaining competence so children don't feel they "failed". 6-8-year-olds should be separated from older groups for developmental reasons. — [TC-LTP](https://assets.tenniscanada.com/production/PDFs/LearnToPlay_ENG.pdf)
- NG Red session template: warm-up (5-10 min) → pledge + "word of the day" character theme (3 min) → skills (25-30 min; C1 run as stations with a coach at each, rotating every 10 min) → team game (10-12 min) → wrap-up with team talk, "play at home" challenge, and a team huddle chant ("1-2-3 Net Generation!"). C2-C3 add athletic skills and 10 min of free play. — [NG-Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Red.pdf)
- NG "game components" to motivate: Numbers (use 4 or 7 to rehearse tennis scoring), Time (fixed start/end), Cards (deck sets points or repetitions), Clothespins (clipped on the net or clothing to track score or reps); can be combined (e.g., points in one minute tracked with pins). — [NG-Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Red.pdf)
- NG uses story/theme framing throughout: Pizza Delivery, Tennis Sandwich (players describe what's in their sandwich — ball as meatball, racquets as bread), Lobster Trap, Fire Ants, Walk the Dog, Ice Cream Cones (call a flavour each catch), Survivor's island/"out to sea", Touchdown/field goal, Messy Bedroom. — [NG-Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Red.pdf)
- NG emphasises "team best" and beating your own record; in the final Red 3 team challenge it advises not announcing team scores — just ensure players understand each point counts for the team — and praising effort and sportsmanship. Free play note: focus on effort since players may struggle. — [NG-Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Red.pdf)
- Judy Murray: start with individual skill, add movement, then add a partner; introduce cooperation in pairs first and competition second. — [JM](https://education.gov.scot/media/tunlek1v/jm-tennis-resource-primary.pdf)
- LTA Go: an "infinite session plan" — repeat the base exercise across sessions to embed skills, with Easier/Harder/Different versions; uses number-coded commands to add cognitive challenge; plan assumes 8-12 players per space. — [LTA-Go](https://lta.org.uk/490853/siteassets/roles/activators/file/lta-youth-go---net-game-lesson-plans.pdf)
- LTA uses characters ("Tennisables": Ace, Bounce, Dash, Slice, Smash, Spin) and award roadmaps (task cards → sticker → certificate). — [LTA Tennis at Home](https://www.lta.org.uk/play-compete/lta-youth/tennis-at-home/); [Rally Awards Stage 2](https://clubspark.lta.org.uk/CarlDouglasCoaching/RALLYAWARDS/STAGE2) (snippet)
- Hot Shots Red Awards provide "regular rewards" so players progress at their own pace. — [Hot Shots Red Ball Rally Awards](https://morningsidetennis.com.au/?p=2900) (search snippet; page 404 on fetch)
- Tots sessions at LTA clubs are 30 minutes and mix in balloons, beach balls and beanbags to keep activities interesting. — [Kinetic Tennis Tots](https://clubspark.lta.org.uk/KineticTennisCoaching/Coaching/Junior/TotsTennis) (snippet)

### Inferences
- For a private/small-group coach the transferable formula is: 1 play game → 2-3 short practice blocks (≤ age + 2 min each) wrapped in a story → 1 play game, with a visible score prop (pyramid, cones, clothespins) and a take-home challenge.
- Cooperative "beat your record" scoring (rather than winner/loser) dominates first-timer material; head-to-head scoring appears only at developing/ready-to-move-up levels.

### Gaps
- No federation source quantified attention spans for 3-5-year-olds specifically (Tennis Canada's age + 2 min rule is stated for its 6+ curriculum).
- The ITF CSSR article on teaching 3-6-year-olds ("Motor coordination and its implications on teaching tennis to three to six-year-old children") could not be opened (403); only its search abstract was seen (coordination gains concentrate between ages 4-7; "learning while playing") — [ITF CSSR](https://itfcoachingreview.com/index.php/journal/article/view/311) (snippet only).

## Q3. What equipment substitutes and drills build hand-eye coordination before racquet use (balloons, beanbags, throwing/catching progressions)?

### Takeaway
The clearest documented pre-racquet ladder is Judy Murray's: balloons → chiffon squares/fluff balls → beanbags → ball on the floor → bouncing ball, always hand first and then racquet; federations add big playground balls, upturned cones and household substitutes (paper-plate racquets, rope/chair nets).

### Cited Findings
- JM progression: balloons (slow, so everyone succeeds; work both sides and all heights, hands or racquets); chiffon squares and fluff balls (slow, bright; fluff balls don't roll away, good for small hands and big classes); beanbags (don't roll away, easier to trap than a ball; ideal for tracking, sending, receiving); start with the hand mimicking the shot, add the racquet when appropriate; "always start with the ball on the floor" and aim through target areas using hands before the racquet; downward "V-shape" throw caught after one bounce, then one racquet, then two; hoops as targets; double-racquet exercise mirrors forehand and backhand. — [JM](https://education.gov.scot/media/tunlek1v/jm-tennis-resource-primary.pdf)
- NG substitutes: large lightweight playground balls ("need help? use a big ball"), poly-spots or paper plates for targets/"pizzas", chalk/TDLs for lines, barrier tape/rope/pennants attached to fences, carts, chairs or cooler stands as a net, ball pyramids and clothespins for scoring; Community 1 runs on any flat surface. — [NG-Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Red.pdf)
- Balloon tennis at home: paper plate + paint-stir-stick racquets, string about 1 m high between chairs. — search summaries of [USTA Florida](https://www.ustaflorida.com/tennis-tip-thursday-balloon-tennis-edgies-fast-feet/) / [Active for Life](https://activeforlife.com/activity/balloon-tennis/) (pages 403 on fetch); [Premier Education](https://www.premier-education.com/news/tennis-games-for-kids/) (hands or paper-plate racquets; count taps)
- LTA Go: upturned "ice cream" cones as catching aids; two cones held like a catching tool instead of a racquet (the "Different" volley version); clap-then-catch throwing progressions. — [LTA-Go](https://lta.org.uk/490853/siteassets/roles/activators/file/lta-youth-go---net-game-lesson-plans.pdf)
- Tennis Canada: rope from the net to the fence propped on chairs as a red net; throw-and-catch rallies (Throw Rally, Sky Ball, Throw Tennis) as the racquet-free version of serve-rally-score. — [TC-LTP](https://assets.tenniscanada.com/production/PDFs/LearnToPlay_ENG.pdf)
- NG athletic-skill sequence for sending/receiving: rolling, underhand tossing, overhand throwing, catching (Tunnel Ball, One Bounce Two Bounce, Ice Cream Cones, Ball Drop, Shake-Hands Toss, Big-Ball Tennis). — [NG-Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Red.pdf)
- Tots programmes use sponge or mini red balls and 17-19 in racquets. — [Kinetic Tennis Tots](https://clubspark.lta.org.uk/KineticTennisCoaching/Coaching/Junior/TotsTennis) (snippet)

### Inferences
- A practical tots-to-red ladder: balloon (hand) → balloon (paper-plate racquet) → beanbag (hand, then on strings) → big ball rolled/trapped → red foam ball rolled (Walk the Dog) → bounce-catch → bungee/jacks → drop-hit to target → fed ball.

### Gaps
- A search summary attributed to an ACT Government resource said not to use balloons with children under 4 (choking hazard); the PDF failed to download, so this is unverified — coaches should check local safety guidance before using balloons with 3-year-olds.

## Q4. What are common faults for young kids, and which games correct them?

### Takeaway
Federation teaching points reveal the recurring faults — facing the net instead of turning sideways, contacting the ball too close or too late, swinging hard with no target, staying planted and not recovering, changing grips, and underarm "pushing" serves — and each has a dedicated corrective game in the library.

### Cited Findings
- Tennis Canada's fundamental teaching points repeatedly require: sideways set-up to send and receive; contact at waist height, out in front at a comfortable distance from the body; strings/racquet facing the target; recovery to a home-base marker with shuffle steps; continental grip for floor rallies, self-rallies and volleys; "catching action" on volleys; trophy position for overhead throws/serves. — [TC-LTP](https://assets.tenniscanada.com/production/PDFs/LearnToPlay_ENG.pdf)
- NG cues target the same faults: turn sideways to receive (Lobster Trap); trap at the side at waist level like a groundstroke (Half-Pound Lobster); keep the ball to the side of the body and follow through to the target (Walk the Dog); move the ball without over-hitting (Keep It Moving); short simple motion, "softer? harder?" (Bullseye); keep head still on the strings (Bungee Jump); bend knees to get under the ball (Jacks); return to ready position after hitting (Rally Pyramids, Card Shark); serve turned to the side with throwing arm bent and hand behind head (Serve Pyramids); feet about 45° to the baseline (Serve/Return Progression); split step when partner hits (Up-and-Back Volley). — [NG-Red](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Red.pdf); [NG-Orange](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/content-fragments/tennis-service-center/assets/pdfs/Net-Generation-Community-Manual-Orange.pdf)
- LTA Go: hold the racquet like a hammer or magic wand so the same grip works for forehand and backhand volleys; beginners can block with the racquet held in two hands in front of the face; the ball goes where the strings point. — [LTA-Go](https://lta.org.uk/490853/siteassets/roles/activators/file/lta-youth-go---net-game-lesson-plans.pdf)
- Rushing toward the ball causes being too close, having less time to prepare, and contacting too high; letting the ball come (even two bounces at first) is the suggested fix. — [TopSpinPro blog](https://topspinpro.com/blog/7-things-beginner-players-do-wrong/) (search snippet; the snippet may come from this or a sibling TopSpinPro "common mistakes" page; commercial source, not youth-specific)
- Search-engine summary of youth-coaching results: most young players move very close to the bounce; drills such as catching the ball in an upturned cone, bucket or cap encourage players to back off and catch as it falls, and hitting after two bounces is another option. — attributed among results including an [ITF CSSR article](https://itfcoachingreview.com/index.php/journal/article/download/606/1617/2529) (403 on fetch; unverified wording)

#### Fault → corrective game map (from the library above)
| Fault | Corrective games (#) | Key cue (source) |
|---|---|---|
| Rushing in / standing too close to the ball | Ice Cream Cones (7), Lobster Trap / Half-Pound (20), Ball Drop (10), One Bounce Two Bounce (8), Feed Hit & Catch (23) | Trap at your side at waist height (NG); comfortable distance, out in front (TC) |
| Facing the net (not turning) | Big-Ball Tennis / Throw Rally (24), Lobster Trap (20), Floor Rally (16), Triangles (38) | Start sideways, finish facing forward (NG); throw and catch sideways (TC) |
| Swinging too early/too hard; no target | Walk the Dog (15), Keep It Moving (16), Bullseye (18), Touchdown (17), Battle Spots (21) | Edge on the ground, follow through to the target (NG); softer? harder? (NG) |
| Feet planted / chasing with flat feet / no recovery | Make 'Em Move (36), Floor Rally with Recovery (37), Fire Ants (12), Tunnel Ball (9), In To Net (40) | Recover to home base; shuffle back to centre (TC/NG) |
| Grip changes between FH/BH; unstable face | Bungee Jump/Flips (4), Jacks (19), Self Volley Keep Ups (33), Volley Ball (32) | Palm up / knuckles up (NG); hammer/magic-wand grip (LTA) |
| Underarm "pushing" serve; no throwing pattern | Messy Bedroom/Clean Your Room (13), Sky Ball (27), Flying Target (28), Serve Pyramids (29), Climb Serve Mountain (30), Serve/Return Progression (31) | Trophy position, hand above shoulder (TC); arm bent, hand behind head (NG) |
| Swinging at volleys | No Bounce → Volley Ball (32), Throw to Volley (34), Self Volley Keep Ups (33) | Catching action, strings to target (TC); block it back (LTA) |
| Over-hitting in rallies | Keep It Moving (16), Rally Pyramid (25), Card Shark (26), Positive-Negative (50) | Move the ball without over-hitting (NG) |

### Inferences
- Because federation activities are built as constraints (cones to catch in, lines to stay on, targets, home-base markers), the "fix" is usually the game itself; coaches correct faults by choosing the constraint rather than by verbal technique instruction — consistent with Tennis Canada's "technique used to answer 'How can I play better?'".

### Gaps
- I found no federation document that explicitly lists "common faults of 4-8-year-olds" as such; the fault list above is inferred from repeated teaching points and cues. The ITF CSSR youth articles that likely cover this were blocked (403).
- "Grip changes" guidance differs by source: Tennis Canada specifies continental grip for floor rallies/volleys and introduces forehand/backhand grips later; NG uses palm-up/knuckles-up language without naming grips; LTA Go recommends one "hammer" grip for both volley sides. No source reconciles these for 5-6-year-olds.
